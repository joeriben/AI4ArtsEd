# Concept Sliders: LoRA Adaptors for Precise Control in Diffusion Models

## PROFIL

The article "Concept Sliders: LoRA Adaptors for Precise Control in Diffusion Models" was written by Rohit Gandikota, Joanna Materzyńska, Tingrui Zhou, Antonio Torralba, and David Bau. It presents a method for creating interpretable, low-rank LoRA adaptors that enable precise, continuous control over specific attributes in images generated by diffusion models, primarily Stable Diffusion XL and SD v1.4; its central claim is that a low-rank constraint on parameter updates, combined with a disentanglement objective, identifies the minimal concept subspace in a diffusion model's parameter space, allowing targeted edits with minimal interference to other attributes. Sliders can be trained from text prompt pairs, paired image data, or latents transferred from StyleGAN, and are plug-and-play, composable (over 50 sliders can be combined), and adjustable at inference time via a scaling factor α without retraining. The paper positions itself against single-image editing methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, Pix2Video, SDEdit), guidance-based methods (classifier-free guidance by Ho et al., compositional guidance by Liu et al., SLD), model editing and customization approaches (Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion, concept-erasure methods, LoRA-based customization by Ryu et al.), and semantic direction research in GANs and diffusion models, arguing that unlike per-image methods, Concept Sliders learn generalizable parameter directions requiring no per-image optimization. Its contents proceed from background on diffusion models and LoRA, through the method (a guided score derived via Tweedie's formula, a disentanglement objective over preservation concepts, and paired-image training for visual concepts), to experiments on textual sliders, visual sliders, StyleGAN latent transfer, and slider composition, followed by two quality-improvement sliders ("fix hands" and "repair") validated with Amazon Mechanical Turk user studies, ablations confirming the necessity of both the disentanglement objective and the low-rank constraint, stated limitations (residual interference between edits and reduced edit intensity from the SDEdit inference technique), and supplementary material covering disentanglement formulation, SDEdit timestep analysis, baseline details, entanglement measurements, composition examples, editing of real images via null inversion, user-study details, and qualitative galleries. The work is usable in disciplines and situations of arts education concerned with AI-assisted image generation, generative-model control, and creative applications of diffusion models, where targeted, composable adjustment of attributes such as age, style, weather, expression, and image quality is taught or practiced. Code, data, and trained sliders are available at sliders.baulab.info and on GitHub.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (pp. 1–2)

The paper introduces "Concept Sliders," a method for creating interpretable, low-rank LoRA adaptors that enable precise, continuous control over specific attributes in images generated by diffusion models (primarily Stable Diffusion XL and SD v1.4). The central thesis is that a low-rank constraint on parameter updates, combined with a disentanglement objective, identifies the minimal concept subspace in a diffusion model's parameter space, allowing targeted edits with minimal interference to other attributes. Sliders can be trained from small sets of text prompt pairs, paired image data, or even latents transferred from StyleGAN, making them usable for visual concepts that are hard to describe in words. They are plug-and-play, composable (over 50 sliders can be combined), and adjustable at inference time via a scaling factor α without retraining. The authors demonstrate applications including weather, age, style and expression control, StyleGAN latent transfer, and two quality-improvement sliders: "fix hands" and "repair" (pp. 1–2, 6–7).

### Related Works (pp. 2–3)

The authors position their work against: (1) single-image editing methods manipulating cross-attentions (Prompt-to-Prompt, Pix2Video) or using conditional inputs (SDEdit); (2) guidance-based methods (classifier-free guidance by Ho et al., compositional guidance by Liu et al., SLD for unsafe content); (3) model editing/customization (Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion, concept-erasure methods, LoRA-based customization by Ryu et al., closed-form cross-attention editing); and (4) semantic direction research in GANs (self-supervised latent trajectories, PCA-based directions, StyleGAN style-space neurons) and diffusion models (h-spaces). Unlike per-image methods, Concept Sliders learn generalizable parameter directions requiring no per-image optimization (pp. 2–3).

### Background (p. 3)

Diffusion models gradually add noise to data (forward process) and learn to reverse it by predicting noise ε conditioned on timestep t and conditioning c; Stable Diffusion and SDXL operate in a VAE latent space for efficiency. LoRA decomposes a weight update ΔW into matrices B ∈ R^(d×r) and A ∈ R^(r×k) with rank r ≪ min(d,k), freezing the pretrained weights W₀ and training only A and B; at inference ΔW merges into W₀ with a scaling factor α and no overhead (p. 3).

### Method (pp. 3–4)

Given a target concept c_t and model θ, the goal is to obtain θ* that increases the likelihood of attribute c⁺ and decreases c⁻ in image X conditioned on c_t. Using Tweedie's formula and the reparametrization trick, the score (gradient of log probability) is expressed as a denoising prediction, yielding a guided score that shifts the target concept's distribution toward c⁺ and away from c⁻ (Eqs. 6–7). Because a single prompt pair can yield entangled directions, the authors add a disentanglement objective (Eq. 8): for a set of preservation concepts p ∈ P (e.g., race names when editing age), the optimization increases P((c⁺, p)|X) and reduces P((c⁻, p)|X) for every p. The LoRA scaling factor α can be modified at inference to strengthen or weaken the edit without retraining. For visual concepts, sliders are trained on paired before/after images (x_A, x_B): the LoRA is applied in both positive and negative directions (ε_θ⁺ and ε_θ⁻) and a loss aligns the direction so the negative direction produces effect A and the positive direction effect B (Eq. 9). This image-pair method is also used to transfer latents from other generative models such as StyleGAN (pp. 3–4).

### Experiments (pp. 4–6)

Evaluated on SDXL (1024-pixel) and SD v1.4, all models trained for 500 epochs; sliders are tested on diverse prompts (e.g., a "person" slider tested on "doctor," "man," "woman," "barista"). Inference follows SDEdit: the pretrained model runs for the first t steps with LoRA multipliers at 0, then the adaptor is turned on for remaining steps (p. 4).

**Textual sliders (pp. 4–5):** Validated on 30 text-based concepts. Table 1 compares against Prompt-to-Prompt and an inference-time composition baseline (inspired by SDEdit and Liu et al.) on 2500 edited images per concept. For the "age" concept: Prompt-to-Prompt ΔCLIP 1.10 / LPIPS 0.15; the composition baseline 3.14 / 0.13; the proposed method 3.93 / 0.06. Similar patterns hold for hair (5.59 vs. 3.45 ΔCLIP), sky, and rusty. The method achieves higher CLIP scores and lower LPIPS in a single inference pass, while baselines need extra passes per concept and are more prone to entanglement.

**Visual sliders (p. 5):** Sliders for "eyebrow shape" and "eye size" trained on image pairs (eye sizes from the Ostris dataset), with optional text guidance to focus the edit region. Quantitative evaluation uses FaceNet for face detection and a face parser to measure eye area: traversing the slider increases average eye area 2.75x. Table 2 (eye size): training data Δeye 1.84 / LPIPS 0.03; Custom Diffusion 0.97 / 0.23; Textual Inversion 0.81 / 0.21; the proposed method 1.75 / 0.06. Customization baselines memorize training subjects rather than generalizing the contrast between pairs.

**StyleGAN transfer (p. 6):** Using StyleGAN-v3 style space trained on FFHQ and the method of [45], the authors identify style neurons controlling hard-to-describe facial features—neuron 77 (cheekbone structure), neuron 646 (left-side face width), neuron 847 (inter-ocular distance)—scale them to create paired images, and train diffusion sliders from these pairs, enabling structured facial editing.

**Composition (p. 6):** Sliders compose efficiently; e.g., blending "cooked" and "fine dining" food sliders traverses a 2D concept space. Up to 50 sliders can be composed simultaneously, using far more than the 77-token context limit of SDXL, enabling control beyond prompt-based methods.

### Concept Sliders to Improve Image Quality (pp. 6–7)

**Fix hands:** A slider trained with the prompt pair boosting "realistic hands, five fingers, 8k hyperrealistic hands" and suppressing "poorly drawn hands, distorted hands, misplaced fingers." In an Amazon Mechanical Turk user study on 300 images (150 SDXL, 150 slider, same seeds and prompts), raters found 62% of SDXL images had distorted hands versus only 22% of slider images (pp. 6–7).

**Repair slider:** A single low-rank direction shifting images away from distorted subjects, unnatural object placement, and inconsistent shapes. In a perceptual study on 250 pairs, participants rated slider images as more realistic in 80.39% of pairs. Notably, FID scores do not align with human judgment—distorting images along the opposite slider direction improves FID even though users prefer the realism-enhancing direction (p. 7).

### Ablations (p. 8)

Table 3 (2500 images, age concept) confirms both components are necessary: the full method achieves ΔCLIP 3.93, LPIPS 0.06, Interference 0.10; without the disentanglement objective, 3.39 / 0.17 / 0.36 (e.g., gender changes when editing age); without the low-rank constraint, 3.18 / 0.23 / 0.19 (background and clothing less preserved). The Interference metric measures the percentage of samples with changed race/gender when making the edit. The SDEdit inference technique expands the usable range of α before coherence declines (p. 8).

### Limitations (p. 8)

Residual interference between edits remains (Table 3), suggesting a need for more careful—preferably automated—selection of preservation directions. The SDEdit inference technique, while preserving structure, can reduce edit intensity compared to inference-time methods, trading edit strength for structural coherence (p. 8).

### Conclusion (p. 8)

Concept Sliders offer a simple, scalable paradigm for interpretable diffusion-model control: continuous, single-pass, composable adjustments learned from text pairs, artist-created image pairs, or transferred GAN latents, with demonstrated gains over prior editing and customization methods and validated quality improvements (hand repair, realism) via human studies. Code, data, and trained sliders are available at sliders.baulab.info and on GitHub (pp. 8–9).

### Supplementary Material (p. 1–14)

- **Disentanglement formulation:** Single prompt pairs can entangle directions (e.g., age with gender/race); multiple paired prompts with explicit preservation directions (dotted blue lines) find an edit direction invariant to preserved features (supp. p. 1).
- **SDEdit analysis:** Ablating SDEdit timesteps over 2,500 images shows inverse trends between LPIPS and CLIP distances; timesteps 750–850 balance structure preservation and edit efficacy (supp. p. 1).
- **Textual sliders:** CLIP score change and LPIPS measured over 15 sliders at 12 scales; concepts with antonym words (e.g., expensive–cheap) show symmetric deltas, while harder-to-negate sliders (e.g., "no glasses") show clipped negative range (supp. pp. 1–2).
- **Baseline details:** Prompt-to-Prompt used with its Refinement strategy (adding a token such as "old" to "picture of person"); the composition baseline composes score functions from both prompts via additive guidance plus SDEdit (supp. p. 1).
- **Entanglement:** Table 4 (age editing, 2500 images): P2P ΔCLIP 1.10 / LPIPS 0.15 / Interference 0.33; Composition 3.14 / 0.13 / 0.38; the proposed method 3.93 / 0.06 / 0.10 (supp. p. 2).
- **Visual concept baselines:** Custom Diffusion and Textual Inversion (modified for SDXL) trained on the large-eyes Ostris dataset entangle spurious attributes (blonde hair in Custom Diffusion, blue eyes in Textual Inversion); paired-image training isolates concepts (supp. pp. 2–3).
- **Composing sliders:** 2D composition of "cooked" and "fine dining" sliders on a thanksgiving dinner image; progressive composition of 10 sliders demonstrated (supp. pp. 3–4).
- **Editing real images:** Null inversion (finetuning the unconditional text embedding in classifier-free guidance) converts real images into diffusion-generated form, after which sliders are applied (supp. p. 3).
- **User study details:** "Fix hands": 150 images each from SDXL and the slider with matched seeds/prompts; raters select issues (misplaced/distorted fingers, incorrect number of fingers, none); 62% of SDXL vs. 22% of slider images have hand issues. "Repair": A/B test on 300 image pairs with randomized left/right placement; raters select the image with fewer flaws and describe their reasoning as a sanity check; the repair slider output preferred in 80.39% of pairs; after filtering generic responses, 250 pairs remain (supp. pp. 3–8).
- **Qualitative galleries:** Figures 27–32 show sliders for "curly hair," "surprised," "chubby"; styles "pixar," "realistic details," "clay," "sculpture"; weather "delightful," "dark," "tropical," "winter"; person attributes "glasses," "muscles," "beard," "long hair"; vehicle attributes "rusty," "futuristic," "damaged"; furniture styles "royal," "Modern" (supp. pp. 9–14).

## GLOSSAR

- **Concept Slider** — A low-rank LoRA adaptor fine-tuned on a diffusion model that enables precise, continuous control over one concept, adjustable at inference via a scaling factor α.
- **Disentanglement objective** — The training formulation (Eq. 8) that, for every preservation concept p, increases P((c⁺, p)|X) and reduces P((c⁻, p)|X), isolating the edit from unwanted attributes.
- **Interference metric** — The percentage of samples in which race or gender changes when making a target edit (e.g., editing age).
- **ΔCLIP** — The change in CLIP score between original and edited images, evaluated on a text prompt describing the desired edit; measures edit efficacy.
- **Fix hands slider** — A slider boosting "realistic hands, five fingers, 8k hyperrealistic hands" and suppressing "poorly drawn hands, distorted hands, misplaced fingers."
- **Repair slider** — A single low-rank parameter direction shifting generations away from common quality issues such as distorted subjects, unnatural object placement, and inconsistent shapes.
- **Preservation concepts** — A set of concepts P (e.g., race names when editing age) used to constrain slider training and avoid entanglement.
- **Null inversion** — A technique that finetunes the unconditional text embedding in classifier-free guidance during inference so that a real image can be treated as a diffusion-generated image and edited with sliders.
