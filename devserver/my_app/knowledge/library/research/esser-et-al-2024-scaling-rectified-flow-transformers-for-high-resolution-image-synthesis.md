# Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis

## PROFIL

The paper "Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis" was written by Patrick Esser, Sumith Kulal, Andreas Blattmann, Rahim Entezari, Jonas Müller, Harry Saini, Yam Levi, Dominik Lorenz, Axel Sauer, Frederic Boesel, Dustin Podell, Tim Dockhorn, Zion English, Kyle Lacey, Alex Goodwin, Yannik Marek, and Robin Rombach, researchers at Stability AI, and is addressed to the machine-learning research community working on generative image models. Its subject is text-to-image synthesis, and its claim is that Rectified Flow — a generative formulation connecting data and noise on a straight line — outperforms established diffusion formulations when combined with re-weighted noise scales during training (logit-normal, mode, and CosMap timestep samplers) and with a novel transformer architecture, MM-DiT, that uses separate weights for text and image with bidirectional attention between them. The paper first frames simulation-free training via Conditional Flow Matching, following Kingma & Gao; then compares flow trajectories against EDM (Karras et al., 2022), cosine schedules (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), and LDM-linear schedules; then presents the MM-DiT architecture building on DiT and LDM; then reports a large-scale empirical study of 61 training formulations on ImageNet and CC12M, improvements to autoencoders, captions (following DALL-E 3, using CogVLM), and backbones; then training at scale, including QK-normalization, positional encodings for varying aspect ratios, resolution-dependent timestep shifting, and DPO alignment; and finally a scaling study up to 8B parameters showing validation loss predicts GenEval, T2I-CompBench, and human-preference performance, with its largest models outperforming open models (SDXL, SDXL-Turbo, PixArt-α) and DALL-E 3. Appendices cover background on diffusion and rectified flows, flow-matching proofs, experiment details, DPO, instruction-based editing, and data preprocessing including SSCD-based deduplication assessed for memorization following Carlini et al. The document is usable in disciplines and situations concerned with generative image synthesis, AI-assisted image production, and the technical foundations of text-to-image systems; the summary gives no explicit statement of arts-education contexts.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (p. 1–2)

This paper from Stability AI (Esser, Kulal, Blattmann, Rombach et al.) argues that **Rectified Flow** — a generative model formulation that connects data and noise on a straight line — can outperform established diffusion formulations for high-resolution text-to-image synthesis, provided two improvements are made: (i) a re-weighting of the noise scales during training (new timestep samplers biased towards perceptually relevant, intermediate timesteps), and (ii) a novel transformer architecture, **MM-DiT**, that uses separate weights for text and image modalities and allows bidirectional information flow between them. The authors support this through a large-scale empirical study of 61 training formulations, a scaling study up to 8B parameters and 5 × 10²² training FLOPs, and show that lower validation loss correlates strongly with improved automatic metrics (T2I-CompBench, GenEval) and human preference ratings. Their largest models outperform open models (SDXL, SDXL-Turbo, PixArt-α) and closed-source DALL-E 3. The authors make their results, code, and model weights publicly available.

### Simulation-Free Training of Flows (p. 2–3)

The paper frames generative modeling as learning a velocity field v, parameterized by network weights Θ, that defines an ODE mapping noise samples x₁ to data samples x₀. Instead of solving the ODE directly (computationally expensive), the model regresses a vector field u_t generating a probability path between data distribution p₀ and noise distribution p₁ = N(0,1). Because regressing the marginal vector field directly is intractable, the tractable **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)** objective is used. The authors reparameterize the loss as a noise-prediction objective using the signal-to-noise ratio λ_t := log(a²_t/b²_t), and note that time-dependent weightings w_t can be introduced without changing the optimum — this unified form (following Kingma & Gao, 2023) allows comparison of classic diffusion formulations and rectified flows.

### Flow Trajectories and Tailored SNR Samplers (p. 3–4)

The paper compares several forward-process variants:

- **Rectified Flow (RF)**: straight paths between data and noise, z_t = (1−t)x₀ + tϵ, with the network directly predicting velocity; uniform timestep weighting w_RF_t = t/(1−t).
- **EDM** (Karras et al., 2022): forward process with b_t = exp(F⁻¹_N(t|P_m, P²_s)); F-prediction parameterization.
- **Cosine** (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021): with ε-parameterization weighting w_t = sech(λ_t/2), or v-prediction weighting w_t = e^(−λ_t/2).
- **(LDM-)Linear**: variance-preserving DDPM/LDM schedules defined via diffusion coefficients β_t.

**New timestep samplers for RF models** (Section 3.1): since the velocity target ϵ − x₀ is hardest in the middle of [0,1], the authors bias sampling towards intermediate timesteps:

1. **Logit-normal sampling**: t sampled via the standard logistic function applied to u ~ N(u; m, s); location m biases towards data (negative) or noise (positive), scale s controls width.
2. **Mode sampling with heavy tails**: a distribution with strictly positive density on [0,1] (unlike logit-normal, which vanishes at endpoints), parameterized by scale s; includes uniform weighting at s = 0.
3. **CosMap**: a mapping matching the log-SNR of the cosine schedule in the RF setting.

### Text-to-Image Architecture: MM-DiT (p. 4–5)

The model follows LDM in operating in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder, with text encoded by pretrained frozen text encoders. The backbone builds on DiT: 2×2 patches of the latent are flattened into a patch sequence, embedded together with the text encoding, and processed by modulated attention and MLPs conditioned on timestep and pooled text embeddings. Crucially, **text and image tokens use two separate sets of weights** — equivalent to two independent transformers joined for the attention operation — so each modality works in its own space while attending to the other. Model size is parameterized by depth d (number of attention blocks), with hidden size 64·d and d attention heads.

### Experiments: Improving Rectified Flows (p. 5–7)

61 formulations were trained on ImageNet and CC12M, including ε- and v-prediction with linear/cosine schedules, RF with mode sampling (7 values of s between −1 and 1.75, plus s = 1.0 and s = 0), RF with logit-normal sampling (30 (m, s) grid values, m ∈ [−1, 1], s ∈ [0.2, 2.2]), RF with CosMap, EDM (15 parameter pairs), and EDM variants matching RF and v/cos log-SNR weightings. Evaluation used validation losses, CLIP scores, and FID on CLIP features (COCO-2014 validation split), with 24 combinations of sampler settings (guidance scales and steps), EMA/non-EMA weights, and datasets, ranked via non-dominated (Pareto) sorting.

Key results (Tables 1–2): **rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00)** consistently achieves the best average rank (1.54), outperforming uniform-timestep rectified flow and the LDM-Linear formulation. Only rectified flows with modified timestep sampling beat eps/linear. Some variants excel in specific settings only (e.g., rf/lognorm(0.50, 0.60) is best at 50 sampling steps but rank 8.5 at 5 steps). Rectified flows degrade least when sampling steps are reduced, confirming their sample efficiency.

### Improving Modality-Specific Representations (p. 7–9)

- **Improved autoencoders** (Table 3): increasing latent channels d from 4 to 8 to 16 improves reconstruction (FID 2.41 → 1.56 → 1.06; PSNR 25.12 → 26.40 → 28.62). Although larger d is harder to model, larger models exploit it better; d = 16 is chosen.
- **Improved captions**: following DALL-E 3, synthetic captions from CogVLM are mixed 50% original / 50% synthetic. GenEval overall score rises from 43.27 to 49.78, with large gains in color attribution (11.75 → 24.75), position (6.50 → 18.00), counting (33.44 → 41.56), and two-object scenes (41.41 → 52.53).
- **Improved backbones**: comparing DiT, CrossDiT (cross-attending variant), UViT, and MM-DiT on CC12M, MM-DiT significantly outperforms all; three weight sets (separating CLIP and T5 tokens) give only small gains over two, so two sets are used.

### Training at Scale (p. 9–11)

- **Data preprocessing**: filtering for sexual content (NSFW detection), aesthetics (low-score removal), and regurgitation (cluster-based deduplication); precomputation of image latents and text embeddings for efficiency.
- **QK-Normalization**: when finetuning at high resolutions, mixed-precision training becomes unstable due to attention-logit growth and attention-entropy collapse (observed in the last transformer blocks). Applying RMSNorm (with learnable scale) to Q and K before attention, combined with ε = 10⁻¹⁵ in AdamW, enables stable bf16-mixed training; it can also be applied to pretrained models retroactively.
- **Positional encodings for varying aspect ratios**: bucketed sampling with a combination of extended and interpolated position grids, center-cropped before frequency embedding.
- **Resolution-dependent timestep shifting**: higher resolutions need more noise; mapping timesteps via σ(t, n) = σ(t, m) yields a shift α = √(m/n). Human preference favors shifts above 1.5; α = 3.0 is used for training and sampling at 1024². This implies a log-SNR shift of log(n/m).
- After shifted training at 1024², the model is aligned with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

### Results at Scale (p. 11–12)

- **Scaling study**: models of different depths trained 500k steps at 256² resolution, batch size 4096 (learning-rate adjustment at 3 × 10⁵ steps needed for depth=38 to prevent divergence). Validation loss decreases smoothly with model size and steps, with no saturation, and correlates strongly with GenEval, T2I-CompBench, and human preference. A preliminary video scaling study (140k steps, batch 512, 16 frames at 256², starting from image weights with 2× temporal patching) shows the same trend.
- **GenEval** (Table 5): the depth=38 model at 1024² with DPO achieves overall 0.74, beating DALL-E 3 (0.67), with 0.99 single-object, 0.94 two-object, 0.72 counting, 0.89 colors, 0.33 position, 0.60 color attribution.
- **Human preference** (Parti-prompts): the 8B model outperforms proprietary (DALL-E 3, Ideogram) and open (SDXL-Turbo, Playground, PixArt-α, Würstchen) models on visual aesthetics, prompt following, and typography.
- **Sampling efficiency** (Table 6): larger models need fewer steps (depth=38 loses only 2.71% relative CLIP score at 5/50 steps vs. 4.30% for depth=15), attributed to better fitting the straight-path objective (shorter path lengths: 185.96 vs. 191.13).
- **Flexible text encoders**: trained with three encoders (CLIP-L/14, OpenCLIP bigG/14, T5-XXL) with 46.3% individual dropout; at inference T5 can be dropped with limited impact (50% win rate on aesthetics, 46% on prompt following, 38% on typography), trading performance for memory.

### Conclusion (p. 12)

The paper presents a scaling analysis of rectified flows for text-to-image synthesis: a novel timestep sampling improving on previous diffusion training formulations while retaining few-step sampling advantages; the MM-DiT architecture respecting the multimodal nature of the task; and a scaling study to 8B parameters showing validation loss predicts benchmark and human-preference performance, competitive with state-of-the-art proprietary models, with no saturation in the scaling trend.

### Appendices (p. 17–28)

- **Background** (p. 17): diffusion models, rectified flows (connections to CNFs and diffusion; no simulation needed during training; faster ODEs than probability-flow ODEs), and scaling of diffusion models (transformer scaling properties; U-Net dominance; DiT as recent exception).
- **Flow matching details** (p. 20–21): proof that u_t generates p_t via the continuity equation; equivalence of L_FM and L_CFM. **Image/text representations**: autoencoder with downsampling factor 8; text encoded with CLIP L/14 (pooled 768) and OpenCLIP bigG/14 (pooled 1280) concatenated to c_vec ∈ R²⁰⁴⁸, plus penultimate hidden states concatenated to c_CLIP_ctxt ∈ R⁷⁷ˣ²⁰⁴⁸ and T5-v1.1-XXL final hidden representation c_T5_ctxt ∈ R⁷⁷ˣ⁴⁰⁹⁶, zero-padded and concatenated to c_ctxt ∈ R¹⁵⁴ˣ⁴⁰⁹⁶.
- **Experiment preliminaries** (p. 21): ImageNet converted to text-to-image with "a photo of a ⟨class name⟩" captions; batch size 1024, AdamW, learning rate 10⁻⁴, 1000 warmup steps, EMA decay 0.99; text-encoder dropout 46.4% (≈10% unconditional steps); stratified loss evaluation on eight equally spaced timesteps; Euler discretization with six sampler settings (50 steps at guidance 1.0/2.5/5.0; 5/10/25 steps at guidance 5.0).
- **DPO** (p. 23): LoRA (rank 128) on all linear layers, 4k iterations (2B model) and 2k (8B); human raters prefer DPO-finetuned models for prompt following and quality.
- **Instruction-based editing** (p. 23–24): concatenating input and target latents along channels before patching; the 2B edit model performs zero-shot text manipulation in images (not in training data), which an SDXL-based editing model on the same data could not reproduce.
- **Data preprocessing** (p. 24–28): precomputing embeddings saves memory and compute (T5 requires 19.05 GB and adds 98.3% step time if run in-loop); deduplication via SSCD embeddings, 16,000 clusters, FAISS-based clustering; memorization assessment following Carlini et al. shows SSCD deduplication at threshold 0.5 yields a 5× reduction in potentially memorized examples (clique size 10).

## GLOSSAR

- **Rectified Flow (RF)** — a generative model formulation connecting data and noise on a straight line, z_t = (1−t)x₀ + tϵ, whose ODE can be simulated with few steps.
- **MM-DiT (Multimodal Diffusion Transformer)** — the paper's transformer backbone using two separate weight sets for text and image tokens, joined for the attention operation to enable bidirectional information flow.
- **Logit-normal sampling** — a timestep sampling density that biases training towards intermediate timesteps, with location m and scale s; the paper's best-performing variant is rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00).
- **Mode sampling with heavy tails** — a timestep density with strictly positive density on [0,1], parameterized by scale s, favoring the midpoint (positive s) or endpoints (negative s).
- **CosMap** — a timestep sampling mapping for RF models that matches the log-SNR weighting of the cosine schedule.
- **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)** — a tractable training objective regressing conditional vector fields that generate the marginal probability paths between data and noise.
- **QK-normalization** — applying RMSNorm (with learnable scale) to Q and K embeddings before attention to prevent attention-logit growth and entropy collapse during high-resolution training.
- **Resolution-dependent timestep shifting** — mapping timesteps between resolutions via σ(t, n) = σ(t, m) so noise levels match perceptual uncertainty; shift value α = √(m/n), set to 3.0 at 1024².
- **CrossDiT** — a DiT variant that cross-attends to text tokens instead of sequence-wise concatenation, used as a comparison baseline.
- **Signal-to-noise ratio (λ_t)** — defined as log(a²_t/b²_t), used to reparameterize the flow matching loss as a noise-prediction objective.
