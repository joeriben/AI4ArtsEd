# Do Vendi Scores Converge with Finite Samples?. Truncated Vendi Score for Finite-Sample Convergence Guarantees

## PROFIL

The paper "Do Vendi Scores Converge with Finite Samples? Truncated Vendi Score for Finite-Sample Convergence Guarantees" was written by Azim Ospanov and Farzan Farnia of the Department of Computer Science and Engineering, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, as a research contribution to the machine-learning literature on evaluating generative AI models without reference data. Its subject is the statistical convergence of the Vendi score, a reference-free diversity metric defined via the Von Neumann entropy of a normalized kernel similarity matrix (Friedman and Dieng, 2023), and of the RKE score, which uses order-2 Rényi entropy (Jalali et al., 2023). The authors' position is that the Vendi score, when estimated with the restricted sample sizes (n ⪅ 20,000) imposed by the O(n³) eigendecomposition cost, does not converge to its population value for infinite-dimensional kernels such as the Gaussian/RBF kernel, while it does converge for finite-dimension kernels (e.g., cosine similarity) with n = O(d) samples; as a remedy they propose the t-truncated Vendi score, built from the top t eigenvalues with the tail mass redistributed, which provably converges with n = O(t) samples regardless of kernel dimension, and they prove that the RKE score converges universally for all kernels. The paper contains: preliminaries on kernels, matrix-based Rényi entropies, and population definitions; convergence theorems (via the Vector Bernstein and Hoffman-Wielandt inequalities); the definition and analysis of the truncated score, including results showing that the Nyström method (Friedman and Dieng, 2023) and the FKEA method (Ospanov et al., 2024b) act as approximations of it; numerical experiments on ImageNet, FFHQ, a synthetic GPT-4 text dataset, Kinetics400, and StyleGAN3/StyleGAN-XL/DALL-E 3/SDXL outputs, with DINOv2, CLIP, SWaV, I3D, and text-embedding-3-large embeddings; and an appendix of proofs plus extended experiments on bandwidth selection, computation time, and embedding-space choice. It builds on the Vendi and RKE lines of work and positions itself against Shawe-Taylor et al. (2005) and Bach (2022), whose eigenvalue and entropy bounds do not guarantee convergence at practical sample sizes. For arts education, the paper is usable in contexts where the diversity of generative outputs (images, text, video) is measured or taught—e.g., courses or projects on AI-assisted artistic creation, generative-media evaluation, and computational aesthetics—wherever a convergent, reference-free diversity estimate is needed.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Background (pp. 1–2)

This paper by Azim Ospanov and Farzan Farnia (Department of Computer Science and Engineering, The Chinese University of Hong Kong) investigates whether the Vendi score—a popular reference-free metric for measuring the diversity of generative AI models—actually converges to its true (population) value when computed from the limited sample sizes used in practice. Because the Vendi score requires an eigendecomposition of an n × n kernel matrix at O(n³) cost, practitioners typically use fewer than 20,000 samples. The authors demonstrate that this restricted sample size may be insufficient for convergence when infinite-dimensional kernels (e.g., Gaussian/RBF) are used, and they propose a remedy: the "t-truncated Vendi score," which provably converges with only n = O(t) samples. They also prove that the alternative RKE score converges universally for all kernels. The code is available at https://github.com/aziksh-ospanov/truncated-vendi.

### Introduction and Research Questions (pp. 1–2)

The paper frames the evaluation of generative models without reference data as a key challenge. The Vendi score (Friedman and Dieng, 2023) quantifies diversity via the entropy of a kernel similarity matrix: given sorted eigenvalues λ₁ ≥ ··· ≥ λₙ of the normalized matrix (1/n)K, the order-1 Vendi score is the exponential of the Von Neumann entropy of that matrix. The RKE score (Jalali et al., 2023) applies order-2 Rényi entropy instead, reducing to the inverse-squared Frobenius norm of the normalized kernel matrix. The central question: does the Vendi score estimated with n ⪅ 20,000 samples converge to its asymptotic ("population") value under infinite sampling?

### Results on Vendi's Convergence (pp. 2–3)

The authors distinguish two kernel types:

1. **Finite-dimension kernels** (e.g., cosine similarity, polynomial kernels): a sample size n = O(d), where d is the feature dimension, suffices for convergence. Numerically, with the cosine kernel on ImageNet (using DINOv2 embeddings of dimension 768), the Vendi score nearly converges by 5,000 samples.
2. **Infinite-dimension kernels** (e.g., Gaussian/RBF): numerical results show the Vendi score keeps growing significantly even at 20,000 samples on ImageNet (bandwidth σ = 30), indicating lack of convergence. The authors note the heavy computational cost prohibits empirically testing larger sample sizes.

To bridge this gap, they introduce the **t-truncated Vendi score**, defined using only the top t eigenvalues λ₁ ≥ ··· ≥ λₜ of the kernel matrix. Each retained eigenvalue is shifted by a constant c = (1 − Σᵢ₌₁ᵗ λᵢ)/t so the truncated eigenvalues sum to 1 and form a valid probability model. When the kernel dimension d ≤ t, the truncated and original Vendi scores coincide; for infinite-dimensional kernels they may differ. The main theoretical result: n = O(t) samples always suffice to estimate the t-truncated population Vendi, regardless of kernel dimension. The authors further show that existing efficient computation methods—the Nyström method (Friedman and Dieng, 2023) and the FKEA method (Ospanov et al., 2024b)—can be viewed as approximations of the truncated Vendi score.

### Results on RKE's Convergence (p. 3)

For the RKE score, the authors prove a universal convergence guarantee holding for every kernel function: every order-α entropy score with α ≥ 2 converges to its population value within O(1/√n) error for n samples. The guarantee transfers to a truncated RKE as well, though truncation is unnecessary there. Numerically, the RKE score nearly converges with fewer than 10,000 samples for both cosine and Gaussian kernels.

The paper's stated contributions are: (1) analyzing statistical convergence of Vendi and RKE scores under restricted sample sizes n ⪅ 2 × 10⁴; (2) providing numerical evidence of the Vendi score's lack of convergence for infinite-dimensional kernels; (3) introducing the truncated Vendi score as a converging extension from finite to infinite dimension; (4) demonstrating universal convergence of the RKE score.

### Related Work (pp. 3–4)

The paper situates itself in four literatures: (1) reference-based diversity metrics (FID, KID, Recall, Coverage, MAUVE) versus reference-free ones (Vendi, RKE); (2) statistical convergence analysis of kernel matrix eigenvalues—distinguishing their work from Shawe-Taylor et al. (2005), whose concentration bounds center on Eₘ[λ̂(S)] rather than asymptotic eigenvalues, and from Bach (2022), whose Von Neumann entropy bound may not guarantee convergence at practical sample sizes (below 10,000); (3) efficient computation of matrix-based entropy, including Dong et al. (2023), incomplete Cholesky and CUR decompositions, the Nyström method (O(nm²)), and Random Fourier Features/FKEA (O(n)); (4) the impact of embedding spaces (DINOv2, InceptionV3, CLIP) on diversity evaluation.

### Preliminaries: Kernels, Entropies, and Population Definitions (pp. 4–5)

A kernel function k is PSD by definition; by Aronzajn's Theorem it admits a feature map φ with k(x, x′) = ⟨φ(x), φ(x′)⟩. Normalized kernels satisfy k(x, x) = 1, so the eigenvalues of (1/n)K sum to 1 and form a probability model. The order-α Rényi entropy of a unit-trace PSD matrix is defined via its eigenvalues; for α = 2 it equals log(1/‖A‖²_F), and for α = 1 it is the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalues. The key structural observation: (1/n)K = (1/n)ΦΦᵀ shares non-zero eigenvalues with the empirical kernel covariance matrix Ĉₓ = (1/n)ΦᵀΦ, so their matrix entropies are equal. This motivates **Definition 1**: the order-α population Vendi is defined via the matrix-based entropy of the population kernel covariance matrix C̃ₓ = E[φ(x)φ(x)ᵀ]. The population RKE equals the population Vendi₂.

### Theoretical Convergence Results (pp. 5–6)

- **Theorem 1**: For a normalized kernel, the vector of sorted eigenvalues λ̂ₙ of (1/n)K concentrates around the sorted eigenvalue vector λ̃ of C̃ₓ; for n ≥ 2 + 8 log(1/δ), the ℓ₂ gap is bounded with probability at least 1 − δ (via Vector Bernstein inequality and Hoffman-Wielandt inequality).
- **Corollary 1**: For every α ≥ 2 (including RKE = Vendi₂), a dimension-free convergence bound holds—the RKE score converges universally across all kernels.
- **Corollary 2**: For finite-dimension kernel maps (dim(φ) = d < ∞), order-1 Vendi (with n ≥ 32e² log(2/δ)) and orders 1 < α < 2 converge with n = O(d^{2−α}) samples. This matches the numerical observations for the cosine kernel.

### Truncated Vendi Score and Approximation Methods (pp. 6–7)

**Definition 2** formalizes the t-truncated Vendi: take the top t eigenvalues of (1/n)K, define Sₜ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵗ λᵢ, and shift each by (1 − Sₜ)/t to obtain a probability vector; the score is the order-α entropy of this vector. Remark 1 notes the corresponding deterministic t-truncated population Vendi obtained by truncating the eigenspectrum of C̃ₓ. The truncation finds the probability model with minimum ℓ₂ distance to the top-t eigenvalue vector.

- **Theorem 2**: The gap between the t-truncated population Vendi and the empirical t-truncated Vendi is bounded with probability at least 1 − δ for n ≥ 2 + 8 log(1/δ); the truncation parameter t plays the role of a bounded feature dimension, so O(t) samples suffice.
- **Theorem 3**: (a) For shift-invariant kernels, the FKEA method with t random Fourier features approximates the truncated Vendi with a bounded gap; (b) the Nyström method with parameter t, assuming the r-th largest eigenvalue satisfies λᵣ ≤ τ and t ≥ rτ log(n), approximates the truncated Vendi with probability at least 1 − δ − 2n⁻³.

### Numerical Results (pp. 7–9)

Experiments used 20,000 samples per score computation, cosine similarity (finite-dimension) and Gaussian (infinite-dimension) kernels, with bandwidths matched to prior work (e.g., σ = 30 for ImageNet, σ = 0.6 for text, σ = 4.0 for Kinetics400). The truncation parameter t was matched to the Nyström and FKEA rank hyperparameters. Datasets: ImageNet, FFHQ, a synthetic text dataset of 400k GPT-4-generated paragraphs about 100 randomly selected countries (text-embedding-3-large, dimension 3072), and the Kinetics400 video dataset (I3D embedding, dimension 1024).

Key findings: with the cosine kernel, the Vendi score converges rapidly and the truncated and Nyström versions are nearly identical to the original; with the Gaussian kernel, the order-1 Vendi score does not converge at 20k samples and keeps growing, while the t-truncated Vendi score with t = 10,000 converged shortly after 10,000 samples, with Nyström and FKEA estimates converging to the same limit. Diversity-correlation experiments applied the truncation technique to StyleGAN3 (FFHQ) and StyleGAN-XL (ImageNet) models, varying the truncation coefficient ψ to simulate different diversity levels; the estimated truncated Vendi score (averaged over 5 independent datasets of size 20k) correlated with underlying diversity for orders α = 1, 1.5, 2.

### Conclusion (p. 9)

The paper concludes that restricted sample sizes (below 10,000 in practice) are unproblematic when the kernel feature dimension is bounded, but the Vendi score with infinite-dimensional kernels (e.g., Gaussian) does not converge. The truncated population Vendi is proposed as an alternative target quantity, with Nyström and FKEA methods concentrating around it. Future directions include relating other kernel approximation techniques to the truncated population Vendi and a comprehensive computational–statistical trade-off analysis.

### Supplementary Material: Proofs (pp. 13–19)

The appendix proves the main results. Theorem 1 uses the Vector Bernstein Inequality (Gross, 2011; Kohler and Lucchi, 2017) applied to the random vectors φ(xᵢ) ⊗ φ(xᵢ), combined with the Hoffman-Wielandt inequality to bound the eigenvalue gap. Corollary 2's proof for α = 1 relies on two lemmas: Lemma 2 bounds the entropy difference |g(α) − g(β)| by c log(1/c) for probability entries within c ≤ 1/e (proved by case analysis around the maximum of g(z) = z log(1/z) at 1/e); Lemma 3 shows that if ‖u‖₂ ≤ ε for a non-negative d-dimensional vector, the entropy gap is at most √d·ε·log(√d/ε), proved via KKT conditions with the maximizer u* = (ε/√d)·1 (also implied by Schur-concavity of entropy). Corollary 1's proof uses the α-norm representation of the Vendi score and the triangle inequality. Theorem 2's proof uses Lemma 4: the truncated eigenvalue vector is the ℓ₂-projection of the original eigenvalue vector onto the t-dimensional probability simplex (proved via KKT conditions); since projection onto a convex set is non-expansive, the eigenvalue concentration transfers to the truncated scores. Theorem 3(a) applies Vector Bernstein to the FKEA kernel approximation; part (b) uses a spectral norm bound from Xu et al. (2015) for Nyström approximation plus Weyl's inequality.

### Additional Numerical Results (pp. 19–28)

- **Extended convergence experiments** (Figures 12–13): on truncated StyleGAN3-t FFHQ (σ = 35) and StyleGAN-XL ImageNet (σ = 40) with DINOv2 embeddings, the truncated, Nyström, and FKEA variants converge to the truncated Vendi statistic across truncation factors ψ. The authors note that finite-dimensional kernels (cosine) remain relatively constant under incremental diversity changes, while infinite-dimensional kernels with a bandwidth parameter σ are useful for controlling sensitivity to diversity change.
- **Bandwidth σ selection** (Figure 10): smaller bandwidths (e.g., σ = 20, 30) treat redundant samples as distinct modes, inflating cluster counts and slowing convergence; large bandwidth (σ = 60) yields near-instant convergence where n = 100 and n = 1000 give almost the same diversity value.
- **Diversity tables** (Tables 1–2): on DALL-E 3 and SDXL generated MSCOCO data, Vendi-1.0 keeps growing with n (e.g., DALL-E 3: 239.91 at n = 2000 rising to 392.36 at n = 8000), while RKE is nearly flat (13.47 to 13.56), and Vendi-t, FKEA-Vendi, and Nystrom-Vendi stabilize (e.g., DALL-E 3 Vendi-t: 239.91 to 354.8; SDXL Vendi-t: 187.17 at n = 2000 to 267.39 at n = 10000). Recall and Coverage metrics are also reported.
- **Computation time** (Table 3): exact Vendi takes 97s at 10,000 samples and 631s at 20,000 (infeasible beyond 30,000), while FKEA-Vendi scales from 19s at 10,000 to 124s at 70,000 samples, and Nystrom-Vendi from 31s to 164s over the same range.
- **Embedding space selection** (Figures 14–17): experiments repeated with CLIP (σ = 5.0) and SWaV (σ = 1.0) embeddings on ImageNet and truncated StyleGAN3 FFHQ confirm that FKEA, Nyström, and t-truncated Vendi correlate with increasing diversity under arbitrary embedding choices. Additional diversity experiments cover text (Figure 9, varying number of countries, σ = 0.5) and video (Figure 11, Kinetics400 with varying number of classes, σ = 4.0).

## GLOSSAR

- **Vendi score** — A reference-free diversity metric; the exponential of the Von Neumann (order-1) entropy of the normalized kernel similarity matrix (1/n)K of generated samples.
- **RKE score (Rényi Kernel Entropy / RKE mode count)** — A diversity score applying order-2 Rényi entropy to the normalized kernel matrix, equal to the inverse-squared Frobenius norm of that matrix.
- **Population Vendi** — The asymptotic limit of the Vendi score under infinite sampling, defined via the matrix-based entropy of the population kernel covariance matrix C̃ₓ = E[φ(x)φ(x)ᵀ].
- **Population RKE** — The population limit of the RKE score; identical to the population Vendi₂ since RKE and Vendi₂ coincide.
- **t-truncated Vendi score** — A modified Vendi statistic using only the top t eigenvalues of the kernel matrix, each shifted by c = (1 − Σᵢ₌₁ᵗ λᵢ)/t so they sum to 1; provably converges to its population limit with n = O(t) samples.
- **t-truncated population Vendi** — The deterministic quantity obtained by applying the same truncation process to the eigenspectrum of the population kernel covariance matrix C̃ₓ.
- **Normalized kernel** — A kernel satisfying k(x, x) = 1 for every x, so the eigenvalues of (1/n)K sum to 1 and form a probability model.
- **Finite-dimension kernel** — A kernel whose feature map φ has finite dimension d (e.g., cosine similarity, polynomial kernels).
- **Infinite-dimension kernel** — A kernel with no finite-dimensional feature map (e.g., the Gaussian/RBF kernel).
- **Order-α Vendi score** — The generalization of the Vendi score applying order-α Rényi entropy to the eigenvalues of the normalized kernel matrix.
- **FKEA method** — A computation-reduction method (Ospanov et al., 2024b) using random Fourier features, scaling linearly O(n) with dataset size; shown to approximate the truncated Vendi.
- **Nyström method (for Vendi)** — An approximation method (Pasarkar and Dieng, 2024) using m randomly selected rows of the kernel matrix with O(nm²) complexity; shown to approximate the truncated Vendi.
- **Truncation parameter t** — The integer hyperparameter of the truncated Vendi score specifying how many top eigenvalues are retained; plays the role of a bounded feature dimension in convergence guarantees.
- **Truncation coefficient ψ** — The parameter of the StyleGAN truncation trick used in experiments to simulate generative models with varying underlying diversity.
