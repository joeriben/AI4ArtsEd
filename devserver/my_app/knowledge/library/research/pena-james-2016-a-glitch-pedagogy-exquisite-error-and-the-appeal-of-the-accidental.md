# A Glitch Pedagogy: Exquisite Error and the Appeal of the Accidental

## PROFIL

The text argues that computer glitches function as creative and productive forces within the lived curriculum by extending and radicalizing Dewey's principle of 'consummatory experience' to digital technologies. It sets itself against the negative stigma attached to error, the instrumentalist design of proprietary digital devices that address the "whats" without the "hows," and Aoki's warning that computer technology reduces reality to a limited standing reserve of manifestations of the real. By defining glitch as a non-moralizable, contextual event, the authors position computational error as a prompt for reflexive inquiry that disrupts perceived affordances and interrogates underlying processes. For arts education, this framework provides a method for utilizing digital accidents and procedural disruptions as hands-on inquiry to foster improvisational experimentation and metaknowledge.

## WISSEN

### Glitch as Consummatory Experience

The paper extends and radicalizes Dewey's (1934) pedagogical principle of 'consummatory experience' by experimenting with computer glitches as provocations of accepted norms of user interaction with digital technologies. Computational error, logical accidents, and procedural glitches are positioned as creative and productive forces within the lived curriculum. The argument holds that disruption of programmed processes—triggered by incommensurable informational input—generates unique, educational by-products. This troubling of expected outcomes is claimed fundamental to understanding our digital humanity. These irregularities carry the same learning potential as learning from mistakes and fortunate accidents in the arts, sciences, and lifelong learning broadly.

### Accident and Error

Error is socially stigmatized yet foundational to all learning: the freedom to err enables strategic comprehension and expression. Error is also foundational to logic, understood as pattern recognition by an observer; error accelerates processing rather than slowing it. Because all coded events entail a goal plus complex constraints, even small errors can cause system failure — yet even artificial agents must learn from errors to make good decisions (Armstrong's value-selection problem). Industrial automation sought to eradicate human error but could not fully automate where materials vary constantly (shoes vs. lamps); humans remained necessary to negotiate non-standard input. Understanding of error and accidental beauty comes from art, not industry: jazz improvisation functions as a closed-loop feedback system comparing intended and actual output, treating errors as a motor of creativity. Glitch exploration similarly requires improvisation within constraints; past basic learning, errors become indistinguishable from exceptional results, initiating new learning and innovation. This transmutation of error into metaknowledge aligns with Dewey's consummatory experience — process over product — and the Process Writing movement's emphasis on compositional experimentation.

### Inquiry, Technology, and Glitch

Dewey's inquiry is a hands-on approach where tools of thought are valued for conveying further knowledge, not mere accuracy. *Consummatory experience*—the interplay of means and ends toward a culminating experience—should emerge from teaching through reflexive inquiry. Proprietary digital devices hinder underlying processes by addressing the "whats" without the "hows," putting instrumentalism at odds with consummatory experience. Additionally, as Aoki warned, computer technology risks reducing reality to a limited *standing reserve of manifestations of the real*, obscuring alternative possibilities.

We contend that digital devices can preserve consummatory experience and reveal alternative realities by adopting *glitch* as a prompt for inquiry. While glitch originated as a lack of control, in art it is a desirable ontological condition requiring a norm to digress from. We define a glitch as a *non-moralizable*, contextual event. Approaching the glitch as a product instrumental to understanding its process, glitches become tools of inquiry that disrupt the standing reserve of realities and interrogate underlying processes, collaboration, agency, authorship, and intentionality.

### Glitch as Hands-On Inquiry: Stitch-Skipping and the Disruption of Affordances

Glitch is positioned not merely as a theoretical concept but as a performative, hands-on form of inquiry that provokes methodological innovation. Four cases illustrate this; the first three address static imagery, automated literacy, and language translation respectively, while the fourth applies a recombinant process across all three.

In Case 1, the argument begins from the concept of *perceived affordances* — noticeable hints of an artifact's interaction possibilities. Glitches are provoked by deliberately disrupting these affordances or contravening explicit instructions. *Stitch-skipping* consists in glitching panorama pictures by purposefully digressing from the creation protocol. The method: first produce a canonical reference panorama, then, under similar conditions, intentionally move the camera or scene elements in non-normative ways, and compare.

Panoramas are assembled via *stitching* — matching edges of contiguous images with a generous margin of error within which glitches emerge. These glitches visualize the sequential process of construction, revealing time and sequence alien to standard photography. The photographer becomes formally present in the image despite physical absence, exposing the malleability of representation and disrupting the illusion of reality, thereby enhancing critical sensitivity to the politics of representation.

### Voice-Vaguing: Exploiting Speech Recognition Limits through Glitch Pedagogy

Automated speech recognition (STT) has advanced through deep learning algorithms that are voice-independent, predicting utterances via spectral analysis of big data rather than matching to an idealized speaker. These systems anticipate linguistic expression feedforward, emulating human guessing. The authors argue that errors in interpretation, especially with non-normative pronunciation or syntax, can be exploited pedagogically through **voice-vaguing**—using improvised and extended vocal technique to create ambiguity and il-logical interference in STT input data. Working from **glitch pedagogy**, they input online videos of infants babbling into proprietary STT software, targeting a known weak spot: young voices, whose formant distribution disrupts spectral analysis. Regardless of language background, STT reduced complex babble—modeled on adult inflections and pauses—to monosyllabic outputs, mainly "are," "or," or "her." This reduction from humanist-sounding speech to near-empty transcription became the data source for further iterative glitch experiments, building critical media literacies through an iterative remix process.

### Glot-swapping and Iterative Glitch as Critical Digital Literacy

Glot-swapping feeds non-English audio into English auto-captioning software, forcing homophonic mistranslation. The resulting texts disproportionately surface corporate entities (Facebook, Google) and nationalistic/religious references, exposing a culturally biased, even xenophobic skew in algorithms trained on US-based big data. This bias inheres in dominant user-group discourses embedded in AI systems, positioning critical literacy scholars to scrutinize automated language processing as governments and institutions increasingly rely on it.

Case 4 extends all prior techniques — voice-vaguing, stitch-skipping, glot-swapping — into a recombinant, iterative code-bending process chaining text-to-speech, OCR, and translation software. Unpredictable outputs reposition the educator as learner. Comparing graphically glitched versus sonically glitched source texts yields distinct distortions: graphemic-level distortion versus visceral, corporate/political overtones (Google, Obama), confirming Case 3 patterns and prompting further transmediation research. Iterative texts are never finished; this models the critical digital literacies educators hope to cultivate. Crucially, trained AI entities begin to train the user — programs write the user, rendering formal textual operations facile then redundant, while users lose awareness of what automation does, how well, and to what ends.

### Glitch Pedagogy: Conclusion and Manifesto

Glitch pedagogy gives learners partial, biased access to the big data substrate underpinning contemporary voice and visual recognition systems. Children raised on digital devices form a "strong and peculiar bond" with algorithmic agents that shape semiosis and psyche; social algorithms function like genres—pre-existing their users, shaping operators while being reshaped through misappropriation of norms. Glitch pedagogy interrogates the served/server relationship dialogically, yielding "surprising resonant clusters of meaning" through collaborative human-robot expression. Framing learning as a game, it celebrates mistakes and processing errors as central to creativity, inquiry, and knowledge construction. The manifesto provokes curriculum thinkers to reconceive digital devices' educational affordances.

## GLOSSAR

- **consummatory experience** — Dewey's pedagogical principle designating an experience that reaches a fulfilling, integrative closure, here radicalized through glitch-induced disruption.
- **glitch** — A computational error, logical accident, or procedural irregularity repositioned as a creative and productive pedagogical force.
- **digital humanity** — The understanding of human being shaped through engagement with digital technologies, including their failures and irregularities.
- **closed-loop feedback system** — A skilled performance model of improvisation involving real-time comparison between intended and actual output, enabling error-correction.
- **error-as-learning cycles** — The iterative process by which an artist incorporates errors as new fundamentals, turning mistakes into innovation and beauty.
- **standing reserve of manifestations of the real** — The limited set of realities that computer technology reveals, obscuring other possibilities.
- **non-moralizable** — A characteristic of a glitch as a contextual event that is neither inherently good nor bad.
- **stitch-skipping** — The practice of glitching panoramic photographs by deliberately deviating from the standard sweeping-motion protocol required for their creation.
- **perceived affordances** — Noticeable hints about the use or interaction possibilities of an artifact, inferable from its design or interface.
- **stitching** — The algorithmic process of assembling a panoramic image from a sequence of discrete photographs by matching the edges of contiguous images.
