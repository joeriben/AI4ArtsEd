# UNESCO World Conference on Cultural Policies and Sustainable Development (MONDIACULT 2022): Final Declaration

## PROFIL

The MONDIACULT 2022 Final Declaration outlines commitments to foster an enabling environment for cultural rights, artistic freedom, and the systemic integration of culture into education amidst contemporary global challenges. It reaffirms the fundamental principles of the Declarations adopted at the UNESCO World Conferences on Cultural Policies (Mexico 1982, Stockholm 1998) and builds on the 2001 Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity. 

The text references multiple UNESCO standard-setting instruments, including the Recommendation concerning the Status of the Artist (1980) and the Convention on the Protection and Promotion of the Diversity of Cultural Expressions (2005). It calls for enhancing synergies between culture and education by expanding learning outcomes, multilingualism, arts education, and digital literacy.

The document is applicable to arts-education platforms focusing on the systemic integration of culture into formal, informal and non-formal education. It is relevant for disciplines involving technical and vocational education and training (TVET) in the cultural sector and the educational and social role of museums, creative hubs, libraries, archives and cultural institutions.

### Call to Action

Commitments to foster an enabling environment for cultural rights by:
- (i) supporting inclusive access to culture and participation in cultural life
- (ii) strengthening economic and social rights of artists, cultural professionals and practitioners, facilitating mobility, reinforcing intellectual property
- (iii) protecting and promoting artistic freedom and freedom of expression
- (iv) protecting and fostering the diversity of cultural contents, as well as linguistic diversity
- (v) implementing legal and public policy frameworks that uphold the rights of peoples and communities to their cultural identity and heritage
- (vi) expanding efforts to promote the protection, return and restitution of cultural property, including in consultation with the populations concerned and with their free, prior and informed consent

Enhancing synergies between culture and education by:
- (i) expanding learning outcomes and enhancing quality education, multilingualism, arts education and digital literacy
- (ii) fostering the systemic integration of culture into formal, informal and non-formal education
- (iii) supporting technical and vocational education and training (TVET) in the cultural sector
- (iv) investing in the educational and social role of museums, creative hubs, libraries, archives and cultural institutions
- Development of a revised UNESCO framework on culture and arts education

Protection of cultural heritage in crises, referencing: UNESCO 1954 Convention and its Protocols (1954 and 1999), UNESCO 1970, 1972, 2001, 2003 and 2005 Conventions, UNIDROIT 1995 Convention, UNESCO Declaration concerning the Intentional Destruction of Cultural Heritage (2003).

Integrating cultural heritage into climate change discussions, referencing: UNESCO 1972, 2001, 2003 and 2005 Conventions, UNESCO 2011 and 2015 Recommendations, UNFCCC, CBD, WMO, FAO, UNEP, IPCC, Flexible Mechanism for addressing the impacts of climate change on cultural and natural heritage (2020).

Fighting illicit trafficking of cultural property via UNESCO 1970 Convention, INTERPOL, WCO, UNIDROIT, ICOM, UNODC by:
- (i) strengthening advocacy and action concerning the impact of illicit trafficking
- (ii) encouraging the introduction of penal or administrative sanctions in national legislations
- (iii) promoting the development of national and local capacities in all countries
- (iv) fostering increased and effective cooperation with art market actors, notably with regard to reinforcing the codification of the certificate of origin of cultural property
- (v) ensuring the engagement, mobilisation and coordination of all stakeholders, including through digital technologies and online platforms

Return and restitution of cultural property via UNESCO Intergovernmental Committee for Promoting the Return of Cultural Property; updating International Code of Ethics for Traders in Cultural Property.

Sustainable cultural and creative sectors via UNESCO 2005 Convention, UNESCO 1980 Recommendation, 2017 Operational Guidelines on the Implementation of the Convention in the Digital Environment, 2021 UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence.

## GLOSSAR

- **cultural value chain** — The full sequence of cultural production, distribution, and access across which structural fragilities and inequalities manifest.
- **cultural ecosystem** — The cultural sector understood as an interconnected whole, profoundly disrupted by the COVID-19 crisis.
- **intentional destruction of cultural heritage** — Deliberate targeting of cultural heritage in armed conflicts, subject of a dedicated 2003 UNESCO Declaration.
- **free, prior and informed consent** — Consent obtained from concerned populations without coercion, prior to a decision, and with full understanding of the implications.
- **technical and vocational education and training (TVET)** — Education and training that equips individuals with structural skills for employment in the cultural sector.
- **Flexible Mechanism for addressing the impacts of climate change on cultural and natural heritage (2020)** — A mechanism established in 2020 to address the impacts of climate change on cultural and natural heritage.
