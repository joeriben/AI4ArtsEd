# Basic Texts of the 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage (2024 Edition)

## PROFIL

- The document presents the foundational texts and 2024 amendments of the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, defining Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) as practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, and skills that communities recognize as part of their cultural heritage.
- It outlines State Party obligations, the functions of the General Assembly and Committee, and mechanisms such as the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, and the Register of Good Safeguarding Practices.
- The text explicitly states that its provisions do not alter protections established by the 1972 World Heritage Convention, nor do they affect obligations regarding intellectual property and biological resources.
- For an arts-education platform, the text applies to disciplines including performing arts, oral traditions and expressions, traditional craftsmanship, and social practices, rituals and festive events.
- Typical educational situations involve integrating safeguarding principles into public policies, facilitating formal and non-formal education transmission, capacity-building, and ensuring the widest possible participation of communities in the management of their living heritage.

## WISSEN

### Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage (2003)

Adopted Paris, 17 October 2003; 183 States Parties as of 2024.

**Purposes (Article 1):**
- (a) to safeguard the intangible cultural heritage;
- (b) to ensure respect for the intangible cultural heritage of the communities, groups and individuals concerned;
- (c) to raise awareness at the local, national and international levels of the importance of the intangible cultural heritage, and of ensuring mutual appreciation thereof;
- (d) to provide for international cooperation and assistance.

**2024 amendments** (following Global reflection on listing mechanisms 2018–2022, General Assembly June 2024): simplified selection criteria for the Register of Good Safeguarding Practices; enlarged access to International Assistance — preparatory assistance now available to States with no element inscribed on the Representative List (excluding multinational nominations).

### Basic Texts

**Definitions (Art. 2):** 'Intangible Cultural Heritage' (ICH) = practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, skills, and associated instruments, objects, artefacts and cultural spaces that communities, groups and, in some cases, individuals recognize as part of their cultural heritage; transmitted across generations, constantly recreated; compatible with international human rights instruments, mutual respect, and sustainable development. Domains: (a) oral traditions and expressions, including language; (b) performing arts; (c) social practices, rituals and festive events; (d) knowledge and practices concerning nature and the universe; (e) traditional craftsmanship. 'Safeguarding' = measures ensuring viability: identification, documentation, research, preservation, protection, promotion, enhancement, transmission (formal and non-formal education), revitalization.

**Relationship (Art. 3):** Does not alter 1972 World Heritage Convention protections or affect intellectual property/biological resource obligations.

**Bodies:** General Assembly (sovereign body, meets biennially). Committee: 18 members initially, expanding to 24 at 50 States Parties; 4-year terms, half renewed biennially, no consecutive terms; equitable geographical representation and rotation. Committee functions: promote/monitor implementation; guidance on best practices; prepare Fund resource plan and operational directives; examine State Party reports; decide on list inscriptions (Arts. 16–18) and international assistance (Art. 22). UNESCO Secretariat assists. Advisory NGOs accredited by General Assembly.

**State Party obligations (Arts. 11–12):** Take measures to safeguard ICH in its territory; identify and define elements with participation of communities, groups and NGOs; draw up and regularly update one or more inventories of ICH present in its territory.

### Safeguarding Measures, Lists, and International Assistance (Articles 13–24)

States Parties shall adopt general policies integrating safeguarding into planning, designate competent bodies, foster studies (especially for heritage in danger), and adopt legal/technical/financial measures including training institutions, documentation institutions, and access respecting customary practices (Art. 13). Education, awareness-raising, and capacity-building shall target the general public, young people, and concerned communities; non-formal transmission and protection of natural spaces and places of memory are promoted (Art. 14). Widest possible participation of communities, groups, and individuals in management shall be ensured (Art. 15).

The Committee establishes and publishes two lists upon State Party proposals: the **Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** (visibility, dialogue, cultural diversity) (Art. 16) and the **List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding** (at State Party request; in extreme urgency, Committee may inscribe in consultation with the State Party) (Art. 17). Criteria for both lists are approved by the General Assembly. The Committee also periodically selects and promotes national, subregional, and regional programmes reflecting Convention principles, with special attention to developing countries (Art. 18).

International cooperation includes information exchange, joint initiatives, and assistance mechanisms (Art. 19). Assistance purposes: safeguarding heritage on the Urgent Safeguarding List, inventory preparation, support for programmes/projects, and other Committee-deemed purposes (Art. 20). Forms include studies, experts, training, standard-setting, infrastructure, equipment, and financial/technical assistance including low-interest loans (Art. 21). Requests are examined per Committee procedures; emergencies prioritised; beneficiary States share costs within their resources and report on use of assistance (Arts. 22–24).

### Basic Texts

- **Fund**: The "Fund for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage" is established. Resources include State Party contributions, UNESCO appropriations, and other gifts. No incompatible conditions may be attached to contributions.
- **State Party obligations**: States Parties pay a uniform percentage contribution (max 1% of UNESCO regular budget contribution) at least every two years; States may declare not bound by this. States in arrears are ineligible for the Committee. States Parties must submit reports on implementation measures to the Committee.
- **Reporting and Lists**: The Committee reports to the General Assembly and UNESCO General Conference. Previously proclaimed "Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity" are incorporated into the Representative List; no further proclamations occur.
- **Final provisions**: Ratification, accession, entry into force (3 months after 30th instrument), denunciation (12 months), and depositary functions are governed by standard UNESCO procedures.

### Basic Texts: Amendments, Registration, and Operational Directives

- **Amendments (Art. 38):** Proposed by a State Party via written communication to the Director-General; circulated to all States Parties. If ≥ half reply favourably within six months, presented to the General Assembly. Adopted by two-thirds majority of States Parties present and voting. Enter into force three months after deposit of instruments by two-thirds of States Parties, solely for those that ratified. Exception: amendments to Article 5 (Committee member number) enter into force upon adoption.
- **Registration (Art. 40):** Convention registered with the UN Secretariat under Article 102 of the UN Charter.
- **Operational Directives:** Periodically revised per General Assembly resolutions; adopted at the 2nd session (2008), amended through the 10th session (2024). Latest version at ich.unesco.org/en/directives.
- **Urgent Safeguarding List criteria (U.1–U.6):** Element must constitute ICH (Art. 2); be in urgent/extremely urgent need of safeguarding; have a safeguarding plan; be nominated with widest community participation and free, prior and informed consent; be included in a national inventory (Arts. 11–12); in extreme urgency, State(s) consulted per Art. 17.3.
- **Representative List criteria (R.1–R.5):** Element constitutes ICH (Art. 2); inscription contributes to visibility, awareness, dialogue, cultural diversity; safeguarding measures elaborated; widest community participation with free, prior and informed consent; included in national inventory (Arts. 11–12).
- **Best practices selection criteria (G.1–G.4):** Involves safeguarding per Art. 2.3; demonstrated effectiveness for ICH viability; implemented with community participation and free, prior, sustained and informed consent; willingness to disseminate good practices. Committee gives special attention to developing countries and equitable geographic distribution.

### International Assistance, Multi-national Files, and Inscription Extension/Reduction

- All States Parties eligible for International Assistance; it supplements national efforts.
- Priority requests: (a) heritage on List in Need of Urgent Safeguarding; (b) inventory preparation (Articles 11–12); (c) national/subregional/regional safeguarding programmes; (d) preparatory assistance.
- Committee considers equitable geographical distribution, special needs of developing countries, cooperation potential, and multiplier effect.
- Emergency assistance permitted (Article 22).
- Decision criteria (A.1–A.7): community participation in preparation/implementation/evaluation; appropriate amount; feasible activities; lasting results; cost-sharing by beneficiary; capacity-building aim; compliance with prior financed activities.
- Multi-national nominations encouraged for elements spanning multiple States Parties; joint International Assistance requests permitted.
- Inscription may be extended or reduced at national/international level. Extension requires demonstrating all inscription criteria met and consent of originally consenting communities. Reduction requires free, prior and informed consent of communities proposed for removal. New nomination replaces original if inscribed; original remains if not.

### Submission and Evaluation of Files

**Forms**
- ICH-01: USL nominations (+ simultaneous International Assistance)
- ICH-02: RL nominations
- ICH-03: programmes/projects/activities
- ICH-01 RL to USL: transfer RL→USL
- ICH-02 USL to RL (with ICH-11): transfer USL→RL
- ICH-05: preparatory assistance
- ICH-04: International Assistance (except with USL nominations or RL→USL transfers)

Preparatory assistance for RL: only States Parties with no inscribed elements (except multinational). States Parties must involve communities, groups, individuals concerned. Withdrawal permitted before Committee examination.

**Evaluation Body**: 12 members (6 experts from non-Committee States Parties, 6 accredited NGOs); max 4 years; 1/4 renewed yearly. USL evaluation: element viability, safeguarding plan feasibility, disappearance risk. Recommendations: inscribe/not/refer; select/not/refer; approve/not/refer; maintain/remove (*enhanced follow-up*).

Extremely urgent (Criterion U.6): *Bureau of the Committee* may invite accelerated USL nomination; raisable by States Parties, community concerned, or advisory organization.

<!-- quelle: I.7 Submission of files … I.9 Nominations to the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding to be processed on an extremely urgent basis -->

### Examination of files by the Committee

- Ceiling of 60 files per two cycles, set two years in advance, covering nominations to the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Humanity, and best safeguarding practice proposals.
- Priority: States with no file in preceding cycle; States with no inscriptions and Urgent Safeguarding nominations; multi-national files; States with fewest inscriptions.
- Committee inscribes/selects/approves or refers for additional information; referral does not guarantee future inscription.

<!-- quelle: I.10 Examination of files by the Committee -->

### Transfer, Removal, and Best Practices

- No simultaneous inscription on both Lists.
- Transfer requires free, prior and informed consent of communities, groups, and individuals concerned.
- RL→USL (Form ICH-01 RL to USL): updated description, safeguarding plan, consent.
- USL→RL (Form ICH-02 USL to RL): updated description re viability, contribution to mutual respect/sustainable development, assessment of safeguarding plan, consent.
- Evaluation Body may recommend inclusion in Register of Good Safeguarding Practices.
- Committee removes element no longer satisfying criteria, particularly U.1/R.1 and U.4/R.4.
- Removal request by State Party, communities, groups, individuals, or third party (non-State submitters may remain anonymous).
- Secretariat registers and transmits; Committee may place under 'enhanced follow-up', remove to Intangible Cultural Heritage Repository, or maintain.
- Evaluation Body evaluates elements under 'enhanced follow-up'.
- Committee encourages research, documentation, dissemination of good practices; compiles information on measures/methodologies; provides guidance on best safeguarding practices.

### International Assistance and Timetable

- International Assistance requests (incl. preparatory) capped at US$100,000, except emergency requests and those with nominations to the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding or transfer requests.
- Emergency: State Party unable to overcome calamity, natural disaster, armed conflict, serious epidemic, or other event with severe consequences for intangible cultural heritage and bearers.
- Requests up to US$100,000 and emergency requests approved by Bureau of the Committee; requests with Urgent Safeguarding nominations/transfer requests approved by Committee.
- Timetable:
  - 31 Mar Y0: preparatory assistance deadline.
  - Dec Y0: transfer requests (Urgent Safeguarding to Representative List) deadline.
  - 31 Mar Y1: nominations (Urgent Safeguarding, Representative List), programme proposals deadline.
  - 30 Sep Y1: missing information deadline.
  - 31 Jan Y2: transfer requests (Representative List to Urgent Safeguarding) deadline.
  - Jun Y2: Evaluation Body meeting; dialogue process initiated.
  - Nov Y2: Committee examination and decisions.

### Basic Texts

- **I.16**: The Committee automatically incorporates pre-Convention "Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity" into the Representative List. Non-party States must consent in writing within one year; silence equals refusal, triggering withdrawal. For shared items, non-parties are invited to consent; absent consent, the Committee recommends refraining from harmful acts.
- **II.1**: Fund resources primarily grant International Assistance. Other uses: Reserve Fund; Committee functions; participation costs for developing States' experts; advisory services by NGOs/experts; participation of invited community/group members.
- **II.2**: Donors include UN agencies, States, public/private bodies, individuals. Contributions cannot have incompatible conditions or come from incompatible entities. Donors have no direct influence, receive no individual reporting, and agreements use a single exchange of letters.

### Benefits for donors; Participation of communities, experts and NGOs

- Donor recognition: States Parties and other public bodies listed alphabetically; private donors listed in decreasing order of contribution amount. Updated lists on the Convention website; printed version every two years at the General Assembly session.
- Private contributors may promote cooperation with the Committee for 24 months post-deposit; materials require advance Secretariat approval and may not explicitly advertise products or services.
- States Parties encouraged to recognize private contributions through fiscal mechanisms such as tax benefits.
- States Parties encouraged to create a consultative body or coordination mechanism for participation of communities, groups, individuals, experts, centres of expertise and research institutes in: identification and definition of elements; inventories; programmes and projects; nomination files; removal or transfer of elements between Lists.
- States Parties shall facilitate access by communities to research results and foster respect for access-governing practices.
- NGO accreditation criteria: proven competence in safeguarding; objectives conforming to the Convention; cooperation with communities in mutual respect; operational capacities including legal personality and at least four years of activity.

### Modalities for NGO Accreditation and Awareness-Raising Provisions

- **NGO accreditation**: Secretariat receives requests, recommends to Committee; Committee recommends to General Assembly (Article 9). Equitable geographical representation applies. Accredited NGOs must abide by applicable legal/ethical standards.
- **Quadrennial review** of each accredited NGO's contribution; termination or suspension may result.
- **Advisory functions** (Article 9.1): NGOs may provide evaluation reports on (a) nomination files for the List of ICH in Need of Urgent Safeguarding; (b) Article 18 programmes/projects/activities; (c) International Assistance requests; (d) effects of safeguarding plans for inscribed elements.
- **Accreditation requirements**: official name, objectives, address, founding date, country/countries of activity, operational capacity proof (regular active membership; legal personality; **at least four years** of existence and activity), ICH safeguarding activities, cooperation experience with communities/groups/practitioners. Form ICH-09; deadline 30 April of odd-numbered years.
- **Awareness-raising principles**: free, prior and informed consent of communities; respect for customary access practices (especially secret/sacred aspects); communities shall benefit. Prohibited: de-contextualization; marking communities as non-contemporary; facilitating discrimination; misappropriation of knowledge; over-commercialization/unsustainable tourism.
- **State obligations**: adopt codes of ethics; protect community rights via intellectual property, privacy, and other legal protections; promote public recognition of bearers/practitioners; support media campaigns, symposiums, festivals, case studies, community associations; disseminate Article 18 selected programmes.

### Operational Directives: Education, Institutions, Media, and Commerce

**Education (Art. 14(a))**: States shall ensure recognition, respect, enhancement of ICH. Measures:
- multilingual education with vernacular languages
- ICH in curricula adapted to local specificities
- teacher capacity-building
- involve parents, practitioners/bearers, youth
- non-formal transmission via games, apprenticeships
- ICT use
- university teaching, interdisciplinary research
- vocational guidance
- small business management training

**Community centres**: Self-managed; non-formal safeguarding, intergenerational transmission.

**Museums/archives**: Involve practitioners/bearers; present as living heritage in constant evolution; focus on knowledge recreation, not objects.

**Media**: Social cohesion, sustainable development, conflict prevention; local broadcasting for local languages; interactive games for youth.

**Commercial activities**: Communities as primary beneficiaries; avoid misappropriation; sustainable tourism; must not distort ICH meaning for community.

### International level and Emblem provisions

- Committee annually updates and publishes the Lists and the Register, encouraging dissemination via schools, community centres, universities, and media, focusing on elements' context and value for communities.
- Secretariat functions as a clearing house, facilitates information exchange, produces training material, and coordinates awareness efforts.
- The Convention's emblem must accompany UNESCO's emblem; each is governed by separate rules.
- Statutory organs (General Assembly and Committee) and the Secretariat may use the emblem without prior authorization.
- Authorizing emblem use is the prerogative of statutory organs, who may delegate to the Director-General; authorization requires relevance to the Convention's objectives and compliance with its principles.

### Basic Texts

- Emblem use for patronage: performances, publications, congresses, works embodying intangible cultural heritage.
- Criteria: Impact (safeguarding, visibility), Reliability (experience, guarantees).
- Conditions: request ≥3 months prior; written authorization by Director-General; obligatory State Party consultation for national activities; regular activities need fixed duration and periodic renewal.
- Commercial use (sale chiefly for profit): Director-General authorization under specific contract; if linked to inscribed element, State(s) Party(ies) consulted; Fund receives fair share of revenues.
- Protection via Article 6ter of the Paris Convention (1883, Stockholm 1967). Director-General institutes proceedings for unauthorized international use; national authorities for national level.

### Reports by States Parties on Implementation of the Convention

- States Parties submit periodic reports every **6 years by 15 December**, on a region-by-region rotation (Form ICH-10, online at ich.unesco.org).
- Reports cover: inventories (Arts. 11–12); safeguarding measures (Arts. 11, 13); institutional capacities (Art. 13); educational/awareness programmes (Art. 14); international cooperation (Art. 19).
- For elements on the **Representative List**: report on social/cultural functions, viability and risks, contribution to List goals, promotion efforts, community participation.
- **Urgent Safeguarding List** reports: by **15 December of the 4th year** after inscription, every 4th year thereafter (Form ICH-11); cover viability, risks, safeguarding plan implementation and update, community participation.
- Special attention to **role of gender**; widest possible participation of communities, groups, individuals, NGOs.
- States non-party with Masterpieces: reports by **15 December 2014**, every 6th year (Form ICH-10).
- Secretariat transmits overview to Committee **4 weeks** prior to session; reports published online.

### Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage and Sustainable Development at the National Level

States Parties shall integrate safeguarding of intangible cultural heritage into development plans at all levels, balancing economic, social, and environmental dimensions. Safeguarding applies solely to heritage compatible with international human rights instruments and sustainable development. States must ensure communities, groups, and individuals are primary beneficiaries, participating actively without their heritage being decontextualized or denaturalized. Impact assessments must consider effects on this heritage. Legal measures, including intellectual property and privacy rights, must protect community rights. Safeguarding plans must be inclusive of indigenous peoples, migrants, refugees, and vulnerable groups. List inscriptions must not be misused for short-term economic gain. For food security, States shall recognize customary rights to land, sea, and forest ecosystems and ensure equitable benefit sharing. For health care, States shall promote access to healing knowledge and raw materials while respecting customary practices.

<!-- quelle: CHAPTER VI SAFEGUARDING INTANGIBLE CULTURAL HERITAGE AND SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT AT THE NATIONAL LEVEL … VI.1.2 Health care -->

### Basic Texts: Sustainable Development and Intangible Cultural Heritage

**Quality education (para 180):** States Parties shall ensure ICH recognition in educational systems. Measures:
- Integrate ICH into educational programmes at curricular, cross-curricular and extra-curricular levels
- Recognize community-recognized transmission modes as part of ICH; harness their potential in formal and non-formal education
- Promote education for biodiversity conservation and protection of natural spaces and places of memory

**Gender equality (para 181):** Ensure gender equality in planning, management and implementation of safeguarding measures at all levels and in all contexts.

**Water (para 182):** Ensure viability of water management systems recognized as ICH that promote equitable access to safe drinking water and sustainable water use.

**Income generation (para 185):** Communities, groups and individuals must be primary beneficiaries of income from their ICH and not be dispossessed of it.

<!-- quelle: VI.1.3 Quality education … VI.2.1 Income generation and sustainable livelihoods -->

### Intangible Cultural Heritage and Sustainable Development

- **Productive employment/decent work**: States Parties shall ensure communities/groups/individuals are primary beneficiaries of work involving their own ICH and are not dispossessed of it; extend social security protection to practitioners.
- **Tourism**: States Parties shall ensure communities are primary beneficiaries and lead managers of tourism associated with their ICH; viability, social functions and cultural meanings must not be diminished.
- **Environmental sustainability**: Recognize communities as bearers of knowledge concerning nature and the universe; conserve natural spaces necessary for expressing ICH; mitigate environmental impacts of ICH practices.
- **Disaster resilience**: Integrate bearers of traditional geoscience/climate knowledge into disaster risk reduction, recovery, and climate adaptation systems.
- **Peace**: Promote ICH practices with peace-making and peace-building at their core; foster social cohesion across differences of gender, ethnicity, origin, class; ensure inclusion of indigenous peoples, migrants, refugees, persons with disabilities and marginalized groups.

<!-- quelle: VI.2.2 Productive employment and decent work … VI.4.1 Social cohesion and equity -->

### Basic Texts: Operational Directives and Rules of Procedure

### Operational Directives (VI.4.2–4.4)
States Parties should foster studies (including by communities) on ICH's role in conflict prevention, peace restoration, and lasting peace. They should promote measures to:
- support ICH expressions/practices
- integrate them into public programmes/policies
- reduce vulnerability during/after conflicts
- use them as complements to other dispute mechanisms
For lasting peace, ensure respect for the ICH of indigenous peoples, migrants, immigrants, refugees, different ages/genders, persons with disabilities, and vulnerable groups; ensure widest participation; promote intercultural dialogue.

### Rules of Procedure of the General Assembly
- Sovereign body; meets biennially. Extraordinary sessions requested by the Committee or at least one-third of States Parties.
- Online sessions only in emergencies; secret ballot elections must be in praesentia.
- Bureau: Chairperson, up to five Vice-Chairpersons, Rapporteur; no immediate re-election after two consecutive terms.
- Agenda circulated at least 60 days (ordinary) or preferably 15 days (extraordinary) before sessions.

### Rules of Procedure of the Assembly

- Chairperson: directs proceedings, no vote (may instruct delegation); absent powers to Vice-Chairperson.
- Subsidiary bodies: Assembly defines composition/terms; elect own Chair; equitable geographical representation.
- Draft resolutions: by States Parties, circulated in working languages reasonably in advance.
- Procedural motions precedence: (a) suspend meeting, (b) adjourn meeting, (c) adjourn debate, (d) closure of debate.
- Working languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish.
- Documents distributed 30 days before ordinary session.
- Summary records in English and French.
- Voting: simple majority of "States Parties present and voting". Show of hands; roll-call if in doubt or requested by >=2 States Parties.
- Amendments voted furthest removed first.
- Committee: 24 Members, UNESCO electoral groups (Group V split into African and Arab), proportional, min 3 seats per group.

### Rules of Procedure: General Assembly and Committee

**General Assembly Rules**
- **Candidatures (R.36):** Secretariat asks States Parties 3 months prior; candidatures due 6 weeks before Assembly. Provisional list sent 4 weeks prior; no payments after 1 week prior. List finalized 3 working days prior.
- **Election (R.37):** Secret ballot unless candidates ≤ seats. Separate ballots per electoral group in unmarked envelopes. Abstention if no ballot paper; invalid if over-voted or blank. Greatest votes elected; ties by second ballot, then by lot.
- **Secretariat (R.38):** Director-General/representative participates without vote; appoints Assembly Secretary.
- **Suspension (R.40):** 2/3 majority of present/voting States Parties, except for Convention provisions.

**Committee Rules** (adopted Algiers 2006; amended through Jeju 2017)
- **Convocation (R.3):** Ordinary: ≥60 days notice; extraordinary: ≥30 days if possible.
- **Delegations (R.5):** One delegate plus advisers. Fund covers developing States Members' experts (requests ≥8 weeks prior, prioritized by increasing GNP per capita, max 1 per State).
- **Advisory (R.6):** Accredited NGOs attend in advisory capacity.

<!-- quelle: Rule 36 Procedures for the presentation of candidatures to the Committee … Rule 6 Organizations acting in an advisory capacity to the Committee -->

### Rules of Procedure of the Committee

- Committee may invite competent public/private bodies or persons for consultation.
- Observers include States Parties, non-party UN/UNESCO members, and competent intergovernmental/public/private bodies/persons upon request. Public meetings open to the public.
- Agenda prepared by Director-General; extraordinary sessions limited to convened questions.
- Bureau (Chairperson, Vice-Chairpersons, Rapporteur) ensures equitable geographical representation; meets between sessions at UNESCO Headquarters. Members eligible for immediate re-election for a second term.
- Chairperson directs discussions, does not vote but may instruct delegation; abstains from functions for elements in their own State Party.
- Quorum requires a majority of States Members.
- Private meeting documents made public after 20 years.
- Ad hoc consultative bodies and subsidiary bodies (only Committee Members) require equitable regional representation.

<!-- quelle: Rule 7 Invitations for consultation … Rule 21 Subsidiary bodies -->

### Rules of Procedure: Speeches, Voting and Secretariat

- State Party representatives may not advocate inclusion of their own nominations (Articles 16, 17 lists) or endorse their own assistance requests; only provide information (Rule 22.4).
- Each State Member has one vote (Rule 35).
- Voting normally by show of hands; roll-call if requested by 2+ members or if doubt; secret ballot if requested by 2+ members or for elections (unless uncontested) (Rule 39).
- UNESCO Secretariat assists; Director-General participates without vote (Rule 40).
- Other procedural rules (speech limits, proposal division, amendment precedence, points of order, adjournment/closure of debate) govern meeting conduct.

### Basic Texts

### Committee Rules of Procedure (selected)
- **Working languages:** English and French; interpretation between them; speakers in other languages must arrange interpretation into a working language; documents issued simultaneously in both.
- **Document distribution:** Agenda documents distributed to Members in both working languages at least four weeks before each session; provided electronically to accredited organizations, invited bodies/persons, and non-member States Parties.
- **Summary record:** Prepared by Secretariat, approved at opening of next session; list of decisions and final summary record transmitted by Director-General to Members, all States Parties, accredited organizations, and invited bodies/persons.
- **Reports:** Committee submits report on activities and decisions to General Assembly of States Parties at each ordinary session and brings it to attention of General Conference of UNESCO (Article 30.2); Chairperson may be authorized to submit on Committee's behalf; copies sent to all States Parties.
- **Amendment/Suspension:** Committee may amend or suspend Rules of Procedure (except provisions reproducing the Convention) by two-thirds majority of Members present and voting; proposed amendment must be on agenda per Rules 9 and 10.

### Financial Regulations of the Special Account for the Fund for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage
- Adopted at first extraordinary session (Chengdu, China, 23–27 May 2007); revised by General Assembly eighth session (UNESCO HQ, Paris, 8–10 September 2020).
- **Creation:** Special Account created under Article 25 of the Convention and Article 6(5)–(6) of UNESCO Financial Regulations.
- **Purpose:** Receive contributions and make payments to assist in safeguarding intangible cultural heritage per Convention and these Regulations.
- **Governance:**
  - General Assembly of States Parties has authority over appropriation of resources.
  - Committee prepares and submits to General Assembly for approval a draft plan for use of Fund resources (Article 7 of Convention).
  - Director-General manages and administers funds per Convention, Operational Directives, decisions of General Assembly and Committee, and these Regulations; submits biennial narrative and financial reports.
- **Income (Article 25.3 of Convention):**
  - (a) contributions by States Parties;
  - (b) UNESCO General Conference appropriations;
  - (c) contributions, gifts, bequests from other States, UN system organizations/programmes (especially UNDP), other international organizations, public/private bodies or individuals;
  - (d) interest on Special Account resources;
  - (e) funds from collections and events for benefit of Special Account;
  - (f) any other resources authorized by Committee.
  - States Parties not making declaration under Article 26.2 contribute per uniform percentage determined by General Assembly (Article 26.1).

- **General provisions:** Amendments approved by General Assembly; Executive Board informed; unless otherwise provided, administered per UNESCO Financial Regulations.

### Ethical Principles and Operational Modalities for Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage

**Ethical Principles for Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage** (adopted by the Intergovernmental Committee, 10th session, Windhoek, 2015) — twelve principles:

- (1) Communities, groups and individuals have the primary role in safeguarding their own ICH.
- (2) Their right to continue practices necessary for ICH viability should be recognized and respected.
- (3) Mutual respect and appreciation of ICH should prevail in interactions.
- (4) Interactions require transparent collaboration and free, prior, sustained and informed consent.
- (5) Access to instruments, objects, spaces and places of memory should be ensured, including in armed conflict; customary access practices should be respected even if limiting public access.
- (6) Communities assess their own ICH value; no external judgements of value or worth.
- (7) Communities should benefit from moral and material interests resulting from ICH use, research, documentation, promotion or adaptation.
- (8) Dynamic and living nature of ICH should be respected; authenticity and exclusivity should not be concerns or obstacles.
- (9) Direct and indirect, short-term and long-term impact of any action affecting ICH viability should be assessed.
- (10) Communities determine threats — including decontextualization, commodification and misrepresentation — and how to prevent and mitigate them.
- (11) Cultural diversity and identities fully respected; specific attention to gender equality, youth involvement and ethnic identities in safeguarding measures.
- (12) Safeguarding is of general interest to humanity through bilateral, subregional, regional and international cooperation; communities should never be alienated from their own ICH.

**Operational Principles and Modalities for Safeguarding ICH in Emergencies** (adopted by General Assembly of States Parties, 8th session, Paris, 2020; endorsed by Intergovernmental Committee, 14th session, Bogotá, 2019). Guidance for States Parties and stakeholders on safeguarding ICH in armed conflicts and disasters caused by natural and human-induced hazards. ICH has a dual role: directly threatened by emergencies, and helping communities prepare for, respond to and recover from emergencies. Aligned with UNESCO Strategy for protection of culture in armed conflict, its Addendum for natural/human-induced hazards, and UN Security Council Resolution 2347 (2017).

### Safeguarding intangible cultural heritage in emergencies

Intangible cultural heritage (ICH) exists only through enactment by practising communities; its safeguarding is indivisible from bearers' lives and well-being. Communities—affected populations, displaced persons, host communities—play a primary role in identifying affected ICH and needed measures, and in drawing on it for resilience, recovery, and reconciliation. States Parties must ensure safeguarding in all contexts, including emergencies, with widest community participation (refugees, IDPs, migrants). Emergency management stakeholders (relief specialists, humanitarian actors, NGOs, armed forces) support safeguarding and community use of ICH. ICH is dynamic and adaptive; safeguarding must respect this.

Modalities span three phases:

**Preparedness**
- Raise awareness/build capacities on ICH's dual nature in emergencies
- Support community risk-reduction capacity, especially in emergency-prone regions
- Integrate into ICH inventories: vulnerability of elements, mitigation capacity, locations/communities
- Include emergency preparedness in safeguarding plans (preventive measures, mitigation engagement, evaluation methodology)
- Incorporate ICH into local-to-regional risk reduction
- Establish links between ICH bodies and emergency preparedness bodies

**Response**
- Identify/locate/reach affected communities early
- Prioritize community-based needs identification: communities address immediate safeguarding needs and use ICH to mitigate effects
- Share information among States Parties and stakeholders to assess disruption and ensure relief accounts for ICH
- Incorporate ICH into post-disaster/post-conflict needs assessments; involve communities

**Recovery**
- Carry out community-based needs identification if not done earlier
- Resource/support community safeguarding measures/plans; sustain through recovery into preparedness and transition from humanitarian to development
- Engage ICH to foster dialogue, mutual understanding, reconciliation between/within communities

Funding: UNESCO Heritage Emergency Fund, Intangible Cultural Heritage Fund (emergency International Assistance). Listing mechanisms (Representative List, List in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, Register of Good Safeguarding Practices) promote visibility of elements contributing to emergency response; accelerated procedure available for nominations to List in Need of Urgent Safeguarding (criterion U.6).

### Overall Results Framework for the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage

Approved by the General Assembly of the States Parties at its 7th session (Paris, 4–6 June 2018), the framework originated from a 2013 UNESCO Internal Oversight Service recommendation. It was developed through a category VI expert meeting (Beijing, 7–9 Sept 2016), endorsed by the Intergovernmental Committee at its 11th session (2016), drafted by an open-ended working group (Chengdu, 11–13 June 2017), and endorsed by the Committee at its 12th session (Dec 2017).

The framework measures the impact of the 2003 Convention through objectives, indicators, and benchmarks. It comprises two tables: Table 1 contains the high-level framework (impacts, expected outcomes, 8 thematic areas, 26 core indicators); Table 2 presents the 26 core indicators and 86 assessment factors arranged by thematic areas. Guidance notes for all 26 indicators were developed and integrated into the online periodic reporting form ICH-10.

The periodic reporting system was reformed to enhance its quality and pertinence. The periodic reporting form ICH-10 was revised and aligned with the framework, allowing States Parties to establish targets and baselines at the country level.

**Impacts:** Intangible cultural heritage is safeguarded by communities, groups and individuals who exercise active and ongoing stewardship over it, thereby contributing to sustainable development for human well-being, dignity and creativity in peaceful and inclusive societies.

**Long-term Outcomes:**
- Continued practice and transmission of intangible cultural heritage ensured.
- Diversity of intangible cultural heritage respected.
- Recognition and awareness of the importance of intangible cultural heritage and its safeguarding ensured.
- Engagement and international cooperation for safeguarding enhanced among all stakeholders at all levels.

**Mid-term Outcomes:**
- Effective relationships built among a diversity of communities, groups and individuals and other stakeholders for safeguarding intangible cultural heritage.
- Dynamic development and implementation of safeguarding measures or plans for specific elements of intangible cultural heritage led by a diversity of communities, groups and individuals.

**Short-term Outcomes:**
- Improved capacities to support the safeguarding of intangible cultural heritage in general.
- Improved capacities to implement safeguarding measures or plans for specific elements of intangible cultural heritage.

**8 Thematic Areas and 26 Core Indicators:**
- *Institutional and human capacities:* 1. Competent bodies support practice and transmission. 2. Programmes support strengthening human capacities for safeguarding. 3. Training is operated by or addressed to communities and those working in the fields of culture and heritage.
- *Transmission and education:* 4. Education, both formal and non-formal, strengthens transmission and promotes respect. 5. ICH integrated into primary and secondary education. 6. Post-secondary education supports safeguarding and study of ICH.
- *Inventorying and research:* 7. Inventories reflect the diversity of ICH and contribute to its safeguarding. 8. Inventorying process is inclusive, respects diversity, and supports safeguarding by communities and groups. 9. Research and documentation contribute to safeguarding. 10. Research findings are accessible and utilized.
- *Policies as well as legal and administrative measures:* 11. Cultural policies and legal and administrative measures reflect diversity of ICH and are implemented. 12. Education policies and legal and administrative measures reflect diversity of ICH and are implemented. 13. Policies and legal and administrative measures in fields other than culture and education reflect diversity of ICH and are implemented. 14. Policies and legal and administrative measures respect customary rights, practices and expressions.
- *Role of intangible cultural heritage and its safeguarding in society:* 15. Importance of ICH in society widely recognized. 16. Inclusive plans and programmes recognize the importance of safeguarding ICH and foster self-respect and mutual respect.
- *Awareness raising:* 17. Communities, groups and individuals participate widely in awareness raising. 18. Media are involved in awareness raising. 19. Public information measures raise awareness. 20. Ethical principles respected when raising awareness.
- *Engagement of communities, groups and individuals as well as other stakeholders:* 21. Engagement for safeguarding ICH enhanced among stakeholders. 22. Civil society contributes to monitoring safeguarding.
- *International engagement:* 23. Committee involves NGOs, public and private bodies, private persons (monitored at global level only). 24. States Parties cooperate for safeguarding. 25. States Parties engage in international networking and institutional cooperation. 26. ICH Fund supports safeguarding and international engagement (monitored at global level only).

## GLOSSAR

- **intangible cultural heritage** — Practices, representations, expressions, knowledge, skills and associated cultural spaces that communities recognize as their heritage, transmitted across generations and constantly recreated, provided they are compatible with human rights, mutual respect, and sustainable development.
- **Register of Good Safeguarding Practices** — A listing mechanism under the Convention for documenting effective safeguarding practices, whose selection criteria were simplified in 2024 to address underutilization.
- **Safeguarding** — Measures aimed at ensuring the viability of intangible cultural heritage, including identification, documentation, research, preservation, protection, promotion, transmission and revitalization.
- **Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** — A list intended to ensure better visibility and awareness of the significance of intangible cultural heritage and to encourage dialogue, reflecting cultural diversity.
- **List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding** — A list established by the Committee for heritage requiring urgent safeguarding measures, inscribed at the request of the concerned State Party.
- **Fund for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage** — The financial mechanism established under the Convention to support safeguarding activities, funded by compulsory and voluntary contributions.
- **Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity** — The title of items proclaimed before the Convention's entry into force, subsequently incorporated into the Representative List.
- **free, prior and informed consent** — A standard requiring that communities, groups, or individuals consent to awareness-raising actions about their intangible cultural heritage without coercion, with full information, and before actions proceed.
- **International Assistance** — Supplementary support provided to States Parties for safeguarding intangible cultural heritage, granted by the Committee based on specified criteria and available resources.
- **extended or reduced basis** — Procedure allowing an inscribed element to be widened to additional communities/States Parties or narrowed, requiring a new nomination demonstrating all inscription criteria and community consent.
- **Evaluation Body** — A consultative body of twelve members (six experts and six accredited NGOs) established under Article 8.3 that evaluates nominations and makes recommendations to the Committee.
- **enhanced follow-up** — Interim status assigned by the Committee to an element pending additional information before a final decision on removal.
- **Bureau of the Committee** — A subsidiary body that may invite accelerated USL nominations in cases of extreme urgency under Criterion U.6.
- **Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of the Humanity** — List showcasing the diversity of intangible cultural heritage.
- **best safeguarding practice** — Programmes, projects and activities that best reflect the principles and objectives of the Convention.
- **Intangible Cultural Heritage Repository** — Destination for elements removed from either List by the Committee at the end of the removal procedure.
- **Intangible Cultural Heritage Fund** — The fund that finances the participation of experts from developing States Members in Committee and Bureau meetings, allocated by increasing order of GNP per capita.
- **living heritage** — ICH presented as constantly evolving rather than fixed or museum-bound.
- **practitioners and bearers** — Individuals and groups who actively practise and carry intangible cultural heritage within their communities.
- **non-formal transmission** — Transfer of knowledge and skills embedded in ICH outside formal educational systems, e.g. through apprenticeships, home-tutoring, or community practice.
- **Register of programmes, projects and activities** — A register of initiatives that best reflect the principles and objectives of the Convention.
- **emblem for the purpose of patronage** — Use of the Convention's emblem for exceptional activities likely to have a real impact on safeguarding intangible cultural heritage and enhancing visibility.
- **commercial use** — The sale of goods or services bearing the emblem of the Convention chiefly for profit.
- **Article 6ter of the Paris Convention** — Provision allowing UNESCO to use Member States' domestic systems to prevent abusive use of the emblem.
- **periodic reporting cycle** — The six-year rotational cycle, organized region by region, during which States Parties submit implementation reports to the Committee.
- **safeguarding plan** — A plan submitted at the time of nomination for elements inscribed on the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, whose implementation and updates must be reported on every four years.
- **decontextualize or denaturalize** — To remove heritage from its original context or alter its inherent nature, negatively affecting its viability.
- **sustainable development** — Development balancing economic, social, and environmental dimensions, interdependent with peace and security.
- **places of memory** — Natural spaces and places whose existence is necessary for expressing intangible cultural heritage.
- **decent work** — Productive employment that extends social security protection and benefits to communities practicing and transmitting their ICH.
- **social cohesion** — Strengthening of the social fabric of communities in an inclusive way, overcoming discrimination and transcending differences.
- **in praesentia** — Meetings or votes conducted with physical presence, as opposed to online sessions.
- **States Parties present and voting** — States Parties casting an affirmative or negative vote; abstentions count as not voting.
- **sine die** — Adjournment without a specified future time.
- **subsidiary bodies** — Bodies established by the Committee, composed exclusively of States Members of the Committee, to assist in its work.
- **Intangible Heritage Committee** — The Intergovernmental Committee for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, composed of States Parties elected per Article 5 of the Convention.
- **Bureau** — The Committee's coordinating body, consisting of the Chairperson, Vice-Chairpersons, and a Rapporteur.
- **States Members present and voting** — States Members casting an affirmative or negative vote; abstentions are not voting.
- **point of order** — A procedural intervention by a State Member during discussion, immediately decided by the Chairperson.
- **Special Account** — The financial account created under UNESCO Financial Regulations to hold and disburse the Fund's resources.
