# Erasing Concepts from Diffusion Models

## PROFIL

- Written by Gandikota et al. (with affiliations at Northeastern University and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology), this scholarly article addresses researchers and practitioners working with large-scale text-to-image diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion, which can generate undesirable output including sexually explicit content and copyrighted artistic styles.
- Its subject is a fine-tuning method, Erased Stable Diffusion (ESD), and its central claim is that a visual concept can be permanently erased from a pre-trained diffusion model's weights given only a short text description, with no additional training data — making removal robust even when weights are openly distributed, unlike dataset filtering, post-generation filtering, or inference-time guidance.
- The paper first situates the method against prior approaches — dataset censorship (e.g., Stable Diffusion 2.0's filtered retraining), post-hoc filtering and inference guidance (e.g., the NSFW filter, Safe Latent Diffusion), image cloaking (Glaze-style perturbations), model editing, machine unlearning, and energy-based composition — then presents the technical background on latent diffusion and classifier-free guidance, the method itself (using negative guidance as a "teacher" during fine-tuning, with variants ESD-x for prompt-specific erasure such as a named artistic style and ESD-u for global erasure such as nudity), and experiments on artistic style removal (a user study with five modern artists reported to be imitated by Stable Diffusion), explicit content removal on the I2P benchmark, and object class erasure on Imagenette.
- Appendices cover visual motivation, implementation details, single-image erasure (e.g., Starry Night), erasure via paraphrased prompts, extended results, and user-study details; the authors note limitations, including a trade-off between complete erasure and interference with other concepts.
- For arts education, the paper is usable in disciplines and situations concerning generative AI and artistic authorship — particularly the imitation of living artists' styles by text-to-image models, copyright and intellectual-property questions, and the ethics of AI-generated imagery — and more broadly in courses on machine learning, computer vision, and responsible AI. Code, data, and weights are made available at erasing.baulab.info and on GitHub.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (p. 1–2)

This paper, "Erasing Concepts from Diffusion Models" by Gandikota et al., addresses concerns that large-scale text-to-image diffusion models (such as Stable Diffusion) can generate undesirable output, including sexually explicit content and copyrighted artistic styles. The authors propose a fine-tuning method, called Erased Stable Diffusion (ESD), that can permanently erase a visual concept from a pre-trained diffusion model's weights, given only a short text description of the concept and no additional training data. Unlike dataset filtering (which requires costly retraining), post-generation filtering, or inference-time guidance (which can be circumvented by anyone with access to the model weights), erasure modifies the model's parameters directly, making the removal robust even when weights are openly distributed. The method uses negative guidance as a "teacher" during fine-tuning.

### Context and Related Work (p. 2–3)

The paper situates itself among several prior approaches:

- **Dataset censorship**: e.g., Stable Diffusion 2.0 was retrained on filtered data (an experiment consuming 150,000 GPU-hours over the 5-billion-image LAION dataset), yet explicit content persists — on the 4,703-prompt Inappropriate Image Prompts (I2P) benchmark, SD 1.4 produces 796 images with exposed body parts while SD 2.0 still produces 417.
- **Post-hoc filtering and inference guidance**: e.g., the NSFW filter bundled with SD 1.4 (easily disabled since code and weights are public) and Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD), the state-of-the-art guidance-based approach.
- **Image cloaking**: adversarial perturbations artists can add to work before posting (e.g., Glaze-style methods), which protects future images but cannot remove an already-learned style.
- **Model editing**: lightweight edits of large generative models (e.g., GAN editing, text-inversion for adding concepts); this paper instead erases a concept from a single textual description.
- **Machine unlearning**: which assumes undesired knowledge corresponds to identifiable training data points; here, by contrast, the goal is to erase a high-level visual concept learned from a large, unknown subset of training data.
- **Energy-based composition**: the insight that "A and not B" can be expressed as a difference of scores/noise predictions; the novelty here is using score composition as a source of unsupervised training data rather than at inference time.

### Technical Background (p. 3–4)

Diffusion models learn to generate images by gradually denoising Gaussian noise over T timesteps, predicting noise ε_t at each step. Latent diffusion models (LDM) operate in a lower-dimensional latent space of a variational autoencoder (encoder E, decoder D), predicting noise ε_θ(z_t, c, t) conditioned on timestep t and text condition c. Classifier-free guidance steers the final score toward the conditioned score and away from the unconditioned score using a guidance scale α > 1.

### Method (p. 4–5)

The goal is to reduce the probability of generating images matching the concept c, scaled by a power factor η. Using Tweedie's formula and the reparametrization trick, each score is expressed as a denoising prediction, and the fine-tuning objective teaches the new model's conditional prediction to mimic negatively guided noise — i.e., guided away from the erased concept.

The training process exploits the model's own knowledge to synthesize training samples, requiring no data collection. Training uses two sets of parameters: one frozen (θ*) and one being trained (θ). Partially denoised images x_t are sampled conditioned on c using θ; the frozen model θ* predicts noise twice (conditioned on c and unconditioned); the two predictions are linearly combined to negate the noise associated with the concept, and the new model is tuned toward that objective.

**Parameter choice is crucial:**

- **ESD-x**: fine-tunes only cross-attention parameters, which serve as a "gateway to the prompt." Used when erasure should be specific to the prompt, e.g., erasing a named artistic style (such as Van Gogh's) with minimal interference with other styles.
- **ESD-u**: fine-tunes non-cross-attention (unconditional) parameters. Used when erasure should be global and independent of prompt text, e.g., removing nudity even when not mentioned in the prompt.
- The subscript η denotes the strength of negative guidance; ESD-x and ESD-u without a subscript mean η = 1.

All models are trained for 1000 gradient update steps, batch size 1, learning rate 1e-5, with the Adam optimizer, on Stable Diffusion v1.4 unless stated otherwise. Baselines are: pretrained SD, SLD (with the target concept substituted for its safety concepts), and SD-Neg-Prompt (using the artist's name as a negative prompt).

### Experiments (p. 5–8)

#### Artistic Style Removal (p. 5–6)

Five modern artists reported to be imitated by Stable Diffusion are studied: Kelly McKernan, Thomas Kinkade, Tyler Edlin, Kilian Eng, and the series "Ajin: Demi-Human." A user study (13 participants, ~170 responses each) compared generated images against real artwork. Participants saw five real artworks plus one additional image (real art from the same artist, from a similar artist, or synthetic) and rated confidence on a five-point Likert scale. Similar-artist pairings used were: (Kelly McKernan, Kirbi Fagan), (Thomas Kinkade, Nicky Boehme), (Ajin: Demi Human, Tokyo Ghoul), (Tyler Edlin, Feng Zhu), (Kilian Eng, Jean Giraud), with 12–25 works per similar artist. 1000 images were generated (40 prompts per artist, 4 seeds each).

Results: genuine artwork rated 3.85, similar artists 3.16, and unedited AI-generated art 3.21 — AI duplicates rated higher than similar genuine artwork, confirming the model captures artists' styles. All removal techniques reduced perceived style: ESD-x achieved 1.12, SLD 2.00, and SD-Neg-Prompt 2.22. ESD-x also showed the least interference with non-erased styles (interference rating 2.92 vs. 2.50 for SLD and 2.44 for SDNG).

#### Explicit Content Removal (p. 7)

Using ESD-u to erase "nudity," the method was evaluated on 4,703 I2P prompts with images classified by the Nudenet detector. SD v1.4's 796 nudity-flagged images were reduced to 134 by ESD. The method outperformed both SLD and SD V2.0 (which filters training data but still generates nudity). On COCO 30K prompts, ESD-u achieved FID-30k of 13.68 (better than SD's 14.50, SLD-Medium's 16.90, and SLD-Max's 18.76), with comparable CLIP scores, indicating preserved general image quality. ESD-u-3 (stronger erasure) had FID 17.27. The SLD FID numbers (16.90 and 18.76) are taken from the SLD paper; in the authors' own experiments, they find the FIDs to be 18.71 and 25.29 for Medium and Max respectively. A note indicates Nudenet has a higher false-positive rate for male genitalia; manual verification showed both ESD and SLD completely erase male genitalia while SD 2.0 erases it by 60%.

#### Object Removal (p. 7–8)

Ten ESD-u models were trained, each erasing one class from the Imagenette subset of ImageNet: cassette player, chain saw, church, gas pump, tench, garbage truck, English springer, golf ball, parachute, French horn. 500 images per class were generated and evaluated with a pretrained ResNet-50 classifier. Average accuracy on erased classes dropped from 78.2 (SD) to 12.6 (ESD-u), while accuracy on other classes fell from 78.2 to 63.2. Some classes erased cleanly (e.g., French horn: 99.6 → 0.4; golf ball: 97.4 → 5.8), while others were harder (church: 73.8 → 54.2), and some erasures caused noticeable interference (removing French horn dropped other-class accuracy to 49.4).

#### Effect of η on Interference (p. 8)

Testing three "nudity"-erased ESD-u models on 1000 images per Imagenette class: η = 10 erases 92% of nudity but reduces 1000-way classification accuracy by 34%; η = 3 erases 88% with 14% impact; η = 1 erases 83% with 7% impact. Lower η mitigates interference and improves image quality but weakens erasure; the appropriate η depends on the application.

#### Limitations (p. 8)

Erasing large concepts imposes a trade-off between complete erasure and interference with other concepts. The method sometimes erases only distinctive attributes (crosses from churches, ribs/texture from parachutes) while leaving the larger concept, and occasionally interferes with other artistic styles.

### Conclusion (p. 8–9)

The paper presents a fast, efficient method for eliminating specific concepts from text-to-image models by editing weights, requiring only the concept's name, no dataset manipulation or expensive retraining, and no post-inference filters — enabling safe distribution of parameters. Efficacy is demonstrated in three applications: explicit content removal (comparable to SLD), artistic style removal (validated by a human study), and object class erasure. Code, data, and weights are available at erasing.baulab.info and on GitHub.

### Appendices (p. 11–23)

- **Visual motivation (A)**: The residual between conditional and unconditional scores visually represents a concept's style/attributes; negating it removes the concept.
- **Implementation details (B)**: ESD-x with negative guidance 1 for 1000 iterations at learning rate 1e-5 for artists; ESD-u with the prompt "nudity" for NSFW; ESD-u per Imagenette class for objects. Baselines include SLD (Weak/Medium/Max) and SD (v1.4, v2.0, v2.1). Evaluation uses Nudenet, CLIP score, and FID (COCO-30k, clean-fid implementation).
- **Single image erasure (C)**: The method can erase individual artworks (e.g., Starry Night) with minimal effect on Van Gogh's style or other memorized artwork; erasing multiple images begins affecting other memorized artwork (measured via LPIPS).
- **Generic prompts (D)**: Erasing paraphrases of "Eiffel Tower" (e.g., "Parisian Iron Lady," "Famous Tower in Paris") reduced direct Eiffel Tower generations from 79 to 38 out of 100, showing the method responds to meaning, not specific words.
- **Extended results (E)**: LPIPS measurements show high distance for erased styles (e.g., Ajin: Demi Human 0.46, Rembrandt 0.47) and low distance for unrelated styles (~0.15–0.26). A combined Q16/NudeNet classifier shows ESD also reduces broader inappropriate categories (hate, harassment, violence, self-harm, sexual, shocking, illegal activity); ESD-u-10 outperforms SLD-Max in all categories.
- **User study details (F)**: 36 evaluations per artist across nine buckets (real art references, original SD, three removal methods, three interference conditions), 180 images total per participant, IRB-compliant interface.

## GLOSSAR

- **Erased Stable Diffusion (ESD)** — The paper's method of fine-tuning a pre-trained diffusion model's weights to erase a visual concept using only its text description and negative guidance as a teacher.
- **ESD-x** — The variant that fine-tunes only cross-attention parameters, so erasure is prompt-specific (suited to erasing named artistic styles).
- **ESD-u** — The variant that fine-tunes non-cross-attention (unconditional) parameters, so erasure is global and independent of prompt text (suited to removing nudity or object classes).
- **η (negative guidance strength)** — A power factor scaling the strength of erasure; higher values erase more strongly but increase interference with other concepts.
- **Negative guidance as a teacher** — Using the difference between conditioned and unconditioned noise predictions of a frozen model as the training target that steers the fine-tuned model away from the concept.
- **Cross-attention parameters** — U-Net parameters that act as a "gateway to the prompt," depending directly on prompt text.
- **I2P benchmark** — The Inappropriate Image Prompts dataset (4,703 prompts) used to evaluate NSFW content generation.
- **Nudenet** — The detector used to classify generated images into nudity classes for evaluation.
- **Imagenette** — A ten-class subset of ImageNet used for object-erasure experiments.
- **SD-Neg-Prompt** — A baseline inference technique that steers generation away from unwanted effects by using the undesired concept (e.g., artist name) as a negative prompt.
- **Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD)** — The state-of-the-art inference-time guidance baseline for suppressing undesired concepts.
- **Image cloaking** — Adding adversarial perturbations to artwork before posting so models cannot imitate it — a different problem from erasing an already-learned concept.
- **Machine unlearning** — Modifying a model to behave as if particular identifiable training data had been absent — distinct from erasing a concept learned from unknown data subsets.
