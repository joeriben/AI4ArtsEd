# Steering Language Models with Activation Engineering

## PROFIL

- The paper "Steering Language Models with Activation Engineering" is written by Alexander Matt Turner, Lisa Thiergart, Gavin Leech, David Udell, Juan J. Vazquez, Ulisse Mini, and Monte MacDiarmid, and is addressed to the machine-learning research community working on language-model control and interpretability.

- Its subject is activation engineering — the inference-time modification of a language model's internal activations to steer its outputs — and its central claim is that prompt engineering and finetuning fail to fully elicit a model's capabilities (an "elicitation overhang"), which the authors' technique, Activation Addition (ActAdd), addresses by computing a steering vector from the difference between activations on a pair of contrasting prompts and adding it, scaled, to the model's forward pass.

- The paper first situates ActAdd within the steering literature (organized by how the vector is obtained and what it intervenes on), then sets out the method itself — operating on decoder-only Transformers' residual streams, with parameters of injection coefficient, injection layer, and alignment position, and requiring no gradient descent — and then reports results: perplexity reduction on target topics, token probability shifts, layer sweeps, topic generality, SOTA detoxification on RealToxicityPrompts, SOTA negative-to-positive sentiment control on the Stanford IMDb set, and negligible impact on general knowledge (ConceptNet/LAMA).

- A discussion section covers composability of forward passes, limitations (hyperparameter supply, unexamined impact on reasoning, need for access to intermediate activations), comparisons with finetuning and prompt engineering, interpretability implications for the linear representation hypothesis, and alignment applications and misuse risks; appendices examine whether ActAdd reduces to prompt engineering, norm analysis, random-vector controls, partial-dimension additions, and replicability across GPT-2-XL, GPT-J-6B, LLaMA-1-13B, OPT-6.7B, and LLaMA-3-8B.

- The authors build on and position themselves against latent-space steering in image generation (Larsen et al. 2016; White 2016), word2vec arithmetic (Mikolov et al. 2013), Plug-and-Play Language Models (Dathathri et al. 2020), gradient-based latent steering (Subramani et al. 2022), knowledge editing (Hernandez et al. 2023; Meng et al. 2023), weight editing (Ilharco et al. 2023), and ITI (Li et al. 2023b), from which ActAdd differs in adding vectors to a subset of sequence positions and needing as few as 2 samples; representation engineering (Zou et al. 2023) and in-context vectors (Liu et al. 2023) followed the present work.

- The paper is usable in the disciplines of machine learning, natural-language processing, and AI interpretability and alignment, in situations of teaching or studying how language models can be controlled at inference time, how their internal representations can be analyzed causally, and how creative or stylistic model outputs (e.g. topic, sentiment, toxicity) can be steered without retraining.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (pp. 1–2)

The paper introduces **activation engineering**: the inference-time modification of a language model's internal activations to control ("steer") its outputs. The authors argue that prompt engineering and finetuning fail to fully elicit a model's capabilities, positing an "elicitation overhang" — abilities exist inside frontier models that no known intervention draws out. Their specific technique, **Activation Addition (ActAdd)**, computes a "steering vector" from the difference between the model's activations on a pair of contrasting prompts (e.g. 'Love' vs. 'Hate') and adds this vector, scaled, to the model's forward pass at inference time. They claim state-of-the-art (SOTA) results on sentiment control and detoxification while preserving general model capabilities, using models including GPT-2-XL, GPT-J-6B, LLaMA-1-13B, OPT-6.7B and LLaMA-3-8B. A motivating example: a model trained on eloquent poets and awkward mathematicians may internally contain both abilities, but no prompt could elicit "eloquent mathematical prose" since it never appeared in training data — activation engineering might activate both circuits simultaneously (pp. 1–2).

### Related Work and Positioning (pp. 2–3)

The authors situate ActAdd within the steering literature, organized in Table 2 by how the vector is obtained (fine-tuning differences, per-query gradient search, or differences between prompt pairs) and what it intervenes on (weights or activations). ActAdd belongs to the last category: directly computed differences between prompt pairs applied to activations. Related work discussed includes: latent-space steering in image generation (smile directions, Larsen et al. 2016; White 2016); word2vec arithmetic (Mikolov et al. 2013); Plug-and-Play Language Models (Dathathri et al. 2020); gradient-based latent steering (Subramani et al. 2022); knowledge editing (Hernandez et al. 2023; Meng et al. 2023); weight editing (Ilharco et al. 2023); and ITI (Li et al. 2023b), which steers toward truthfulness using trained linear probes. ActAdd differs from ITI in adding vectors to a subset of sequence positions and requiring as few as 2 samples, versus ITI's all-position additions and dozens of samples. Representation engineering (Zou et al. 2023) and in-context vectors (Liu et al. 2023) followed the present work (pp. 2–3).

### How ActAdd Works (pp. 4–5)

ActAdd operates on decoder-only Transformers' **residual streams** — the sequences of intermediate activation vectors processed by each layer. The method (Algorithm 1):

1. Take a steering prompt pair (p+, p−), where p+ represents the desired property (e.g. love) and p− its opposite (e.g. hate or indifference).
2. Pad both prompts to the same token length (right-padding with whitespace works well).
3. Run a forward pass on each prompt and record the activations at target layer l.
4. Compute the steering vector as the difference h+l − h−l.
5. Add c times this vector to the residual stream at layer l during the forward pass on the user prompt, at sequence alignment position a (fixed at a = 1, "front" addition, in all experiments).

Key parameters: injection coefficient c (intervention strength, typically magnitude under 15), injection layer l (middle layers are most effective), and alignment position a. The method requires no gradient descent or other optimization — only forward passes — enabling rapid iteration; the authors found most example contrast pairs "in minutes" (pp. 4–5, 23–24).

### Results (pp. 5–8)

**Perplexity on a target topic (GPT-2-XL, wedding vector from p+ = 'weddings', p− = ' ', l = 16, c = 1):** On 300k OpenWebText documents (half wedding-related, identified by keywords: wedding, weddings, wed, marry, married, marriage, bride, groom, honeymoon), ActAdd lowers perplexity on wedding-related sentences while preserving performance on unrelated text (perplexity ratio 0.875 related vs. 0.994 unrelated) (pp. 5–6, 23).

**Token probability shifts:** Across ~500k tokens, the distribution of mean log-probability changes is approximately normal with heavy positive tails; the most-increased tokens are primarily wedding-related (e.g. 'bride' +500% probability), while decreased tokens share no theme (pp. 5–6, 25).

**Layer sweep:** The wedding intervention is effective from the first layer, peaks around layer 6 with >90% topic-steering success (vs. ~2% baseline), then declines (pp. 6, 11).

**Topic generality:** GPT-3.5-scored relevance boosts of 5–20% across a range of topics at c = 2, with 'art' the exception (p. 6).

**Detoxification (RealToxicityPrompts, n = 1,000, scored by Perspective API):** ActAdd-OPT achieves toxicity 0.112 — 8% lower than the second-best PREADD-D-OPT (0.122) — with disfluency 13.8 and relevance 0.329. ActAdd-LLaMA-3 (8B) gives 0.108 toxicity vs. 0.114 unsteered, with a very small fluency penalty (6.7 vs. 6.3). ActAdd-OPT even outperforms an unsteered LLaMA-3. Baselines include FUDGE, PREADD-S/D, and Air-Decoding (p. 7).

**Sentiment control (Stanford IMDb, n = 1,000, classified by SiEBERT):** ActAdd achieves SOTA on negative-to-positive sentiment shift: 0.564 (OPT) and 0.669 (LLaMA-3) steering success vs. baselines of 0.445/0.417. In the positive-to-negative direction, ActAdd-OPT scores 0.432; the document states that the only method outperforming ActAdd in this direction incurs a large penalty to fluency (68.4 vs. 24.2, when matching methods on the same pretrained model) and relevance — though Table 4 shows both FUDGE (0.532) and PREADD-S-OPT (0.631) above ActAdd-OPT's 0.432. PREADD-S-OPT's full results are 0.631 steering, 68.4 disfluency, 0.253 relevance (positive-to-negative) and 0.624 steering, 67.1 disfluency, 0.258 relevance (negative-to-positive). Fluency is worse under all steering methods: 1.5x–3x worse for ActAdd, 7x for PREADD (pp. 7–8).

**Preservation of general knowledge (ConceptNet/LAMA, n = 29,774):** ActAdd has negligible impact on off-target factual answer probabilities (P@K) (p. 8).

### Discussion (pp. 8–10)

- **Composability:** Forward passes can be algebraically composed (h+, h−, h∗), which the authors find surprising and cite as evidence for compositional representations (pp. 8–9).
- **Limitations:** Hyperparameters (c, l) must be supplied, making ActAdd less user-friendly than prompting, though hyperparameters are stable across use cases; impact on reasoning is unexamined; API access is insufficient — the model must cache and expose intermediate activations (p. 8).
- **Vs. finetuning:** ActAdd needs no backward passes, minimal labelled data (just the prompt pair), and preserves interpretability since weights are unchanged; but finetuning is more flexible and better understood (pp. 9, 26).
- **Vs. prompt engineering:** Activation additions are continuously weighted (adjust c rather than editing tokens), don't consume context-window space, and may elicit capabilities prompting cannot (p. 9).
- **Interpretability:** ActAdd's success is presented as experimental evidence for the linear representation hypothesis — that networks represent features as directions in activation space — and steering vectors "establish causality" in the contexts examined (p. 9).
- **Alignment:** Activation engineering could enable general steering (e.g. a "Be helpful" vector analogue) and safety progress, though it could also be misused to circumvent superficial alignment (pp. 9, 17).

### Appendix Findings (pp. 17–32)

- **Is ActAdd just prompt engineering?** Two experiments argue no: (1) adding only the embedding component (0→20) of the Anger−Calm vector has little effect, showing layers 0–19 do substantial steering-relevant work; (2) prepending ' weddings' as an actual prompt token degrades unrelated-text perplexity (ratio 1.132) far more than ActAdd (0.994) (pp. 17–23).
- **Norm analysis:** At effective coefficients (c = +10 for Anger−Calm), the steering vector can be nearly ten times the norm of the underlying forward pass — around 90% of the post-layer-norm residual stream — yet the model remains coherent (pp. 27–28).
- **Random vectors:** GPT-2-XL is robust to random perturbations of similar magnitude; the anger vector shifts output distributions *less* than a random vector, indicating targeted effects (pp. 27, 29).
- **Partial ActAdd:** Adding only the first n of 1,600 residual-stream dimensions increases wedding-relatedness relatively smoothly, with a non-monotonic anomaly for the prompt 'I went up to my friend and said' (first 1,120 dimensions outperform all 1,600) — suggestive evidence of axis-alignment (pp. 30–31).
- **Replicability:** Qualitative results transfer to GPT-J-6B, Llama-1-13B (with a failed replication on the Eiffel Tower/Rome example and the anger and harm vectors), OPT-6.7B (17% toxicity drop, 21% absolute increase in positive classification), and Llama-3-8B (5% toxicity drop, 25% absolute increase in negative-to-positive classification) (pp. 27, 32).
- **Reproducibility:** Code at https://zenodo.org/records/13879423; sampling hyperparameters temperature = 1.0, freq_penalty = 1.0, top_p = 0.3 (precommitted; a blinded external reproduction suggested temperature = 0.6, top_p = 0.5, at which all qualitative results reproduced); seed 0 with validation on seeds 1 and 2; grid search over c ∈ [3, 20] and l ∈ [6, 24]; hardware Nvidia RTX A5000 (p. 11).

### Conclusion (p. 10)

ActAdd is a lightweight, optimization-free steering method achieving SOTA on toxicity reduction and sentiment shift while retaining overall capabilities, demonstrating the promise of activation engineering for eliciting models' full capabilities.

## GLOSSAR

- **Activation engineering** — The inference-time modification of activations in order to control (steer) model outputs.
- **Activation Addition (ActAdd)** — The introduced technique that contrasts intermediate activations on prompt pairs to compute a steering vector, tactically added during the forward pass.
- **Steering vector** — The activation-difference vector (h+l − h−l) computed from a contrast pair of prompts, acting like a "virtual bias term" when added to the forward pass.
- **Elicitation overhang** — The hypothesized gap between a model's full capabilities and what known interventions (like prompting) can elicit.
- **Injection coefficient (c)** — The hyperparameter rescaling the steering vector's contribution to the residual stream, representing intervention strength.
- **Intervention/injection layer (l)** — The Transformer layer at whose input the steering vector is added.
- **Sequence alignment (a)** — The sequence position at which the steering vector activations are aligned with the user-prompt activations (fixed at a = 1, "front" addition).
- **Residual stream** — The sequence of intermediate activation vectors processed by each Transformer layer, which ActAdd manipulates.
- **Contrast pair / steering prompt pair (p+, p−)** — Two otherwise-similar prompts differing along the target dimension (e.g. 'Love' vs. 'Hate'), used to compute the steering vector.
- **Perplexity ratio** — The relative predictive performance of ActAdd versus an unmodified model, used to measure targeted steering effects.
- **Ballistic vs. online steering** — A distinction (following Sloman 2002) between steering once at train time (finetuning) and steering repeatedly at inference time (ActAdd).
