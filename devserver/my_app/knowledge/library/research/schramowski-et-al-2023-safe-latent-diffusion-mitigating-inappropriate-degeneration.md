# Safe Latent Diffusion. Mitigating Inappropriate Degeneration in Diffusion Models

## PROFIL

The paper "Safe Latent Diffusion: Mitigating Inappropriate Degeneration in Diffusion Models" was written by Patrick Schramowski, Manuel Brack, Björn Deiseroth, and Kristian Kersting (DFKI, Hessian.AI, TU Darmstadt, Aleph Alpha, LAION) as a contribution to research on text-conditioned image generation. Its subject is the problem that diffusion models trained on large, unfiltered, internet-scraped datasets inherit degenerated and biased human behavior and may reinforce it; the authors demonstrate this empirically for Stable Diffusion, trained on subsets of LAION-5B, and claim to be the first to consider image editing from an ethical perspective to counteract such degeneration. The paper first reviews the risks of unfiltered training data, building on Birhane et al.'s critique of LAION-400M and on work showing that large models retain moral knowledge from pre-training, and presents an ethnic bias experiment showing that sexualized content associated with Asian women in western internet data transfers to the model (Japan generating over 75% nude images versus a global average of 35%). It then proposes Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD), a method that extends classifier-free guidance by pushing the unconditioned noise estimate away from an estimate conditioned on a textual description of an inappropriate concept, using a safety guidance term, a warm-up parameter, and a momentum term—requiring no additional training, no external classifier, and no adverse effect on image quality or text alignment, and set against fine-tuning approaches such as Text2LIVE, Imagic, and UniTune and against Prompt-to-Prompt's cross-attention editing. Four configurations of increasing strength (Hyp-Weak to Hyp-Max) are proposed, with a general safety concept inspired by DALL-E's content policy, and negative prompting is shown to achieve worse mitigation. The paper further introduces the Inappropriate Image Prompts (I2P) benchmark: 4703 real-world prompts from lexica.art covering seven categories, with inappropriateness defined following Gebru et al. Evaluation combines the Q16 classifier (trained on SMID) and NudeNet; SLD's Hyp-Max reduces inappropriate generation by over 75% (to 9% overall), reduces nude-image generation across all countries by 75%, and outperforms SD-v2's standalone dataset filtering, with combining both achieving the highest mitigation. The discussion advises against removing all inappropriate content from training data, acknowledges censorship risks, and notes that SLD attenuates but does not eliminate racial bias, so dataset curation remains required. Appendices cover ethnic bias details, the rejected RealToxicityPrompts benchmark, I2P keywords, the experimental protocol, ablations, and a datasheet (I2P released open-source under MIT license). The paper is usable in computer science, machine learning, and AI ethics, and in situations of arts education concerned with generative image tools, their biases, and safety mechanisms for different audiences.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (p. 1–2)

The paper "Safe Latent Diffusion: Mitigating Inappropriate Degeneration in Diffusion Models" by Patrick Schramowski, Manuel Brack, Björn Deiseroth, and Kristian Kersting (DFKI, Hessian.AI, TU Darmstadt, Aleph Alpha, LAION) addresses a problem in text-conditioned image generation: models trained on large, unfiltered, internet-scraped datasets inherit degenerated and biased human behavior and may even reinforce such biases. The authors demonstrate this problem empirically for the open-source model Stable Diffusion (SD), trained on subsets of LAION-5B, and propose two main contributions: (1) a new benchmark, **Inappropriate Image Prompts (I2P)**, for measuring inappropriate image generation, and (2) a mitigation method, **Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD)**, which suppresses inappropriate content during the diffusion process itself—requiring no additional training, no external classifier, and no adverse effect on image quality or text alignment. The authors claim their work is the first to consider image editing from an ethical perspective to counteract inappropriate degeneration of diffusion models.

### Risks and Promises of Unfiltered Data (p. 2–3)

The authors first review the risks of large-scale unfiltered training data: models such as GPT-3 may produce stereotypical, derogatory, gender- and racially biased content, reflecting biases in the data rather than counteracting them. They cite Birhane et al.'s critique of LAION-400M, a predecessor of LAION-5B. LAION-5B's creators annotated (but did not remove) potentially inappropriate content, advising against deployment without careful investigation of model behavior.

Stable Diffusion was trained on LAION-2B-en (over 2.32 billion English image-text pairs), then fine-tuned on "LAION High Resolution" and "LAION-Aesthetics v2 5+". Unlike other model providers (e.g., OpenAI's DALL-E with guardrails, or rigorously filtered models), SD released its model publicly without excluding annotated content.

On the "promises" side, the authors note that large models retain factual and moral knowledge from pre-training—citing work showing language models can self-debias toxic text and carry information about moral norms. SLD exploits this: it uses the model's own learned representations of "right" and "wrong" to guide generation away from inappropriateness, without fine-tuning (unlike Text2LIVE, Imagic, or UniTune) and enabling more complex changes than Prompt-to-Prompt's cross-attention editing.

**Ethnic bias experiment:** Searching LAION-2B-en for the 100 images closest (in CLIP space) to "japanese body" yielded almost exclusively inappropriate material, with 90% explicit nudity, versus a global average of 22% for 50 selected countries. Prompting SD with "<country> body" (100 images per country, evaluated with the NudeNet classifier for exposed genitalia) showed the bias transfers to the model: Japan generated over 75% nude images versus a global average of 35%; European, Asian, and Oceanic countries were more likely to be linked with nudity than African or American ones. The authors attribute this to the over-representation of sexualized content associated with Asian women in western internet data.

**SD's post-hoc safety measures:** SD includes an "NSFW" safety checker applied after generation, but the authors found that the platform lexica.art generated known-inappropriate content apparently without restrictions at the time of the study.

### Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD) — The Method (p. 3–4)

Diffusion models iteratively denoise a Gaussian variable to produce images, conditioned on a text prompt. SLD extends **classifier-free guidance**: instead of only pushing the unconditioned noise estimate toward the prompt-conditioned estimate, SLD simultaneously pushes it *away* from an estimate conditioned on a textual description of an inappropriate concept S. This yields three ε-predictions per step: unconditioned, prompt-conditioned, and safety-concept-conditioned.

Key components:
- A **safety guidance term γ** with a function μ that applies the safety guidance scale s_S element-wise, but only to those dimensions where the difference between prompt-conditioned and safety-conditioned estimates falls below a threshold λ; the scaling factor is clipped to avoid artifacts (predictions must stay within training bounds [-1, 1]).
- A **warm-up parameter δ**: safety guidance is not applied before step δ, preserving overall image composition; higher δ means less alteration.
- A **momentum term ν_t** that accelerates guidance for dimensions continuously guided in the same direction, with momentum scale s_m ∈ [0, 1] and β_m ∈ [0, 1); momentum builds up even during warm-up.

SLD is described as a balancing act: removing inappropriate content while keeping changes minimal. The safety guidance removes and suppresses inappropriate image parts as soon as they are constructed in the latent space.

### Configuring SLD (p. 5–6)

Because inappropriateness is subjective (a professional artist's needs differ from a 4-year-old child's), the authors propose four configurations of increasing strength.

**Unsafe concept:** inspired by DALL-E's content policy, the general safety concept is the text prompt: "hate, harassment, violence, suffering, humiliation, harm, suicide, sexual, nudity, bodily fluids, blood, obscene gestures, illegal activity, drug use, theft, vandalism, weapons, child abuse, brutality, cruelty". Concepts can easily be added or removed.

**Parameter recommendations:**
- Threshold λ ∈ [0.0, 0.03] (conservative small positive values).
- Safety guidance scale s_S ∈ [100, 3000] (no adverse effect at high values because μ is clipped).
- Warm-up δ ∈ [5, 20], never more than half the total diffusion steps.
- Momentum s_m ∈ [0, 0.5], β_m ∈ [0.3, 0.7].

**The four configuration sets:**

| Config | δ | s_S | λ | s_m | β_m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hyp-Weak | 15 | 200 | 0.0 | 0.0 | – |
| Hyp-Medium | 10 | 1000 | 0.01 | 0.3 | 0.4 |
| Hyp-Strong | 7 | 2000 | 0.025 | 0.5 | 0.7 |
| Hyp-Max | 0 | 5000 | 1.0 | 0.5 | 0.7 |

The weak configuration suffices for superficial blood splatters; stronger settings are needed for severe injuries. For nudity, the medium set may generate e.g. a bikini, while strong and maximum produce progressively more clothing like a dress. Hyp-Max can drive inappropriate generation to zero but diverges from the goal of minimal changes—suitable for sensitive applications (e.g., involving children), where post-hoc interventions like SD's safety checker are additionally recommended. Negative prompting (replacing the unconditioned estimate with the safety-conditioned one) achieves worse mitigation than SLD, lacks fine-grained control, and would consume a tool users need for other purposes.

### The I2P Benchmark (p. 6)

To measure inappropriate degeneration, the authors introduce **Inappropriate Image Prompts (I2P)**: 4703 unique real-world text prompts (after duplicate removal) collected from lexica.art (a collection of user-generated SD prompts from the official Discord server), covering seven categories: hate, harassment, violence, self-harm, sexual content, shocking images, illegal activity. Prompts were gathered via 26 keywords/phrases (up to 250 prompts per keyword, crawling the top 250 lexica results per keyword). Inappropriateness is defined following Gebru et al. as data that "if viewed directly, might be offensive, insulting, threatening, or might otherwise cause anxiety."

Dataset statistics: prompts average 20 tokens; only 1.5% of 4702 prompts are toxic per the PerspectiveAPI, and there is only a weak correlation between prompt toxicity and image inappropriateness—prompting with low-toxicity text still yields high probabilities of inappropriate images. This shows that suppressing "bad" words is not a reliable mitigation. Each prompt includes metadata: categories, a "hard" flag (at least half of generated images inappropriate), inappropriate/nudity/Q16/safety-checker percentages, toxicity score, lexica URL, and SD generation parameters (seed, guidance scale, dimensions).

### Experimental Evaluation (p. 6–8)

**Protocol:** 10 images generated per I2P prompt with SD v1.4 (Linear Multistep Scheduler, β_start = 8.5e-4, β_end = 0.012), evaluated by combining the Q16 classifier (trained on the SMID dataset; detects a wide range of inappropriate content but is conservative with a notable false-positive rate) and NudeNet (only exposed genitalia counted as inappropriate). An image is inappropriate if either classifier fires.

**Results (Table 1, SD v1.4):** Without safety measures, SD's probability of generating inappropriate content ranges from 34% to 52% by category (overall 0.39), despite only 1.5% of prompts being toxic. The expected maximum inappropriateness over 25 prompts is 96% overall for plain SD. SLD reduces inappropriate generation gradually with stronger settings; Hyp-Max reduces it by over 75%, to 9% overall (sexual: 0.06; hate: 0.09; shocking: 0.13). The authors assume most remaining Hyp-Max flags are classifier false positives (the Q16 false-positive rate roughly equals the Hyp-Max probabilities). Negative prompting achieves only 0.18 overall—worse than SLD's Hyp-Max.

**Bias mitigation:** SLD (strong setting) reduces nude-image generation across all countries by 75%; Japan drops from over 75% to 12.0%, close to the global 9.25%. However, a medium but statistically significant correlation remains (Spearman r = 0.52, p = 0.01), so SLD alone does not fully eliminate racial bias—dataset curation is still required.

**Stable Diffusion v2.0 comparison (Appendix E, p. 13–14):** SD-v2 applied rigorous dataset filtering of sexual/nudity content (but not to the text encoder). SLD on SD-v1.4 outperforms SD-v2's standalone filtering (overall: 0.09 vs. 0.40 for SD-v2 unmitigated; sexual: 0.06 vs. 0.29); combining filtering and SLD achieves the highest mitigation (SD-v2 + SLD: sexual 0.05, overall 0.10, body-ethnicity 0.06).

### Discussion, Limitations, and Image Quality (p. 8)

**Ethical implications:** The authors advise against removing all inappropriate content from training data, since the model's acquired knowledge of inappropriateness is what enables targeted suppression at inference. They acknowledge risks: similar techniques could be used for censorship, and reversing the guidance direction could generate mainly inappropriate content—hence they urge transparent disclosure of suppressed contents. Inappropriateness is based on social norms; the benchmark covers only specific concepts and the model's representation may reflect the dispositions of the social groups in the training data.

**Image fidelity and text alignment (Table 2):** COCO FID-30k rises slightly with stronger settings (SD: 14.43; Weak: 15.81; Medium: 16.90; Strong: 18.28; Max: 18.76); CLIP distance stays roughly constant (0.75–0.76). A user study on DrawBench (Amazon Mechanical Turk, three annotators per pair, 25 pairs per user, paid $0.70/$0.80 per batch) shows users slightly *prefer* SLD images over plain SD ones (around 59–64% judged better or equal in quality and text alignment), indicating safety does not sacrifice quality.

### Conclusion (p. 8)

The paper demonstrates that inappropriate degeneration transfers from unfiltered, imbalanced training datasets to text-to-image models; introduces I2P as a test bed; and presents SLD, which removes and suppresses inappropriate image parts during diffusion with no additional training and no adverse effect on image quality. Strong representation biases are attenuated but not completely removed, so the authors advocate careful use of unfiltered, clearly imbalanced datasets.

### Appendices (p. 11–26)

- **A. Ethnic bias details:** LAION-2B-en analysis confirms dataset bias (Japan over 90% explicit material among top-100 CLIP matches; four of the top-5 countries are Asian). Lexica generates biased content for "Japanese body". Roughly 8% of LAION-2B-en images were unavailable at download time.
- **B. RealToxicityPrompts:** initially tried as a benchmark, but its prompts produce unnatural images with random letter fragments; results on its toxic subset are reported (SD: 0.40 inappropriate probability; Hyp-Strong: 0.10).
- **C. I2P keywords:** the 26 collection keywords per category are listed (e.g., hate: 'hate'; harassment: 'mocking', 'bullying', 'threatening', 'harassment'; violence: 'humiliation', 'violence acts', 'suffering', 'violence'; self-harm: 'suicide', 'eating disorder', 'self-harm', 'attempts at harming oneself'; sexual: 'sexual acts', 'content meant to arouse sexual excitement', 'sexual', 'nudity'; shocking: 'profane subjects that may shock or disgust', 'bodily fluids', 'shocking', 'obscene gestures'; illegal activity: 'vandalism', 'illegal activity', 'drug use', 'theft').
- **D. Experimental protocol:** Q16 was trained on SMID (user scores 1–5 from 'immoral/blameworthy' to 'moral/praiseworthy'; below 2.5 classified inappropriate); SD's built-in NSFW checker was not used due to its high false-positive rate.
- **F. Expected maximum inappropriateness:** plotted for 10–200 generations per category; the metric amplifies Q16's false positives at large N, so only N = 25 is reported in the main text.
- **H. Implementation:** pseudocode for SLD is provided; the safety concept is user-adaptable in line with SD's transparency policy.
- **I. Ablations:** more warm-up steps lead to less compositional change; larger λ alters images more strongly; momentum scales above 0.5 may produce significant artifacts; stronger configurations diverge from the original image earlier in the diffusion process.
- **J. Datasheet:** full datasheet for I2P (funded by DFKI project 'SAINT', BMBF Grant No. 01IS22091, EU TAILOR GA No. 952215, LOEWE WhiteBox, HMWK clusters 'The Adaptive Mind' and 'The Third Wave of AI'); collected September–October 2022; released open-source on Huggingface under MIT license (text copyright not owned); the dataset should only be used to measure inappropriate degeneration and "should not be used to increase the inappropriateness of AI-generated images."

## GLOSSAR

- **Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD)** — The authors' inference-time method that extends classifier-free guidance to steer the diffusion process away from a textually defined inappropriate concept, suppressing inappropriate content without retraining or external classifiers.
- **Inappropriate Image Prompts (I2P)** — A benchmark of 4703 real-world text-to-image prompts (from lexica.art) covering seven inappropriate concepts, with inappropriateness scores and generation metadata, for evaluating text-to-image models' inappropriate degeneration.
- **Inappropriate degeneration** — The phenomenon that models trained on large-scale unfiltered, imbalanced data generate biased or inappropriate content, potentially reinforcing problematic social phenomena.
- **Safety guidance term (γ)** — The term added to the guided noise prediction that pushes generation away from the safety-concept-conditioned estimate, applied only after the warm-up period.
- **Safety threshold (λ)** — The hyperparameter determining the location of the hyperplane dividing the latent space into appropriate and inappropriate; recommended λ ∈ [0.0, 0.03].
- **Safety guidance scale (s_S)** — The element-wise scaling of the difference between prompt- and safety-conditioned estimates; recommended s_S ∈ [100, 3000], clipped to avoid artifacts.
- **Warm-up (δ)** — The number of initial diffusion steps during which safety guidance is not applied, preserving overall image composition; recommended δ ∈ [5, 20].
- **Momentum term (ν_t)** — A term accelerating safety guidance over time steps for dimensions continuously guided in the same direction, with momentum scale s_m and volatility parameter β_m.
- **Configuration sets (Hyp-Weak, Hyp-Medium, Hyp-Strong, Hyp-Max)** — Four predefined hyperparameter sets of gradually increasing aggressiveness of safety intervention.
- **Unsafe concept (S)** — The textual description of inappropriate content (e.g., "hate, harassment, violence, suffering, humiliation, harm, suicide, sexual, nudity, bodily fluids, blood, obscene gestures, illegal activity, drug use, theft, vandalism, weapons, child abuse, brutality, cruelty") used to condition the safety guidance.
- **Expected maximum inappropriateness** — A bootstrap estimate of the percentage of inappropriate images a user would encounter at least once over a batch of N prompts (N = 25 in the main text).
- **Hard prompt** — An I2P prompt labeled "hard" if at least half of its ten generated images are classified as inappropriate.
- **Negative prompting** — Replacing the unconditioned estimate with the safety-concept-conditioned estimate during classifier-free guidance; a baseline that mitigates less effectively than SLD and lacks fine-grained control.
