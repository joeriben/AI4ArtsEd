# Seoul Agenda: Goals for the Development of Arts Education

## PROFIL

The Seoul Agenda asserts that arts education is foundational for the constructive transformation of educational systems and the resolution of social and cultural challenges, positioning itself against educational frameworks struggling to meet the needs of learners in a rapidly changing world marked by technological advances and intractable injustices. Building on the UNESCO Road Map for Arts Education, the text argues that realizing this potential requires achieving high standards in the conception and delivery of programmes, ensuring accessibility, and applying arts education principles to global issues. For arts education, it serves as a concrete action plan for Member States and stakeholders to implement interdisciplinary, lifelong learning strategies that safeguard traditional knowledge and foster social cohesion across diverse community contexts.

## WISSEN

### Context and Development of the Seoul Agenda

The Seoul Agenda: Goals for the Development of Arts Education resulted from UNESCO's Second World Conference on Arts Education (Seoul, May 2010), involving 650+ experts from 95 countries. Its development began in July 2009 via the International Advisory Committee (IAC). The General Rapporteur subsequently revised the document during and after the Conference to integrate participant insights and proposals.

### Preamble: The Role and Purpose of Arts Education

Arts education can drive the constructive transformation of educational systems facing technological advance and intractable social injustice. The Seoul Agenda builds on the UNESCO Road Map (2006), organizing its action plan around three goals: renewing educational systems, achieving social and cultural objectives, and ensuring high standards in programme conception and delivery. It calls on Member States, civil society, and professional organizations to implement these strategies.

### Goal 1: Accessibility and Transformation of Educational Systems

Goal 1 asserts arts education as foundational to educational renewal. 1.a affirms balanced development—creative, cognitive, emotional, aesthetic, social—requiring access to:
- comprehensive arts studies at all schooling levels
- out-of-school experiences for diverse community learners
- interdisciplinary arts including digital/emerging forms
1.b transforms systems via arts as cross-disciplinary model and innovative pedagogies. 1.c establishes lifelong and intergenerational learning in, about and through arts education, safeguarding traditional knowledge. 1.d builds leadership for policy reform including marginalized groups, advocacy, public awareness.

### Quality Assurance in Arts Education Programmes

Quality assurance requires five coordinated strategies, framing quality as both conceptual and delivery-oriented and context-responsive rather than uniform.

**2.a — Standards responsive to local needs, infrastructure, cultural contexts:**
- (i) Establish high standards for delivery in school and community
- (ii) Institute formally recognized qualifications for teachers and community facilitators
- (iii) Provide necessary and appropriate facilities and resources

**2.b — Sustainable training for educators, artists, communities:**
- (i) Offer skills/knowledge for teachers (general and arts) and artists through sustainable professional learning mechanisms
- (ii) Integrate artistic principles and practices within pre-service teacher education and professional development of practising teachers
- (iii) Ensure implementation through quality monitoring procedures such as supervision and mentoring

**2.c — Exchange between research and practice:**
- (i) Support arts education theory and research globally; link theory, research and practice
- (ii) Encourage cooperation in developing research; distribute research and exemplary practices through international structures such as clearing houses and observatories
- (iii) Consolidate high quality evidence of impact; assure equitable distribution

**2.d — Collaboration between educators and artists:**
- (i) Encourage schools to initiate partnerships between artists and teachers in delivering curricula
- (ii) Encourage community organizations to cooperate with teachers across different learning environments
- (iii) Elaborate cultural projects involving parents, family members and community members

**2.e — Partnerships among stakeholders and sectors:**
- (i) Build partnerships within and beyond governments across educational, cultural, social, health, industrial and communication sectors
- (ii) Coordinate governments, civil society organizations, higher education institutions and professional associations
- (iii) Engage private sector entities including foundations and philanthropic agencies

### Goal 3: Arts Education for Social and Cultural Challenges

Arts education is positioned as instrumental to societal creative capacity, social cohesion, and global challenge response. Four strategies: (3.a) fostering creative citizens via schools, communities, and communication technology; (3.b) recognizing well-being dimensions—traditional/contemporary arts experiences, therapeutic and health dimensions, identity/heritage conservation, diversity/dialogue promotion, restorative dimensions in post-conflict/post-disaster contexts—plus training integration and dropout reduction; (3.c) learner-context adaptation for minorities/migrants, diverse expression knowledge, and intercultural dialogue skills in training; (3.d) addressing environment, migration, sustainability through multicultural practice, mobility for global citizenship, and democracy/peace support in post-conflict societies.

## GLOSSAR

- **Seoul Agenda: Goals for the Development of Arts Education** — The major outcome document of UNESCO's Second World Conference on Arts Education.
- **International Advisory Committee (IAC)** — The body responsible for initiating and refining the goals of the Seoul Agenda prior to the Conference.
- **Seoul Agenda** — A concrete action plan from UNESCO's Second World Conference on Arts Education, structuring three goals with strategies and action items.
- **Road Map** — The UNESCO theoretical and practical framework from the 2006 Lisbon conference, providing guidance for arts education development.
- **constructive transformation of educational systems** — The conviction that arts education can positively reshape education to meet learner needs amid rapid change.
- **in, about and through arts education** — Three modalities of arts learning: learning through artistic practice, learning about the arts as disciplines, and learning in arts as experiential engagement.
- **intergenerational learning** — Learning across age groups to safeguard knowledge of traditional arts and foster mutual understanding between generations.
- **sustainable professional learning mechanisms** — Ongoing, structurally embedded training systems that ensure educators and artists maintain and develop their skills over time rather than through one-off sessions.
- **clearing houses and observatories** — International structures for collecting, coordinating and distributing arts education research and exemplary practices across countries and institutions.
- **quality monitoring procedures** — Systematic oversight methods such as supervision and mentoring used to ensure that arts education training is actually implemented as intended.
