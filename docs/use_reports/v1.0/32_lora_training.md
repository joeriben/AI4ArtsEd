## 6. What the help text should tell a user

The Help panel's own text, which opens on arrival, is quoted in 1: it says what a LoRA adds to Stable Diffusion 3.5, that the output shifts towards your images once the trigger word stands in the prompt, that a captioning model describes every uploaded image before training, and what is learned about curating, responsibility and energy. It should add the handling: write the trigger word first and tags after it, and drop ten to fifty pictures; the descriptions are editable, and the trigger word is put in front of every description for training; "Start Training" first checks the card's free memory, and if other models hold the 50 GB it offers to unload them, which interrupts whatever else is running on the server; a run takes one to three hours, blocks picture generation meanwhile, and costs what the note estimates; intermediate adapters are saved every two epochs and the finished adapter appears in the LoRA Explorer. It should say where the result is read, in the Explorer's own words: "The third row against the second is the reach: what the training added on top of what the words already carried. The fourth row against the first is the limit: whether the adapter also answers when it is not called, which is how it shows that it has overwritten what the model knew rather than added to it." And it should say what is trained: the adapters attach to the denoiser and to the two CLIP text encoders, T5 stays frozen, and SD 3.5 Large is the only trainable model (the platform's own overview). The Help's last question is the instrument's: does the model learn style or content, and does it learn what was defined beforehand, or something else.

## 7. Use possibilities, action-oriented

- Try to put your own place into the model: twenty to forty pictures of your street, your school, your region (buildings, objects, landscapes, no people), a trigger word, a training, and the next day the Explorer with motifs from the series and from outside it; read the reach and the limit.
- Try to correct a default: find something the base model gets wrong about what you know (the storybook farmhouse, the person at work), gather pictures that show it as it is, train, and see whether thirty pictures and a card correct it or lay a varnish over the model's own.
- Decide the series together: what goes in, what stays out, one reason per exclusion; the Help names this as what there is to learn, aesthetic reasons under scarcity.
- Read the captions as what the machine saw of your pictures and correct where it saw something else; then see in the Explorer whether the correction was learned.
- Two adapters, one on a narrow series and one on a mixed one, or one on eight pictures and one on thirty (two sessions): compare in the Explorer what size and mixture did.
- Weigh the energy: the note's estimate against the reason for the training, decided by the group that trains.

## 8. Teaching scenarios

The instrument is the one place on the platform where a group changes a model instead of a prompt, with means a school has: thirty pictures, a word, one card for one to three hours. What it teaches is how far such means reach into a model trained at the cost of millions, and where they stop: the Explorer's third row against its second is the reach, its fourth against its first the limit, and a motif the series never showed asks whether a manner carried over or the subjects came along. The decisions are the group's: what the model is to learn, which pictures stand for it, what the caption says, whether the reason justifies the energy. The question underneath is cultural: whether a local form, a group's manner, a place as its people know it, can be made to hold in a model, and what of the model's own order survives the attempt.

**S1 — Our place in the model** (formal, art, geography or local history, grades 9–13, two sessions of 90 min and a training in between). The class collects thirty pictures of its own place, buildings, objects, landscapes, no people, and decides together which go in and which stay out, with a reason written for every exclusion; the trigger word is the place's name. The captions are read and corrected where the describer saw something else than the class knows to be there. The training runs between the sessions. In the second session the LoRA Explorer runs with motifs from the series ("a house", "a street in the morning") and one the series never showed; the class reads the third row against the second for what the training added, the fourth against the first for whether the adapter answers uncalled, and the outside motif for whether the place came along as subjects or as a manner. The closing question is whether the model took the place in, or put a varnish of it over its own houses. Sequence (session 1): 20 min selection and reasons; 15 min captions; 10 min start and the VRAM check; (session 2): 30 min Explorer; 40 min reading; 20 min plenary. Subject link: the local and the default; representation of place; dataset, caption, trigger word. Uncertainty: the training needs 50 GB free and one to three hours; the shared server did not have the memory.

**S2 — Correcting a default** (formal, art or ethics, grades 10–13, two sessions). The class first finds a default in the base model that it knows to be wrong or narrow (the describer called a generated farmhouse "storybook-style"; the Explorer's base rows for "a person at work" are another), then assembles twenty to forty pictures that show the thing as the class knows it, trains, and reads in the Explorer whether the correction holds with the trigger word, without it, and on motifs the series never showed. The finding is the limit: what thirty pictures can correct in a model and what the model keeps, and the class writes it down as such. Subject link: the default as a claim about the world; correction with one's own material; the limit of correctability. Uncertainty: not run; the outcome is not known to me.

**S3 — What the machine saw** (formal, language teaching or art, grades 9–12, 45 min, no training). Pupils upload a series of their own pictures (objects, not faces), wait for the descriptions, and correct each one by hand: what is wrong, what is missing, what the describer added; each description is checked against the picture it describes. Then they discuss that what is learned is decided by whoever writes the sentence, and that the sentence is theirs now. Sequence: 10 min upload; 20 min reading; 15 min corrections and discussion. Subject link: description, caption, dataset; the author of the training text.

**S4 — What it costs, and for what** (formal, ethics or physics, grades 10–13, 45 min). The class reads the note's estimate (0.6–1.8 kWh, 240–720 g CO₂, 4–12 tree-days) and the VRAM check's numbers, and the Help's sentence that the reason for training should be able to justify the energy; it writes a one-page sheet that puts the run's kWh beside the classroom's devices and beside a published figure for training a large model, and then decides, with reasons given, whether its own reason for a training carries the run. Subject link: energy, units, scale; a decision taken together. Uncertainty: the figures on the surface are stated as an estimate; the class treats them as such.

**S5 — A manner of our own** (non-formal, art workshop, age 14+, two days). The group agrees on a manner before drawing (a palette, a line, a way of framing) and makes twenty to forty drawings in it, together; the adapter is trained on them. Day 2 in the Explorer: motifs from the drawings and from outside; the group marks in the adapter's pictures what of its manner is recognisable, whether the adapter brings the group's subjects along, and whether it answers uncalled. Then three things side by side: their drawings, the adapter's pictures, and pictures made with the adapter by them; for each, who made which choice and who would sign. The round asks whether a manner made together can be taken into a model, what that does to the group that made it, and whether they would find it fair on a stranger's adapter trained on their drawings. Sequence: day 1: 60 min manner and drawing; 30 min selection and captions; training; day 2: 40 min Explorer; 40 min marking; 40 min the three side by side; 30 min round. Subject link: style as a collective decision; authorship; what a model takes from a series. Uncertainty: a reserved card for the training.
