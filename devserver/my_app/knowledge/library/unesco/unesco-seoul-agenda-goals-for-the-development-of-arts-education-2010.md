# Seoul Agenda: Goals for the Development of Arts Education

## PROFIL

- Author: UNESCO. The document is the key result of UNESCO's Second World Conference on Arts Education, held in Seoul, Republic of Korea, on 25–28 May 2010 and convened jointly with the Republic of Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism; the text was drafted by the conference's International Advisory Committee and adjusted in light of the conference debates. It is addressed to UNESCO Member States, civil society, professional organizations and communities, and is intended as a concrete action plan that extends the UNESCO Road Map for Arts Education, the main result of the First World Conference in Lisbon (2006), by folding its substance into three goals, each supported by practical strategies and specific action items.
- Its position is that arts education can positively reshape educational systems that are failing to keep pace with learners' needs in a fast-changing world, and can directly help address the social and cultural problems confronting the world, provided programmes are conceived and delivered to high standards.
- Goal 1 seeks to make arts education accessible as a fundamental and sustainable component of a high quality renewal of education, through strategies positioning it as the basis for balanced creative, cognitive, emotional, aesthetic and social development; using it to transform educational systems and structures; creating lifelong and intergenerational learning systems; and strengthening leadership, advocacy and policy development capacities.
- Goal 2 seeks to assure that arts education activities and programmes are of a high quality in conception and delivery, through strategies forging shared high standards responsive to local needs and cultural contexts; making sustainable training available to educators, artists and communities; promoting exchange between research and practice; enabling educators and artists to work together in schools and out-of-school programmes; and building partnerships among stakeholders and sectors.
- Goal 3 seeks to apply arts education principles and practices to resolving the social and cultural challenges facing today's world, through strategies strengthening society's creative and innovative capacity; acknowledging and cultivating the social and cultural well-being dimensions of arts education; reinforcing its contribution to social responsibility, social cohesion, cultural diversity and intercultural dialogue; and building the capacity to confront major global challenges, from peace to sustainability.
- The document is usable across all arts fields — including digital and other emerging art forms, and traditional arts — in school settings at all levels, in out-of-school and community settings, in lifelong and intergenerational learning, and in situations ranging from formal curricula to post-conflict and post-disaster reconstruction.

## WISSEN

### Background and Origin of the Seoul Agenda (p. 1)

The Seoul Agenda is the principal outcome of the second worldwide conference on arts education that UNESCO convened, held from 25 to 28 May 2010 in Seoul, Republic of Korea. UNESCO organized the event jointly with the Republic of Korea's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism; over 650 officials and specialists in arts education from 95 countries attended. The programme featured a round table of ministers, keynote addresses, panels, workshops run in parallel, discussions organized by region, a meeting with NGOs and foundations, and a special session on arts education's contribution to the rapprochement of cultures.

Drafting started a full year ahead of the event: the International Advisory Committee (IAC) met at UNESCO Headquarters in July 2009 and then continued polishing the goals by e-mail in the following months. Just before the conference, the committee produced a revised text incorporating comments and proposals from its members, and this version was distributed to the experts during the event. The General Rapporteur tracked the presentations and debates as the conference unfolded and adjusted the text to capture the priorities and insights participants voiced; after the closing presentation, further editorial changes were made in light of feedback received from participants.

### Preamble: Purpose and Organizing Principles (p. 2)

The Agenda rests on the shared belief of IAC members and conference experts that arts education can play a significant part in positively reshaping educational systems that are failing to keep pace with learners' needs in a fast-changing world — one marked by striking technological progress but also by stubborn social and cultural injustices. The IAC's concerns ranged over peace, cultural diversity, intercultural understanding, and the demand for a workforce that is creative and adaptable in post-industrial economies. Participants also concurred that arts education can directly help address the social and cultural problems confronting the world, and that meeting these challenges successfully requires programmes conceived and delivered to high standards. These three concerns form the document's organizing principles.

Emerging from the Seoul conference, the Agenda is meant to build on UNESCO's "Road Map for Arts Education", the main result of the First World Conference in Lisbon, Portugal (2006), which had offered a valuable theoretical and practical framework guiding the qualitative growth of the field. A key aim in Seoul was to take stock of the Road Map and push its implementation further. The Agenda therefore functions as a concrete action plan that folds the Road Map's substance into three broad goals, each supported by practical strategies and specific action items. It calls on UNESCO's member states, on civil society, on professional bodies and on communities to endorse its goals, adopt its strategies and carry out its action items, so that high-quality arts education can genuinely renew educational systems, deliver vital social and cultural outcomes, and ultimately serve children, young people and lifelong learners of every age.

### Goal 1: Ensure that arts education is accessible as a fundamental and sustainable component of a high quality renewal of education (p. 3–5)

**Strategy 1.a – Position arts education as the foundation for the well-rounded development — creative, cognitive, emotional, aesthetic and social — of children, youth and lifelong learners.** Action items:
- 1.a (i) Adopt policies and commit resources so that sustainable access is guaranteed to: comprehensive study of every arts field for students at all school levels, within a broad, holistic education; arts experiences outside school across all fields, for diverse learners within communities; and interdisciplinary arts experiences — including digital and other emerging art forms — both inside and outside school.
- 1.a (ii) Strengthen the interplay among the various dimensions of development (creative, cognitive, emotional, aesthetic and social).
- 1.a (iii) Set up rigorous evaluation systems so that learners' development in arts education is well-rounded.

**Strategy 1.b – Use arts education to help educational systems and structures transform in constructive ways.** Action items:
- 1.b (i) Use the arts as an educational model, bringing artistic and cultural dimensions into other academic disciplines.
- 1.b (ii) Use arts education to build a culture of creativity among teachers and school administrators.
- 1.b (iii) Use arts education to bring innovative pedagogies and creative approaches into curricula in ways that engage a diverse range of learners.

**Strategy 1.c – Set up frameworks for learning across the whole of life and between generations, in, about and through the arts.** Action items:
- 1.c (i) Guarantee that learners of every social background can access arts education throughout their lives, across many different community and institutional settings.
- 1.c (ii) Guarantee arts education experiences for the various age groups.
- 1.c (iii) Promote learning between generations, so that knowledge of traditional arts is preserved and understanding across generations is strengthened.

**Strategy 1.d – Strengthen leadership, advocacy and policy-development capacity in the field of arts education.** Action items:
- 1.d (i) Equip practitioners and researchers to reform arts education policy, ensuring that marginalized populations and under-privileged groups take part in policy-planning processes.
- 1.d (ii) Improve communications and advocacy by building stronger ties with the information media, developing a suitable language for communication, and using information technology and virtual networking systems to connect existing national and regional initiatives.
- 1.d (iii) Publicize what arts education achieves for individuals and for society, so that the public recognizes its value and both the public and private sectors are moved to support it.

### Goal 2: Assure that arts education activities and programmes are of a high quality in conception and delivery (p. 5–8)

**Strategy 2.a – Forge shared high standards for arts education that answer to the needs, infrastructure and cultural context of each locality.** Action items:
- 2.a (i) Set high standards for how arts education programmes are delivered in schools and the community.
- 2.a (ii) Create formally recognized qualifications for those who teach or facilitate arts education in schools and communities.
- 2.a (iii) Supply the facilities and resources that arts education requires and that are appropriate to it.

**Strategy 2.b – Make sustainable training in arts education available to educators, artists and communities.** Action items:
- 2.b (i) Provide teachers (both generalist and arts specialists) and artists working in education with the skills and knowledge they need, through durable professional learning mechanisms.
- 2.b (ii) Weave artistic principles and practices into pre-service teacher education and into the continuing professional development of practising teachers.
- 2.b (iii) See that arts education training is actually implemented, using monitoring mechanisms of good quality, for instance supervision and mentoring.

**Strategy 2.c – Stimulate a two-way exchange linking research and practice in the field.** Action items:
- 2.c (i) Back arts education theory and research worldwide, and connect theory, research and practice.
- 2.c (ii) Foster cooperation in arts education research, and share both research findings and exemplary practices internationally, via mechanisms like clearing houses and observatories.
- 2.c (iii) Pull together high-quality evidence of arts education's impact and make sure it is distributed equitably.

**Strategy 2.d – Bring educators and artists together, both within schools and in programmes outside school.** Action items:
- 2.d (i) Urge schools to form partnerships pairing artists with teachers to deliver curricula.
- 2.d (ii) Urge community organizations to work with teachers in offering arts education programmes across many different learning environments.
- 2.d (iii) Develop cultural projects in various learning environments that actively draw in parents, family members and community members.

**Strategy 2.e – Initiate partnerships in arts education that connect stakeholders and sectors.** Action items:
- 2.e (i) Construct partnerships inside and outside government that reinforce arts education's role in society, particularly spanning sectors such as education, culture, social affairs, health, industry and communications.
- 2.e (ii) Align the work of state institutions, civil society bodies, universities and professional associations so that arts education principles, policies and practices are strengthened.
- 2.e (iii) Bring private sector actors — foundations and philanthropic agencies among them — on board as partners in developing arts education programmes.

### Goal 3: Apply arts education principles and practices to contribute to resolving the social and cultural challenges facing today's world (p. 8–10)

**Strategy 3.a – Harness arts education to strengthen society's creative and innovative capacity.** Action items:
- 3.a (i) Use arts education across schools and communities to develop individuals' creativity and innovativeness, and to grow a new generation of creative citizens.
- 3.a (ii) Use arts education to advance creative and innovative practices that support society's holistic social, cultural and economic development.
- 3.a (iii) Draw on new developments in communication technology as a stimulus for critical and creative thinking.

**Strategy 3.b – Acknowledge and cultivate how arts education contributes to social and cultural well-being.** Action items:
- 3.b (i) Promote recognition of these dimensions, which include: the value of the full spectrum of traditional and contemporary arts experiences; the therapeutic and health benefits of arts education; its potential to build and preserve identity and heritage while encouraging diversity and dialogue among cultures; and its restorative power in post-conflict and post-disaster situations.
- 3.b (ii) Include social and cultural well-being in the training offered to arts education professionals.
- 3.b (iii) Use arts education as a motivating process that increases learner engagement and lowers dropout rates.

**Strategy 3.c – Reinforce and expand arts education's contribution to responsibility toward society, social cohesion, cultural diversity and dialogue between cultures.** Action items:
- 3.c (i) Put the learner's specific context first, and promote educational practices tailored to what is locally relevant for learners — minorities and migrants included.
- 3.c (ii) Deepen knowledge and understanding of the range of cultural and artistic expressions.
- 3.c (iii) Bring skills for dialogue between cultures, along with suitable pedagogy, equipment and teaching materials, into arts education training programmes.

**Strategy 3.d – Build the capacity, through arts education, to confront the great challenges facing the globe today, peace and sustainability among them.** Action items:
- 3.d (i) Direct arts education activities toward a broad set of contemporary social and cultural issues — the environment, global migration and sustainable development among them.
- 3.d (ii) Broaden the multi-cultural dimensions of arts education practice and step up the intercultural mobility of students and teachers, in order to nurture global citizenship.
- 3.d (iii) Use arts education to foster peace and democratic life in communities, and to assist reconstruction in societies emerging from conflict.

## GLOSSAR

- **Seoul Agenda** — UNESCO's concrete plan of action for arts education, integrating the Road Map within three broad goals with strategies and action items, adopted at the 2010 Seoul conference.
- **Road Map for Arts Education** — The theoretical and practical framework from the First World Conference (Lisbon, 2006) on which the Seoul Agenda builds.
- **IAC (International Advisory Committee)** — The body that drafted and refined the Seoul Agenda before and during the conference.
- **Strategies** — The named sub-goals under each of the three broad goals (e.g., 1.a, 2.b), each accompanied by specific action items.
- **Action items** — The concrete, numbered steps (e.g., 1.a (i)) through which each strategy is to be implemented.
- **Lifelong and intergenerational learning** — Frameworks guaranteeing access to the arts across all ages and social backgrounds, including transmission of traditional arts knowledge between generations, in, about and through the arts.
- **Arts as an educational model** — Using the arts to introduce artistic and cultural dimensions into other academic disciplines.
- **Social and cultural well-being** — The therapeutic, health-related, identity- and heritage-conserving, diversity-promoting and restorative (post-conflict/post-disaster) contributions of arts education.
- **Creative citizens** — A new generation cultivated through arts education, possessing creative and innovative capacity.
- **Global citizenship** — A goal fostered through multi-cultural dimensions in arts education practice and increased intercultural mobility of students and teachers.
