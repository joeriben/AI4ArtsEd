## 5. Where a newcomer stumbles

- The picture area shows "Step 1 / 25" after the run, i.e. noise; a user may think the run failed.
- Twenty of the 25 steps look alike (noise); the three labels promise composition and content where nothing can be seen.
- The caption disappears at the last position; "Step 25" is never written.
- The run list is at the bottom and in a drawer; the Help text says every run stays under "Runs", and the Outputs button carries no label apart from its title.
- Advanced settings are collapsed with no hint of what is inside; the seed is fixed, so two runs with the same prompt give the same picture.
- A single step can be enlarged by clicking the picture, and the enlarged frame carries "Step n / 25" and "Download Image"; nothing on the picture says so (from the page's code; not clicked).

## 6. What the help text should tell a user

The Help panel's own text, which opens on arrival, is quoted in 1: it says that the generator starts from noise and removes it step by step, preset at 25, that the platform holds every step and the slider picks the one in view, that two nets stand at the Backbone, that every run stays under "Runs", and to watch for the step from which the scene is recognisable and to compare two runs with the same prompt. It should say what the instrument is for: understanding what denoising is, as something the eye does and as something painters have done, and taking the mystery out of image generation by showing the whole run. It should say what the frame is: the noisy state of the run at that step, and not what the net at that step predicts the finished picture to be. The platform's code records the working latent; the live preview of ComfyUI, the engine underneath, shows the prediction instead, and the platform's own source check of 2026-08-13 names the difference as a didactic one that the surface leaves out. A frame that is noise to the eye is a frame from which the net already computes a whole picture; the first readable shape comes later for the eye than the three labels suggest, and the labels are the orientation from Choi et al. (2022) by noise level. It should say that a step can be enlarged by clicking the picture and saved with "Download Image", which makes one step a sheet to draw on. It should say that the surface shows one frame and no strip of thumbnails on purpose; the platform's decision of 2026-08-19 reads "Thumbnails take away the experience of imagining the next denoising step." And it should add: the view starts at step 1, which is noise; the seed is fixed in the advanced settings, so the same prompt gives the same run until the seed is changed, and two prompts under the same seed start from the same noise.

## 8. Teaching scenarios

The instrument shows one generation as 25 frames, each the noisy state of the run at that step, one at a time, so that the next step has to be imagined. It is for understanding what denoising is, with the eye and the hand as much as with the slider, and for taking the mystery out of the generator: no picture is fetched, nothing is painted stroke by stroke, the whole frame changes at every step, and the picture is not there until the end.

**S1 — When does the house appear?** (formal, computer science or art, grades 8–11, 45 min). Pairs generate one prompt each, scrub, and note the first step with a readable shape; the class collects the numbers on the board and compares with the slider's three labels. Sequence: 5 min prompt; 5 min run; 15 min scrubbing; 20 min collection on the board, one sentence per pair on what the slider did between step 0 and their number, and reading of the note. Subject link: iterative processes, noise.

**S2 — Two nets, and the words on the surface** (formal, computer science, grades 11–13, 90 min). Groups run the same prompt on both backbones, read the note's two sentences on cross-attention and joint attention, and present the step at which each net shows structure, in sentences that name steps, noise and the net, and not seeing, knowing or wanting. Then the class reads the surface's own words, "Watch the model work", "Backbone (the net that removes the noise)", "Archaeology", and the three labels Composition, Semantics, Detail, and says of each whether it names a process or makes the net sound like someone at work, and what each does to the trust a newcomer puts in the picture. Sequence: 15 min two runs; 25 min reading the note and scrubbing; 25 min presentations; 25 min the words on the surface. Subject link: architecture, attention; language about machines.

**S3 — The reveal** (non-formal, family workshop, 30 min). The facilitator shows step 21 of a run and the group guesses the prompt; then the final picture. Subject link: perception, how little is needed to recognise a scene.

**S4 — The Human Denoiser** (non-formal, family workshop, 30 min). The facilitator shows step 21 of a run and makes a printout. The print is used as a template to draw a picture according to the prompt along the noise pattern. Subject link: perception, imagination.

**S5 — Blot, wall, step** (formal, art, grades 7–10, 90 min). First the painters: the teacher reads Leonardo's advice to look at stained walls for landscapes and figures, and shows one of Alexander Cozens's blots (1785) with the landscape he drew from it. Pupils make an ink blot and draw a landscape from it. Then the model's material: a printed step-7 frame of a run whose prompt they know, and the task of S4, the prompt's scene drawn along the noise. Then the comparison: the drawing next to step 21 and the final picture of the same run. Where did the pupil put the house, where did the net; what in the noise did each take for a shape; what did the net settle first (the big masses, the division of the frame) and what last. Closing talk: the eye finds figures in noise, painters have used this on purpose for five hundred years, and the net does something else with the same noise: it computes, at every step, a prediction of the whole picture, in numbers, without an eye. Sequence: 15 min Leonardo and Cozens; 20 min blots; 25 min step-7 drawings; 20 min comparison; 10 min closing talk. Subject link: perception, imagination, chance as a method in art (Leonardo, Cozens, Max Ernst's frottage of 1925). Uncertainty: I did not try saving a frame; the enlarged frame's bar carries "Download Image" in the page's strings.

**S6 — Same noise, two prompts** (formal, computer science or art, grades 8–11, 45 min). The seed stays as it is. Pairs run two prompts, say a house in a landscape and a street in the evening, scrub both from step 1 side by side, and find the first step at which the eye can tell the two runs apart, and then the step at which each becomes its scene. One sentence per pair on what the prompt did to the same noise. Sequence: 5 min prompts; 10 min two runs; 20 min scrubbing in parallel; 10 min sentences on the board. Subject link: randomness and instruction, what a prompt does. Uncertainty: not run; that two prompts under one seed start from the same noise is how the platform's generator works and was not compared frame by frame here.

**S7 — How we talk about it** (formal, any subject, grades 7–10, 90 min). Before the run the teacher collects on the board how the class talks about image AI: "it paints", "it searches for pictures and puts them together", "it knows what a house looks like", "it sees the picture before it is finished". One run, scrubbed together on the projector from step 1 to the end. Each phrase is held against the 25 frames: which ones are misleading, and why; each is rewritten in words that name what was seen (noise, steps, the whole frame changing at once, a picture that was not there until late). Then pairs save the frames of steps 1, 7, 13 and 21 and the final picture, print them, and make a poster for the corridor: five frames, one rewritten sentence each, and the title of the myth it answers. Sequence: 10 min phrases; 25 min run and scrubbing with the phrases; 20 min rewriting; 35 min posters. Subject link: language about machines, description. Uncertainty: I did not try saving a frame.

**S8 — The words on the surface** (formal, computer science or art, grades 9–12, 90 min). The surface is full of terms, and each one stands next to the control that carries it. The class lists them: Denoising, Backbone, U-Net, MMDiT, cross-attention and joint attention (in the note), Steps, CFG, Seed, Negative Prompt, Random variation, and the three labels Composition, Semantics, Detail. Pairs take two terms each, move the control (Steps 10 and 50, CFG 1 and 20, a changed seed, a negative prompt, the other backbone), scrub the run, and write one sentence per term that names what the control did to the run, in their own words and without a human trait; for U-Net and MMDiT the sentence comes from the note's two sentences and from the two runs. The sentences are read aloud and tested by another pair against the surface; a sentence that cannot be checked there is rewritten. Then the class reads what the platform says the Latent Lab is for, in the Help's description of the lab, and writes the first line of the glossary: what this instrument does, for whom, and what it cannot do (one picture per run, 10 to 50 steps, two nets). Sequence: 10 min list; 30 min controls and runs; 20 min sentences; 15 min testing; 15 min the first line. Subject link: the vocabulary of generative AI, learned from the control that carries each word; description. Uncertainty: I did not change CFG and the negative prompt, and the Help's description of the lab was not opened on this surface.
