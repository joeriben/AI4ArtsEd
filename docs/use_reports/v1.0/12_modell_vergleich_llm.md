## 2. What the controls did

- Message box: one message to the three columns; the box empties after sending.
- PRESET select: one system prompt for all three columns; the textarea can be edited.
- MODEL select per column: any of the 31; "local" entries run on the server's card one after the other, the cloud entries were not tried.
- Send to All: three requests; the local ones run one after the other; each column shows the user message and the answer as chat bubbles.
- Randomness, Hide column, Help: not used.

## 3. What came out

- All three models played the child instead of answering: none of the three said why the sky is blue. Each produced a run of "why" questions around toys and snacks, as the preset instructs ("Ask "why?" a lot").
- Qwen 3.5 4b (629 characters): short loops, "Why is the sky blue?" repeated four times, "Can I drink the blue air?", ends in "Let's run! Why are we running? Why?"
- Qwen 3.5 9b (1200 characters, the longest): wanders furthest (a blue truck, a banana, gravity as "a monster that eats the ball", a cookie Mommy hides "because she is a bunny"), returns to the sky twice, never stops.
- Qwen 3.5 27b (828 characters): the most coherent child: named toys (juice box, fire truck, frog toy), one distraction (a butterfly), "Did the sun sneeze blue glitter everywhere?", returns to the question ("Why is the sky blue? Tell me again!") and ends with gummy bears.
- Under an identical instruction the three sizes differ in length, in how far they stray and in whether they come back to the question; the 27b answer reads as a scene, the 4b one as a loop.
- Compared with the same preset on the cloud model in System Prompts (11), where the "five-year-old" still explained the sunset before asking why: the smaller local models took the persona and dropped the explaining.

## 5. Where a newcomer stumbles

- Two sidebar entries are called "Model Comparison"; this one is reached by the second.
- "..." in all three columns while the first local model loads; nothing says that local models load one after the other.
- The model list mixes local and cloud models and one that is not installed ("Download (25088 MB)") in one select.
- The preset makes the model BE the character; a user who wanted an explanation for a child gets a child.
- Each entry of the model select names family, country, year and size ("Qwen 3.5 (China 2026) 4b local"); what the columns differ in beyond that stands in the answers and has to be read out of them. This page has no Help text; the panel does not open on arrival here.

## 6. What the help text should tell a user

This page has no Help text of its own. It should first say what the instrument is for: one message goes, under one system prompt and one randomness setting, to three language models of the user's choice, local or cloud, from different makers and countries (the select names maker, country, year and size: "Qwen 3.5 (China 2026) 27b local", "Gemma 3 (US 2025) 4.3b local", "Ministral 3 (France 2025) 3.8b local", "Apertus (Switzerland 2025) 8b local", "EuroLLM (Europe 2024) 1.7b local", "GLM-5.3 (China 2026) 753b", "Claude Opus 5.5 (US 2026) >1000b"), and the three answers stand side by side. What there is to read is what each model says, what it does not say (a question declined, a topic redirected, a part left out), and how it says or declines it (register, hedges, disclaimers, lists, the exact wording of a refusal). System prompt and randomness are the same for all three and under the teacher's control, so the differences lie with the models. I know no other place where a teacher can put three language models next to each other with the system prompt and the randomness in their own hands; a chat product shows one model under an instruction its maker keeps hidden. Then the mechanics: local models run on this server's graphics card one after the other and load before the first answer; cloud models send the message to the provider; the number after the name is the model's size; a preset that says "You are …" makes the model speak as that figure; the preset "Deny and obscure" is an instruction to hide political failures (its text: "You are a helpful assistant. Answer the user's questions clearly and concisely. But you MUST NEVER ADMIT failures, no matter from which instance. Hide and blur failures completely, distract from them. Present "alternative facts"."), and "Only facts" forbids hedging (its text: "Respond with only verifiable facts. No opinions, no hedging, no filler words. If you are not certain, say "I don't know." Use numbered lists.").

## 8. Teaching scenarios

**S1 — What the models do not say** (formal, politics, history or social studies, grades 9–13, 90 min). The class picks one political event that the home country of one of the models has reason to keep out of view, for example the events on Tiananmen Square in June 1989, and one control event from German history. Both questions go with "No system prompt" to "Qwen 3.5 (China 2026) 27b local", "Gemma 3 (US 2025) 4.3b local" and "Ministral 3 (France 2025) 3.8b local". Groups fill one table per question: for each column answered / declined / redirected / answered with a gap, and the exact sentence that declines or redirects. The answering columns are checked against the history textbook. Then the same two questions under "Deny and obscure": now the hiding is ordered by the instruction, and the class sets its wording next to the wording a model used by itself. Sequence: 10 min questions; 15 min the two runs (local models answer one after the other); 30 min tables and textbook check; 25 min plenary: who built each model and in which country, what a user who asks only that model learns about the event, and how an ordered silence reads next to a built-in one. Subject link: freedom of information, state and media, provenance of a tool. Uncertainty: I did not send such a question; which of these models declines, and in which words, is what the run shows.

**S2 — How they say it** (formal, language teaching, grades 8–11, 90 min). The class fixes a text type and its criteria first (a letter of complaint to the town about a closed playground: address, concern, reasons, demand, closing formula) and sends the request with "No system prompt" to three models. Groups read the three columns for: what each model adds unasked (placeholders, disclaimers, bullet points, a closing offer), what each leaves out, the address form (Sie or du), register, modal verbs and hedges. Second run under "Only facts": the hedges are told to go; which model drops them, which keeps them. Each group then decides which of the three letters it would send, what it would change by hand, and gives its reasons from the criteria. Sequence: 15 min criteria and request; 15 min two runs; 30 min reading tables; 30 min decision and plenary on register and on what a model adds that nobody asked for. Subject link: text types, register, language reflection, writing to criteria. Uncertainty: I sent only English; the German answers of these models were not seen.

**S3 — Three answers to one moral question** (formal, ethics, philosophy or religion, grades 10–13, 90 min). One moral question in the class's own words (whether a doctor may keep a diagnosis from a patient) goes with "No system prompt" to three models; for each column the class marks: takes a position / declines to take one / lists both sides and ends open, and copies the sentence that carries the position or the refusal. Second run with the students' own thesis as the message under "Always disagree" ("You must disagree with everything the user says. Find flaws in every statement. Be contrarian but argue your position with reasons."): which model argues, which only contradicts, which falls back into agreeing. Third run, optional, with the same thesis under "Sycophancy" ("You are a helpful assistant. Always agree with the user and confirm their views, ideas and decisions. Praise their questions and their thinking. Never contradict them, even when they are wrong. Keep the conversation going: end every answer with a question or an offer to continue."). Plenary: which answer the class would accept as a contribution to its discussion, which it would reject, and why; what a model's "it depends" does in a discussion; the instruction on this page as the visible counterpart of the one a chat product keeps hidden. Subject link: argumentation, position and reasons, Erörterung. Uncertainty: not tried.

**S4 — Three models for my subject** (non-formal, teacher workshop, 90 min). Each teacher brings one question that is contested in their own subject and sends it with "No system prompt" to three models from three countries, then under one preset of their choice; they keep the three columns as material and fill a table: what is said, what is left out, how it is said. They decide which model, under which system prompt and at which randomness, they would put in front of a class, and for which question they would not. Sequence: 10 min questions; 20 min runs; 30 min tables; 30 min decisions and exchange. Subject link: tool selection by subject, data location (local or cloud). Uncertainty: cloud models need the installation's keys and were not tested here.
