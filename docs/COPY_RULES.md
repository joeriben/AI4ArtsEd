# Copy Rules

The rules for user-facing text: labels, glosses, addresses (story and purpose texts), explanatory prose on instruments, and the copy of comparison surfaces. They are enforced in part by `devserver/tests/test_i18n_label_grammar.py`, `devserver/tests/test_i18n_direct_address.py` and `devserver/tests/test_i18n_filler_phrases.py`, which run as gates 5 to 7 in `scripts/git-hooks/pre-commit`. A gate is a floor: a string it passes has not yet been judged.

## 1. Label grammar: the string is the noun of its key

An informing label (rubric, field name, heading, card title, note title, tab, the one-line subtitle under a tab name (`…tabs.<name>.short`), tooltip, settings caption) is a naming form: no article, no tense, no pronoun, no subordinate clause, no question mark. It names the category of the content under it. Where the i18n key carries a noun, that noun is the string: `promptLabel` → "Prompt", `motivation` → "Reason", `summaryLabel` → "Summary", `modelsLabel` → "Models", `surfaceSettings` → "Surface settings". Where the key is itself a question (`whatHappensHere`, `whyRevealing`), the noun comes from reading what stands under the slot: the procedure, the material, the finding, the protocol. The same noun over two unlike bodies is a flag, not a pattern. A block rubric carries no colon; an inline rubric carries one.

Two forms are forbidden in a label slot:

- Phrase with relative clause or participle, with or without an article: "The prompt that made it", "Der Prompt, der es erzeugt hat", "Several AIs connected". The article presupposes a referent nobody has introduced, and the clause narrates the surface instead of naming it.
- Question title, open or closed, with or without "?": "What happens here", "Why this was offered", "Which machine makes it", "How It Works", "What does this tool show?". A question title carries an unwritten frame ("[here you learn] what happens here"); the slot holds the category of the answer, not the question that leads to it. A noun phrase that restates the question is still the question ("Origin of the surreal effect" for "Why do images become surreal?"): the naming form is the category of the answer. A gerund heading ("Reading the heatmap") names an activity, not a category; use the noun.

A gloss slot (a hint under a field, a settings description, a tab subtitle) takes one declarative sentence or a nominal description, not the naming form. A gloss the gate reports for its opening word ("Which transformer layer receives the steering vector.") is corrected into a declarative sentence, never reduced to a bare noun.

A genuine question addressed to the user (confirm dialog, reflection prompt, placeholder asking for input, error message) is content, not a label, and stays a question.

The label-grammar gate sees a trailing question mark, an initial interrogative in any letter case, and an article followed by a relative clause, in the label-slot keys (last segment ending in Label, Title, Head, Heading, Caption, Tooltip, Hint, Help on a camel-case boundary, or being name, short, sub, pick, motivation, apparatus, noteTitle, surfaceSettings; a segment of five camel words or more is a sentence used as an identifier and stays out of scope). Both lists in that test only shrink: `ALLOWED` holds genuine questions, `INHERITED_DEBT` holds the stock of 2026-09-04 and dead keys; a string written or rewritten after that date never enters either list. Renaming a key so that its last segment leaves the label suffixes is the same evasion as an allowlist entry; the slot decides, not the spelling of the key.

## 2. Supplied wording and the address

**Supplied wording precedes every criterion.** Text that was supplied for a surface is used verbatim; if it seems to violate a criterion, the violation is noted in one sentence and the text is used verbatim anyway. The source comment above such a key marks it with `SUPPLIED WORDING` (English comment) or `Vorgegebener Wortlaut` (German comment) and says which part is verbatim. The direct-address and filler gates read these markers; deleting the comment makes the string undocumented again.

**The address is the exception, not the form.** Addressing the reader as a subject ("Ihr", "you", "Sie") posits a subject on the other side and is allowed only with a specific reason, named in the source comment: a collective action context is addressed (a group working together on this surface), or there is no action context and attention is to be steered ("daran könnt Ihr erforschen, dass …"). Every other address is corrected. An action the reader can take is written as an open imperative or an instruction ("Ladet ein Bild hoch", "Upload an image"), never as a constative second person ("Ihr ladet ein Bild hoch", "You upload an image"), which states the reader's action as a fact and reads as an order. What there is to see is stated impersonally. Nothing turns into a timeless passive ("An image is uploaded"): nothing is uploaded at reading time. Text supplied in the address form stays verbatim. The address must not become the cliché and standard form of the platform's copy.

**The address-free form is the standard, not the substitute.** Addresses (story and purpose texts) are written address-free by default: the nominal phrasing of the operation („Eine Prompt-Eingabe geht an mehrere Bildmodelle") is the standard form, not the form a violation is corrected into. The imperative of an action („Schreibt", „Ladet ein Bild hoch") is not an address-free form; it is the same pseudo-personalisation in command form and is corrected into the nominal form like any other address.

## 3. Explanatory prose on an instrument

Explanatory prose (note paragraphs, guides, help texts) follows four prohibitions: no metaphor and no humanising; no dash standing in for the statement; no name the page has not introduced; nothing the reader already knows. On top of those: a name or coinage is introduced on first use with its role; a source's wording is quoted and attributed, never spoken in the platform's voice; at most one quotation per source and text, phrase-length or longer, decodable on its own; a number this project measured itself is not copy, and copy on an instrument reports only results from published sources, with the source named; no aphorism, antithesis or wordplay as explanation; the order is: what this instrument does, then what the source claims as attributed paraphrase, then the one quotation, then the reference.

**The test is the reader, not the feature.** For every sentence: what does someone understand who has the instrument in front of them and does not know the source? If the answer needs the source, the sentence is rejected; quotation marks, an introduced role, or a naming form do not save it. A quotation whose I/me/my/here/this cannot be resolved from the surrounding text is rejected. Meeting the individual requirements above is not by itself a pass.

**A sentence about the software is a technical claim, checked before its form.** For every sentence of copy, before any question of form: who technically does what, with what, and is that what the sentence says? "Schreibt einen Satz; daraus entsteht ein Bild" is false: no image arises from a sentence; the platform sends the prompt to an image model, and the model generates an image to it. Agentless verbs of growth and arrival (entstehen, kommen, ankommen, arise, emerge, come out) hide the machine and the operation and are not used for what the platform or a model does. The surface's word is the word: a thing is called in the copy what its label on the surface calls it (the field labelled "Prompt" holds a prompt, not a sentence, a line or an idea); a synonym breaks the reference between text and surface, and a label that itself departs from the platform's term is corrected at the label. The verb names the operation: what the reader does on the surface is named as that operation ("Gebt einen Prompt ein", "wählt", "kreuzt an", "klickt auf Generieren"), never as a paraphrase of it. Every definite article is covered by something the text has introduced or the surface labels. A claim about what the software does that the writer has not read in the view or the route is not written down; it is checked or left out.

**The task voice is the teacher voice without the teacher.** "Zu sehen ist, wie …", "Zu untersuchen ist, …", "What there is to see is …": the modal infinitive with its agent deleted hands out an assignment and pre-announces the finding. Attention is steered by an imperative of attention that names the concrete thing ("Achtet darauf, wann die Szene erscheint", "Hört darauf, wie …", "Vergleicht, ob …"), and what will be found stays open.

**No stage directions.** A sentence whose whole content is where something sits on the surface ("Links steht der Satz.", "Daneben stehen drei Systeme.") tells the reader what the reader sees and is deleted; a place is named only inside a statement of function or relation ("Über dem Bild steht die Übersetzung, die das Modell erhalten hat"). No staccato opener ("Three columns, three system prompts, one message.").

**One fact per sentence, and the doer is the doer.** No subject packed from three facts. The agent of a verb is what technically does it: the platform sends, the model receives and answers, the column shows; never the layout. The present tense is licensed as the consequence of an action named in the imperative ("Schreibt eine Nachricht; dasselbe Sprachmodell antwortet dreimal, in jeder Spalte unter einem anderen System-Prompt") or as a hedged description of what the instrument tends to do ("manchmal", "oft"), never as a timeless statement of what "is sent" or "is received".

**What belongs on an instrument is what holds only there.** Before the form of a sentence comes its subject matter: does it say something that is the case on this surface and not on the others? Three kinds of true sentence are struck without further examination. What the platform does everywhere (translating a prompt into English before an image model, checking it, scaling an image down, the order in which runs execute, a position in a queue) is platform matter and belongs in no instrument's address; the reader meets it on every surface, so naming it here says nothing about here. Machinery that changes nothing for the visitor is struck, however true („Die Modelle rechnen nacheinander, ein Bild je Modell"). A place on the screen is not content („Ganz unten …"). A sentence earns its place because it says what this instrument does that no other one does.

**Removing slop is a diff, not a rewrite.** The unit of work is the sentence that already stands there. For each one: keep it letter for letter, which is the default; or correct the smallest span that carries the fault while its content stays; or strike it under the criteria above. No sentence is added, and the text is never re-derived from the view and the route: what the existing text carries (the subject matter, the supplied wording, the reason this instrument exists) cannot be recovered from source code, and a pass that starts there loses it and fills the gap with protocol prose. A slop pass counts as finished only with the applied diff, a green gate and a commit.

**The evidence duty is asymmetric.** A sentence about the software is checked in the view or in the route and is corrected or struck when they contradict it. A sentence that says what the instrument is for (what is at stake beyond this screen) needs no render site and is never struck for lacking one. The source comments in `de.ts` and in `src/stores/pageAddress.ts` record which passages carry that weight and which are supplied wording; they are read before a text is touched, and what they mark stays.

**Prompts are not copy.** A string that reaches a model (example prompts, the additions of the census, perspective instructions, system-prompt presets, the sentence list of the voices, templates) is never touched by a label or prose pass, in any language, letter for letter; a pass that rewrites the words around a quoted prompt leaves the prompt itself and its language as they are.

**Filler says nothing.** A pleonasm („Wort für Wort" / "word for word" in an assertive use) and a metaphor for machine reading („wie eine Bauanleitung" / "like a set of instructions") carry no information the plain sentence lacks and are struck; the filler gate names the classes it can see without judging prose.

## 4. Comparison surfaces: one stage, and copy that says what a model is for

**The stage is shared.** Every surface that puts the same input to several systems stands on `assets/compare-stage.css`: page frame, column box, head row, name, queue badge, starting number, and the order "preparing column left, results right" (`.cmp-stage.cmp-work` + `.cmp-work > .cmp-ask` / `> .cmp-seed` / `> .cmp-results`). A second copy in a view is a parallel element structure (`docs/ELEMENT_STRUCTURE_REGISTRY.md`). Breakpoints are container queries: `.cmp-page` is the container, and the work area is 240 px narrower than the window whenever the instrument rail is open, so a `@media` query measures the wrong box and stacks the columns at the wrong moment.

**The info box is `.lab-note`** at the foot of the preparing column, keys `noteTitle` + `noteBody`, `LabReferences` flat underneath. `noteBody` may be an object keyed by whatever the surface lets the reader choose, so the text is about the systems actually running. The rest is in `docs/ELEMENT_STRUCTURE_REGISTRY.md`, which is read first.

**What the box says: what the maker wanted this model to be able to do.** The reason the thing was built and paid for, in the maker's own terms, from the paper or the release page, with the title named under the box. That is the whole subject. Strictly out: licences and revenue thresholds; founding histories and who left which company; parameter counts, encoder names and architecture names as an end in themselves; anything else that is company politics rather than capability. The differences between models are never reduced to seed, sampler, step count or resolution; the models differ because they are competing projects trained at great cost, and that is what the reader is looking at.

**The audience is schools and children.** Material about adult content, filtering for it, or what a dataset had removed from it stays out of the copy, however well sourced, however true, however much a maker published it themselves. A sentence can be correct and still have no business on this platform.

**No noun phrase whose referent nobody introduced.** "SD 3.5 was built for what the picture says": which picture, saying what, to whom? A figure that reads as a summary is not one. Name the operation: the writing inside the picture, how closely the picture follows the prompt.

**A failure is said, not drawn.** A picture that does not arrive says so in a line; an unreachable service never reaches the room as a broken-image icon, an empty box, or a gap. The error carries the real cause, never a house sentence.
