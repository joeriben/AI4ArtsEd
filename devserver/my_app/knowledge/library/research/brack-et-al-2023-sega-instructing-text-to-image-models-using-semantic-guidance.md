# SEGA: Instructing Text-to-Image Models using Semantic Guidance

## PROFIL

- Written by Manuel Brack, Felix Friedrich, Dominik Hintersdorf, Patrick Schramowski, Lukas Struppek, and Kristian Kersting (DFKI, TU Darmstadt, Hessian.AI, LAION), this scholarly article introduces SEGA (Semantic Guidance), a method for steering text-to-image diffusion models along semantic directions during generation. The authors argue that users of such models lack fine-grained semantic control—small prompt changes yield entirely different images—and that SEGA remedies this by manipulating the model's noise estimates with simple textual concept descriptions, requiring no additional training, architectural extensions, external guidance, or optimization, and computed within a single forward pass of the existing diffusion iteration. The paper also refutes earlier claims, notably by Kwon et al., that noise-estimate spaces are unsuitable for semantic manipulation.

- The article first situates SEGA among research traditions: word2vec-style embedding arithmetic and StyleGAN's semantic dimensions; latent diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion; image-editing approaches requiring segmentation masks or fine-tuning; and semantic-control methods it sets itself against—Prompt-to-Prompt (limited to token-based conditioning), Wu et al.'s Disentanglement (per-prompt optimization, fails at small edits), Composable Diffusion (cannot handle subtle changes), and Safe Latent Diffusion (restricted to inappropriate-content suppression). It then presents the method itself: concept vectors are isolated by taking the difference between concept-conditioned and unconditioned noise estimates and retaining only tail dimensions (1–5% suffice), with hyperparameters for warm-up and momentum, and support for multiple simultaneous concepts; a theoretical motivation is derived from an implicit classifier formulation. Four properties of the resulting semantic space are demonstrated: robustness, uniqueness, monotonicity, and isolation.

- The experimental evaluation covers a face-attribute benchmark with a user study (positive guidance succeeding in 95% of cases, negative in 92.1%, simultaneous edits up to 91%), inappropriate-content mitigation on the I2P benchmark for Stable Diffusion and DeepFloyd-IF, and comparisons showing SEGA outperforming Prompt2Prompt and Disentanglement on all tasks and beating Composable Diffusion overall (72.72% vs. 60.50%). A section on broader impact acknowledges misuse potential and inherited gender biases while noting SEGA can mitigate bias at inference time. Appendices provide implementation details on the diffusers library, hyperparameter intuition, further properties, user-study details, extensions to Paella and DeepFloyd-IF, comparisons against StyleCLIP and StyleGAN-NADA, and further qualitative results.

- The article is usable in arts-education contexts concerning generative AI, digital image-making, and interactive artwork: it demonstrates text-driven style transfer (famous artists, artistic epochs, drawing techniques), local and global image edits, and optimization of artistic conception for portrait photography, animation, concept art, character design, and modern architecture, while also serving discussions of bias, ethics of training data, and the disentanglement debate in machine-learning education.

## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (p. 1–2)

This scholarly article introduces **SEGA (Semantic Guidance)**, a method for steering text-to-image diffusion models along semantic directions during the generation process. The authors—Manuel Brack, Felix Friedrich, Dominik Hintersdorf, Patrick Schramowski, Lukas Struppek, and Kristian Kersting (DFKI, TU Darmstadt, Hessian.AI, LAION)—argue that while text-to-image diffusion models produce high-fidelity images, users lack fine-grained semantic control: small prompt changes yield entirely different images. SEGA addresses this by manipulating the model's noise estimates using simple textual concept descriptions, requiring no additional training, no architectural extensions, no external guidance, and no optimization—it is computed within a single forward pass of the existing diffusion iteration. The paper also refutes earlier claims (notably by Kwon et al. [15]) that noise-estimate spaces are unsuitable for semantic manipulation. The method is demonstrated on latent and pixel-based diffusion models including Stable Diffusion, Paella, and DeepFloyd-IF.

### Background and Related Work (p. 2–3)

The authors situate SEGA among several research traditions:

- **Semantic dimensions**: Word2vec-style embedding arithmetic (e.g., "King − male + female ≈ Queen") and StyleGAN's inherent semantic dimensions; SEGA enables analogous arithmetic for diffusion-based image generation.
- **Image diffusion**: Latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) perform diffusion in a compressed latent space, reducing computational cost.
- **Image editing**: Existing approaches require segmentation masks (inpainting) or expensive fine-tuning; SEGA edits relevant image regions through text alone.
- **Semantic control**: Prompt-to-Prompt manipulates cross-attention maps but is limited to token-based conditioning; Wu et al.'s Disentanglement requires per-prompt optimization and fails at small edits; Composable Diffusion combines noise estimates but cannot handle subtle changes.
- **Noise-estimate manipulation**: Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD) suppresses inappropriate content via noise-estimate manipulation, but is limited to that single task and to latent diffusion.

### The SEGA Method (p. 3–5)

**Guided diffusion foundation**: Diffusion models iteratively denoise a Gaussian variable, conditioned on a text prompt. Classifier-free guidance adjusts the unconditioned noise estimate toward the conditioned one, with a guidance scale s_g.

**Core intuition**: The high-dimensional noise-estimate (ε) space can be understood as composed of sub-spaces representing semantic concepts. For example, guiding toward "a portrait of a king" moves into a region where "male" and "royal" overlap. Subtracting the "male" direction and adding the "female" direction yields a "queen"—guidance arithmetic analogous to word2vec.

**Isolating semantics**: Noise-estimate values are Gaussian distributed. SEGA computes the difference between a concept-conditioned estimate ε_θ(z_t, c_e) and the unconditioned estimate ε_θ(z_t), scales it, and retains only the dimensions in the upper and lower tails of the distribution (defined by percentile threshold λ). Empirically, only 1–5% of dimensions suffice to encode a concept, making concept vectors largely isolated and simultaneously applicable. The space of these sparse vectors is called the **semantic space**.

**Formal definition**: The guided estimate combines the prompt-conditioned and concept-conditioned estimates; the semantic guidance term γ applies an edit guidance scale s_e elementwise to tail dimensions, with the sign ψ depending on whether the concept is added or removed. A larger s_e increases SEGA's effect.

**Theoretical motivation**: The authors derive SEGA from an implicit classifier formulation, showing it is fundamentally similar to classifier guidance with µ isolating the largest classifier-signal dimensions—while noting that, since estimates come from an unconstrained network rather than a true classifier gradient, no formal performance guarantees exist.

**Hyperparameters**:
1. **Warm-up δ**: guidance γ is zeroed before step δ; higher δ preserves overall composition and alters only fine details.
2. **Momentum ν_t**: accelerates guidance for dimensions continuously guided in the same direction, with momentum scale s_m ∈ [0,1] and β_m ∈ [0,1); momentum builds even during warm-up.

**Multiple concepts**: Each concept e_i gets its own γ_i_t with individual λ_i, s_i_e, and δ_i; the weighted sum of all guidance terms is applied. Momentum is built from all editing prompts and applied once all warm-up periods complete. Pseudo-code is provided in Appendix A.

### Properties of the Semantic Space (p. 5–6)

Four properties are demonstrated:

1. **Robustness**: Concepts like "glasses" are incorporated into images across domains in semantically grounded ways, even without instructions on how.
2. **Uniqueness**: A guidance vector computed once (e.g., for "glasses") can be applied to other prompts with the same seed, even across domain shifts such as photo-realism to drawings—though transfer is limited to the same initial noise latent and similar composition, and fails across larger shifts (e.g., faces to animals) without recalculation. Failed transfers introduce no visible artifacts.
3. **Monotonicity**: The magnitude of a concept scales monotonically with guidance strength s_e, for both positive and negative guidance (e.g., strength of "smile").
4. **Isolation**: Because each concept vector uses only a fraction of the noise estimate, multiple concepts can be applied simultaneously without interference—e.g., changing gender by removing "male" and adding "female" at once.

### Experimental Evaluation (p. 6–9)

**Face-attribute benchmark**: 250 images generated with the prompt "an image of the face of a random person" (Stable Diffusion), manipulating ten CelebA-derived facial attributes, in positive, negative, and simultaneous combinations (21 attribute changes total). A user study (Amazon Mechanical Turk; annotators labeled batches of 28 image/attribute pairs, with CelebA sanity-check images; each combination labeled by 3 annotators) found:
- Positive guidance adds the target concept on average in 95% of cases; Gender and Glasses reach 100% success; outliers were "Bald" (82.1%) and "Bangs" (82.7%), attributed to annotator unfamiliarity with "bangs" and to baldness requiring stronger hyperparameters.
- Negative guidance works similarly well (overall 92.1% success), though "beard," "bald," and "gray hair" were reduced but not fully removed in ~10% of images.
- Simultaneous edits: success rates remain similar for four concurrent concepts versus one (up to 91% for all four attributes applied at once), empirically confirming isolation.
- **Image quality**: FID against FFHQ improved from 117.73 (unedited) to 59.86 (edited), because SEGA's guidance frequently removed uncanny artifacts in faces.

**Inappropriate-content mitigation**: On the I2P benchmark, SEGA suppressed inappropriate content for both Stable Diffusion v1.5 and DeepFloyd-IF (parameters chosen to match SLD's "Strong" configuration). E.g., SD's overall inappropriateness probability dropped from 0.38 to 0.11; IF's from 0.38 to 0.15.

**Comparisons**: Randomized user studies against Composable Diffusion, Prompt2Prompt, and Disentanglement across four task types (multi-conditioning, minor changes, style transfer, concept removal) showed SEGA clearly outperforming Prompt2Prompt and Disentanglement on all tasks, and significantly beating Composable Diffusion on multi-conditioning (80.00% vs. 35.00%) and minor changes (90.91% vs. 72.00%), with comparable style transfer (90.00% vs. 100.0%) and removal (30.00% vs. 35.00%); overall 72.72% vs. 60.50% for Composable Diffusion. In pairwise fidelity comparisons, users preferred SEGA over Composable Diffusion for 83.33% of successfully edited samples (vs. 13.33%).

**Qualitative results**: SEGA performs style transfers (famous artists, artistic epochs, drawing techniques) requiring only λ ≈ 0.9—i.e., 10% of the ε-space suffices to change an entire image's style—while preserving composition, unlike prompt extensions which alter composition significantly.

### Broader Impact on Society (p. 9–10)

The authors acknowledge that SEGA could be misused to steer generation toward inappropriate material, and that Stable Diffusion's limited, biased gender representations are inherited by SEGA's edits (they restrict to binary labels while noting gender expression is not binary). Conversely, SEGA can mitigate bias at inference time without the adverse effects of removing training data. On labor concerns, they note creative jobs are threatened by generative models and training-data collection is ethically questionable, but argue SEGA promotes interactive artwork requiring substantial iterative human feedback.

### Conclusions and Future Work (p. 10)

SEGA provides sophisticated semantic control over image generation with no training, architectural change, or external guidance, and is compatible with any classifier-free-guidance diffusion architecture. The findings are relevant to the disentanglement debate: the level of control SEGA achieves is feasible without actively pursuing disentanglement. Future work includes quantifying concept representations in latent space, targeting different objects separately, and automatically detecting concepts to mitigate biases and address privacy concerns regarding memorized real people.

### Appendices: Implementation, Hyperparameters, and Further Results (p. 14–25)

- **Implementation (App. A)**: Built on the diffusers library's Stable Diffusion pipeline; one U-Net pass per concept description; pseudo-code in Algorithm 1.
- **Numerical properties (App. B)**: All noise estimates (unconditioned, prompt-conditioned, edit-conditioned) follow nearly identical Gaussian distributions.
- **Hyperparameter intuition (App. C)**: s_e of 3–4 suffices for delicate changes and can scale to 20+ without artifacts; λ ≥ 0.95 for small edits, 0.8–0.9 for whole-image changes like style transfer; δ ≥ 15 for detail-only changes, δ ≥ 5 for compositional editing; momentum is optional, mainly useful combined with warm-up. Visual ablations of all hyperparameter interplay are provided.
- **Further properties (App. D)**: Guidance-vector transfer succeeds or fails cleanly without artifacts; multiple concepts scale monotonically and independently (e.g., gradually removing crowds and trees from a scene).
- **User study details (App. E)**: A CelebA-trained classifier was unreliable on synthetic images (below 20% accuracy for some classes), motivating human evaluation; annotators paid $0.70 per batch (~5 minutes).
- **Architectures (App. F)**: SEGA implemented on Paella (compressed, quantized latent space) and DeepFloyd-IF (pixel-level, Imagen-based).
- **Comparisons (App. G)**: Composable Diffusion produces artifacts with 3+ concepts and worse composition preservation; Prompt2Prompt is limited to one editing prompt; Disentanglement fails at small edits and requires optimization; negative prompting offers no strength control and breaks with multiple concepts. Against StyleGAN-based StyleCLIP and StyleGAN-NADA, SEGA achieves similar fidelity but works ad hoc, without prompt-to-attribute mapping or model tuning, and on a general-purpose diffusion model rather than a single-domain StyleGAN.
- **Further qualitative results (App. H)**: Local and global edits on a car image (color, vehicle type, scenery) with minimal changes elsewhere; optimization of artistic conception using average embeddings of prompt collections for portrait photography, animation, concept art, character design, and modern architecture; probing learned concepts—adding "carbon emissions" to a car produces an older vehicle, reducing "safety" yields a roofless convertible—suggesting deeper concept "understanding" in diffusion models.

## GLOSSAR

- **SEGA (Semantic Guidance)** — A method to steer the diffusion process along semantic directions by manipulating noise estimates with textual concept descriptions, requiring no training or architectural change.
- **Semantic space** — The space of sparse noise-estimate vectors whose tail dimensions encode semantic concepts.
- **Guidance arithmetic** — Combining concept-conditioned and unconditioned noise estimates to perform vector arithmetic on concepts (e.g., "king" − "male" + "female" → "queen").
- **Edit guidance scale (s_e)** — Hyperparameter scaling the strength of a concept's expression; larger values increase SEGA's effect.
- **Percentile threshold (λ)** — Defines which tail dimensions of the noise-estimate difference encode the target concept; higher λ means smaller image regions are altered.
- **Warm-up (δ)** — Number of initial diffusion steps during which guidance is withheld; higher δ preserves composition and changes only details.
- **Momentum (ν_t, s_m, β_m)** — A term accelerating guidance for dimensions continuously guided in the same direction, gathered even during warm-up.
- **Isolation** — The property that different concept vectors occupy disjoint dimensions and can be applied simultaneously without interference.
- **Uniqueness** — The property that a guidance vector computed once can be transferred to other prompts (same seed) without recomputation.
- **Monotonicity** — The property that a concept's magnitude in the image scales monotonically with guidance strength.
- **Robustness** — SEGA's reliable, semantically grounded incorporation of arbitrary concepts across domains.
- **Implicit classifier** — A theoretical construct (p(c_e|z_t) ∝ p(z_t|c_e)/p(z_t)) used to motivate SEGA as a form of classifier guidance.
