# Road Map for Arts Education

## PROFIL

The Road Map for Arts Education argues that creative and cultural development constitutes a foundational, universal function of education grounded in human-rights instruments, rather than a peripheral or specialized pursuit. It positions itself against three persistent debates: whether Arts Education serves appreciation alone or also enhances learning in other subjects, whether art should be taught as a discipline for its own sake or for derived knowledge and values, and whether it is for a gifted few or for all learners. Drawing on Damasio's critique of cognitive overemphasis and "multiple intelligences" theory, the document asserts that Arts Education restores cognitive-emotional balance and operates through both "Arts Education" as individual study subjects and "Arts in Education" as a cross-curricular method. For arts education practitioners, the text provides a structural framework for teacher and artist preparation, partnerships between education and cultural systems, and the integration of formal and non-formal learning across diverse cultural contexts.

## WISSEN

### Background and Problem Framing

The Road Map for Arts Education, arising from the 2006 Lisbon World Conference, positions Arts Education as essential to meeting 21st-century needs for creativity and cultural awareness. It seeks consensus among stakeholders on Arts Education's role in improving educational quality, defining concepts, identifying good practices, and outlining concrete steps to introduce or strengthen Arts Education across formal and non-formal settings.

The document confronts three persistent debates that shape practitioner, teacher, and policy approaches:
- Whether Arts Education serves appreciation alone or also enhances learning in other subjects
- Whether art should be taught as a discipline for its own sake, for the body of knowledge, skills and values derived from it, or both
- Whether Arts Education is for a gifted few in selected disciplines or for all

The Road Map's comprehensive response asserts that creative and cultural development should be a basic function of education—not peripheral but foundational.

### Aims of Arts Education

Arts Education's rationale derives from human-rights instruments (UDHR Arts. 22, 26, 27; CRC Arts. 29, 31), grounding it as universal compulsory entitlement—including immigrants, minorities, and persons with disabilities.

Arts cultivate capabilities unavailable through other means: creativity, emotional intelligence, moral compass, critical reflection, autonomy, freedom of thought. Citing Damasio, modern education's cognitive overemphasis undermines moral behavior; Arts Education restores cognitive-emotional balance, equips learners for 21st-century workforce demands, and enables nations to leverage cultural capital for creative industries.

Quality education is defined by three principles:
- education that is relevant to the learner but also promotes universal values
- education which is equitable in terms of access and outcomes and guarantees social inclusion rather than exclusion
- education which reflects and helps to fulfil individual rights

Learning in and through the arts (Arts Education and Arts-in-Education) enhances at least four quality factors:
- active learning
- a locally-relevant curriculum that captures the interest and enthusiasm of learners
- respect for, and engagement with, local communities and cultures
- trained and motivated teachers

Arts manifest culture and transmit cultural knowledge intergenerationally. Where education systems fail to value cultural heritage, Arts Education—formal and non-formal—serves as vehicle for transmission, aligning with UNESCO Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity Action Lines:
- Encouraging linguistic diversity - while respecting the mother tongue - at all levels of education, wherever possible, and fostering the learning of several languages from the earliest age
- Promoting through education an awareness of the positive value of cultural diversity and improving to this end both curriculum design and teacher education
- Incorporating, where appropriate, traditional pedagogies into the education process with a view to preserving and making full use of culturally appropriate methods of communication and transmission of knowledge

### Concepts Related to Arts Education

Art labels such as 'dance' and 'music' are used worldwide yet carry culturally divergent meanings; any categorization of arts fields is therefore pragmatic, evolving, and non-exclusive. A tentative list includes performing arts (dance, drama, music, etc.), literature and poetry, craft, design, digital arts, storytelling, heritage, visual arts and film, media, and photography. Arts education should value process alongside result and emphasize interdisciplinary commonalities, since many art forms cannot be limited to one discipline.

Imagination is the characteristic feature of human intelligence; creativity is the application of imagination; innovation completes the process by utilizing critical judgement in applying an idea. Any approach must take the learner's own culture as point of departure — confidence rooted in one's culture enables appreciation of others — while acknowledging culture's perpetual evolution.

Two complementary approaches: (1) arts taught as individual study subjects, developing skills, sensitivity, and appreciation; (2) arts as a method of teaching and learning across all curriculum subjects. The Arts in Education (AiE) approach uses arts practices and cultural traditions as a medium for teaching general subjects (e.g., visual arts elements to teach physics, biology, geometry; drama or music to teach languages), drawing on 'multiple intelligences' theory to extend benefits to all students and subjects, requiring changes in teaching methods and teacher training.

Three dimensions of Arts Education: (1) knowledge gained in interaction with the artistic object or performance, the artist, and the teacher; (2) knowledge gained through the student's own artistic practice; (3) knowledge gained through research and study of an art form and of art's relationship to history.

### Education of Teachers and Artists

Effective Arts Education rests on two strategies: relevant education of teachers and artists, and partnerships between education and cultural systems. Partnerships require mutual understanding of goals and respect for competencies; both educators and artists must enter their professions with insights into the other's field, including a shared interest in pedagogy.

Generalist teachers need sensitivity to artistic values, skills to cooperate with artists, and capacity to produce, analyse, interpret, evaluate, and appreciate works across periods and cultures. Primary teachers often use the Arts in Education (AiE) approach — e.g., songs to memorise key words — to integrate arts into other subjects and reduce curriculum overload, yet integration fails without parallel specific arts teaching.

Arts teacher education must move beyond studio competency toward broader preparation, developing knowledge and skills in:
- One or more arts disciplines
- Interdisciplinary arts expression
- Methodologies for teaching the arts
- Methodologies for interdisciplinary teaching in and through the arts
- Curriculum design
- Assessment and evaluation appropriate for arts education
- Formal (school based) arts education
- Informal (community based) arts education

New technologies (computer art, electronic music, new media, online teaching) expand Arts Education's role but remain undertaught due to lack of experience, pedagogical training, and resources. Standards for art teacher preparation should be established at local and national levels. Artists likewise need pedagogical development and skills to cooperate with educators; joint training activities between artists- and teachers-in-training can secure future collaboration. Progress is crippled by lack of financial and cultural resources, especially in non-urban settings.

### Partnerships for Effective Arts Education

Despite policy rankings of creativity, quality education as its principal means is underrecognized. Implementing Arts Education is inexpensive and feasible if its philosophy rests on partnerships, necessitating joint responsibility between Ministries of Culture and Education with clearly defined roles.

Synergies for creative learning are best achieved through three partnership levels:
- Ministerial/municipality level: joint policies and budgets for curricular and extracurricular projects linking cultural institutions and schools to center art in education.
- School level: partnerships between schools and cultural institutions expand pedagogy beyond school limits; visits offer artistic encounters and integrated teaching practices.
- Teacher level: inviting artists into in-school and extra-curricular projects enriches teaching methods; institutions may provide online resources.

Challenges: Centralized budgets, bureaucratic narrowness, and differing structural 'cultures' between education and cultural fields hinder cooperation.

### Research and Knowledge Sharing in Arts Education

Informed decision-making requires evidence of Arts Education's effectiveness, yet the mechanisms by which it nurtures creativity remain poorly documented, weakening advocacy with policy makers. Existing evidence is scarce, anecdotal, and difficult to access; successful programmes often fail to convey theoretical assumptions or adequately document outcomes, leaving few best-practice case studies. This lack of an accessible knowledge base impedes practice improvement, policy influence, and systemic integration. Because Arts Education learning activities encompass creating art alongside reflecting on appreciation, observation, interpretation, critique, and philosophising about creative arts, researchers must look, think, and observe from both artistic and pedagogical perspectives. Research should focus on:

- Descriptions of the nature and extent of current Arts Education programmes.
- The links between Arts Education and creativity.
- The links between Arts Education and social abilities/active citizenship/empowerment.
- Evaluations of Arts Education programmes and methods, in particular of the value they add in terms of social and individual outcomes.
- The diversity of methods for delivering Arts Education.
- The effectiveness of Arts Education policies.
- The nature and impact of partnerships between education and culture in the implementation of Arts Education.
- The development and use of teacher education standards.
- Assessment of students' learning in Arts Education (evaluating best practice in assessment techniques).
- The influence of cultural industries (such as television and film) on children and other learners in terms of their education in the arts, and methods to ensure the cultural industries provide citizens with responsible kinds of Arts Education.

Implementation steps: creating an arts research agenda and seeking funding; organising seminars; surveying research interests among arts educators; promoting interdisciplinary collaboration on research methodologies. Universities and institutions should collaborate with a clearinghouse (or 'Observatory') that collects, analyses, repackages, and disseminates Arts Education knowledge—serving as a reliable data source for advocacy and lobbying. Universal quality education depends on Arts Education delivering the creativity, critical reflection, and ethical grounding necessary for responsible global citizenship.

### Recommendations for Educators, Parents, Artists, and Directors

UNESCO World Conference participants endorse prior regional declarations, asserting Arts Education as every child's right—developing aesthetic sense, creativity, and critical thinking—and as a response to 21st-century challenges: cultural diversity threatened by globalization, imagination needs in knowledge-based multicultural societies, cognitive and social skill development, social integration, therapy for children with disabilities and post-conflict contexts, and improved education quality. They acknowledge that art traditionally permeates everyday life and cultural transmission. Systemic challenges are identified: education policies devalue Arts Education; cultural and educational systems are dissociated; teacher training is insufficient; artists' participation is unrecognized; experience remains unresearched; budgets are inadequate.

**Advocacy, Support and Education**
- Raise public awareness and promote the value and social impact of Arts Education, creating a demand for Arts Education and skilled arts educators
- Provide leadership, support and assistance for teaching and learning in and through the arts
- Promote active participation in, and accessibility to, the arts for all children, as a core component of education
- Encourage the use of local, contextualized human and material resources as both the providers and the content of quality education
- Provide resources and learning materials to assist educators to develop, utilize, and share new arts-rich pedagogy
- Provide assistance to enable Arts Education practitioners to harness technological developments which will enable Arts Education to reach marginalized groups, and facilitate the creation of innovative knowledge products and the sharing of knowledge
- Support ongoing professional development of teachers, artists and community workers, in order to develop in professionals an appreciation of cultural diversity and enable them to develop their students' potential to create, critique and innovate
- Encourage and promote the development of art practices through digital media
- Set up, if they do not exist, cultural centres and other Arts Education spaces and facilities for youth

**Partnerships and Cooperation**
- Encourage active and sustainable partnerships between educational contexts (formal and non-formal) and the wider community
- Facilitate participation in learning contexts by local arts practitioners and the inclusion of local art forms and techniques in learning processes in order to strengthen local cultures and identity
- Facilitate cooperation between schools and parents, community organizations and institutions, and mobilize local resources within communities to develop Arts Education programmes, so as to enable communities to share transmitting cultural values and local art forms

**Implementation, Evaluation and Knowledge-sharing**
- Implement and evaluate collaborative school-community projects that are based on the principles of inclusive cooperation, integration and relevance
- Encourage effective documentation and sharing of knowledge between teachers
- Share information and evidence with stakeholders, including governments, communities, the media, NGOs and the private sector

### Recommendations for Government Ministries and Policy Makers

Governments must move from acknowledging Arts Education's value to systemic, cross-sectoral, regionally coordinated action. The argument advances across five domains: recognition must precede resource commitment; policy must be evidence-based, continuous across administrations, and regionally harmonized; implementation requires trained teachers, material resources, and culturally appropriate provision for indigenous and vulnerable populations; partnerships must span ministries, civil society, and the private sector; and knowledge-sharing must be institutionalized through databanks, documentation of oral cultures, and dissemination by Ministries of Education and Culture.

**Recognition**
- Recognize Arts Education's role in preparing audiences and public sectors to appreciate artistic manifestations;
- Acknowledge the importance of developing an Arts Education policy articulating links between communities, educational and social institutions, and the world of work;
- Recognize the value of successful locally-developed, culturally-relevant Arts Education practices and projects; future projects should replicate successful practices;
- Give priority to generating better public understanding and deeper recognition of Arts Education's essential contributions to individuals and society.

**Policy Development**
- Translate growing understanding into commitment of resources sufficient to translate principles into action, creating awareness of arts and creativity benefits for all and supporting a new vision for arts and learning;
- Design policies for national and regional Arts Education research, taking into account specificities of ancestral cultures and vulnerable population groups;
- Encourage development of strategies for implementation and monitoring to ensure quality;
- Give Arts Education a permanent central place in the educational curriculum, funded appropriately, staffed by teachers of appropriate quality and skill;
- Take research into account when making funding and programme decisions; articulate new norms of assessment of Arts Education's impact (demonstrated contributions to literacy, numeracy, and human and social benefits);
- Guarantee continuity transcending governmental programmes in States' public policies;
- Adopt regional arts education policies for all countries of a region (e.g. African Union);
- Include Arts Education in Cultural Charters adopted by all Member States.

**Education, Implementation and Support**
- Make professional education for artists and teachers available; where they don't exist, set up arts-education departments in universities;
- Make education of arts teachers a new priority; enable them to contribute more effectively to learning and cultural development; make arts sensitization part of training of all teachers and education actors;
- Make trained teachers and artists available in educational institutions and non-formal settings;
- Implement the arts throughout the school curriculum and in non-formal education;
- Make Arts Education available inside and outside schools to all individuals, whatever their abilities, needs, and social, physical, mental or geographical situation;
- Produce and make available to all schools and libraries the material resources necessary for effective delivery: space, media, books, art materials and tools;
- Provide Arts Education to indigenous peoples in a manner appropriate to their cultural methods of teaching and learning, accessible in their own languages, recalling UNESCO Declaration on Cultural Diversity principles;
- Study ways and means to draw up localized Arts Education programmes based on local values and traditions.

**Partnerships and Cooperation**
- Promote partnerships among all concerned ministries and governmental organizations to develop coherent and sustainable Arts Education policies and strategies;
- Encourage government officials at every level to join forces with educators, artists, NGOs, lobby groups, business community, labour movement, and civil society to create specific advocacy action plans and messages;
- Encourage active involvement in education of arts and cultural institutions, foundations, media, industry, and private sector;
- Integrate partnerships among schools, artists, and cultural institutions into the core educational process;
- Promote sub-regional and regional cooperation in arts education to reinforce regional integration.

**Research and Knowledge-sharing**
- Develop a complete databank of human and material Arts Education resources, available to all educational institutions including via the Internet;
- Ensure dissemination of information about Arts Education, implementation, and follow-up by Ministries of Education and Culture;
- Encourage creation of collections and inventories of works of art enriching Arts Education;
- Document the current oral culture of societies-in-crisis.

### Recommendations for UNESCO and NGOs

UNESCO and intergovernmental/non-governmental organizations are urged to advance Arts Education across three axes: advocacy, partnership, and research-based knowledge-sharing.

**Advocacy and Support**
- Reflect Arts Education's contributions to all areas of society; identify it as a major cross-sectoral strategy
- Link Arts Education with resources and related areas (Education for All, Education for Sustainable Development)
- Emphasize bottom-up strategies empowering grassroots initiatives
- Promote knowledge of socio-cultural and environmental problems through Arts Education so pupils develop values, belonging, and commitment to sustainable development
- Encourage communication media to support Arts Education objectives and foster aesthetic sensitivity and artistic values in the public
- Continue including Arts Education in international programmes
- Make budget provisions to foster Arts Education and promote curricular inclusion
- Promote interdisciplinary and trans-disciplinary Arts Education at different levels and modalities to open new aesthetic channels
- Promote investments providing cultural goods, material resources and funding to:
  - Create specialized areas in schools and cultural spaces offering varied forms of Arts Education
  - Provide specialized didactic materials, including publications in mother-tongue languages
  - Ensure development of Arts Education and fair pay and working conditions for teaching professionals
- Encourage governments and agencies to facilitate collaboration between ministries, departments, cultural institutions, NGOs and arts professionals
- Convene future conferences for regular reflection and continuous improvement; support Republic of Korea's offer to host a second World Conference in Seoul

**Partnerships and Cooperation**
- Facilitate coordination between cultural and educational institutions to agree upon and implement Arts Education policies and activities
- Encourage defining abilities and mechanisms for articulating formal and non-formal Arts Education between educational and cultural institutions
- Create cooperative networks between Member States and within their education and cultural systems, basing successful development on cooperative activities and alliances
- With reference to the African Union–UNESCO partnership agreement (Khartoum, January 2006):
  1. Support adoption and proclamation by UN Member States of a Decade for Arts Education for All (2006–2016)
  2. Rethink Education-for-all objectives to include arts education
  3. Consolidate support to national institutions promoting culture and the arts in Africa (e.g. CRAC in Togo, CELTHO in Niger), to Arts Education institutions (public or private), and to civilian initiatives consolidating endogenous artistic abilities
  4. With the African Union and sub-regional organizations (CEDEAO, SADDEC, CEMAC, etc.), support holding an African Regional Conference on Arts Education

**Research, Evaluation and Knowledge-sharing**
- Promote ongoing evaluation of emotional, social, cultural, cognitive and creative impacts of Arts Education
- Promote a regional system to gather and disseminate information on Arts Education
- Promote knowledge-sharing and networking through Arts in Education Observatories (clearinghouses), with UNESCO Chairs and the UNITWIN Network
- Promote research in the arts to inform future initiatives in this expanding field
- Establish an international data-base of research providing scientifically sound evidence of individual and social significance of Arts Education and creative involvement, including: development of the integrated human being, social cohesion, conflict resolution, public health, and use of new technologies in creative expression in schools
- Commission case studies and research as guides for participatory and practice-led research, leading to an international network of researchers sharing methodologies and building better assessment models with students, artists, teachers and parents as active participants; building capacity and informing lifelong learning and assessment
- Encourage research and rediscovery of traditional use of arts in learning and everyday life
- Record and evaluate bibliographical resources and other information sources for analysis, re-packaging and dissemination
- Systematize significant experiences to prepare quality indicators for Arts Education and promote exchange of experiences

## GLOSSAR

- **Road Map for Arts Education** — The document's own name for itself, conceived as an evolving reference for introducing or promoting Arts Education in educational settings.
- **formal and non-formal** — The distinction between institutionalized education systems and non-institutional learning environments, both targeted by the Road Map.
- **Arts-in-Education** — Learning through the arts integrated into other subjects, distinguished from Arts Education as standalone arts learning.
- **cultural capital** — A nation's cultural resources and heritage that can be leveraged to develop creative industries and socio-economic development.
- **Arts in Education (AiE)** — An approach in which the arts are integrated into teaching of general curricula subjects, especially at primary level, to support learning across disciplines.
- **multiple intelligences** — A theory drawn upon by the AiE approach to extend the benefits of arts education to all students and subjects.
- **studio competency** — The practical artistic skill base in a given discipline that arts teacher education must move beyond toward broader pedagogical preparation.
- **Artists in School (AIS)** — Programmes bringing professional artists into educational settings to enhance arts learning through direct engagement with students and teachers.
- **creative learning** — Learning promoted through synergies between the arts and education.
- **curricular and extracurricular** — Class projects that take place inside or outside school hours.
