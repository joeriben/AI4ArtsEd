# DIMCIM: A Quantitative Evaluation Framework for Default-mode Diversity and Generalization in Text-to-Image Generative Models

## PROFIL

The article is written by Revant Teotia, Candace Ross, Karen Ullrich, Sumit Chopra, Adriana Romero-Soriano, Melissa Hall, and Matthew Muckley, affiliated with FAIR at Meta, NYU's Courant Institute, Mila, McGill University, and CIFAR, and is addressed to researchers working on the evaluation of text-to-image (T2I) generative models. Its subject is DIMCIM ("Does-it/Can-it"), a quantitative, reference-free framework measuring two properties of T2I models: default-mode diversity (whether generic prompts yield a varied distribution of expected attributes) and generalization capacity (whether explicitly prompted attributes are actually generated). The authors' position is that existing metrics are inadequate — reference-free metrics like the Vendi Score do not capture real-world diversity, reference-based metrics like FID, Recall, and Coverage require curated datasets per category, and most yield only a single summary statistic; they also position the work against the concurrent GRADE, prompt datasets such as Winoground-T2I, T2I-Compbench, ABC-6K, and ConceptMix, and consistency metrics like TIFA and VQAScore, whose scoring they modify to consider only the specific attribute-object pair. The paper contains: the DIMCIM framework with its two metrics (DIM from coarse prompts, CIM from dense prompts) built hierarchically over concepts, attribute types, and attributes; the COCO-DIMCIM benchmark (30 concepts, 494 attributes, 930 coarse prompts, 14,641 dense prompts) constructed from COCO captions with Llama3.1; experiments on LDM2.1, Flow-Int, LDM3.5L, and FLUX.1-dev revealing a trade-off in which larger models and higher classifier-free guidance improve generalization at the cost of default-mode diversity; failure-mode analyses covering negation and a newly identified case of attributes generable by default but not on request (e.g., "closed"-wings birds, "full" refrigerators); a training-data study showing a 0.85 Pearson correlation between training-image and generated diversity; and appendices with training details, further failure modes, and the dataset-creation pipeline. The document is usable in disciplines and situations concerned with evaluating and auditing generative image models, benchmark construction, and the study of how training-data composition shapes model output.

## WISSEN

### Overview and Purpose (p. 1–2)

The paper introduces **DIMCIM** ("Does-it/Can-it"), a quantitative, reference-free evaluation framework for measuring two properties of text-to-image (T2I) generative models: **default-mode diversity** (Does the model generate images with a varied distribution of expected attributes when given generic prompts?) and **generalization capacity** (Can the model generate specific diverse attributes for a concept when explicitly prompted?). The authors argue that existing diversity metrics are inadequate: reference-free metrics like the Vendi Score do not capture real-world diversity, while reference-based metrics like FID, Recall, and Coverage require curating new image datasets for every new category of interest, making them expensive and inflexible. Existing metrics also typically produce a single summary statistic without fine-grained, interpretable information. The paper's contributions are:

- The DIMCIM framework with its two metrics, the Does-It Metric (DIM) and Can-It Metric (CIM).
- The COCO-DIMCIM benchmark, built from COCO concepts and captions and augmented with Llama3.1.
- An analysis of state-of-the-art T2I models revealing a trade-off: models improve in generalization at the cost of default-mode diversity when scaling from 1.5B to 8.1B parameters.
- Identification of fine-grained failure cases, both expected (e.g., negation) and unexpected (e.g., "closed refrigerator").
- A training-data study showing a correlation of 0.85 between diversity in training images and default-mode diversity.

The authors are Revant Teotia, Candace Ross, Karen Ullrich, Sumit Chopra, Adriana Romero-Soriano, Melissa Hall, and Matthew Muckley, affiliated with FAIR at Meta, NYU's Courant Institute, Mila, McGill University, and CIFAR (p. 1).

### Related Work (p. 2–3)

The authors situate DIMCIM against three strands of prior work:

1. **Diversity metrics**: reference-free metrics (Vendi Score) compute pairwise similarity within a batch but do not capture real-world diversity; reference-based metrics (FID, recall, coverage) require large comparison image sets for each diversity axis. The concurrent work GRADE measures the distribution of generated attributes for a concept but addresses only default-mode bias, not generalization capacity.
2. **Prompt datasets** for attribute-object composition (Winoground-T2I, T2I-Compbench, ABC-6K, ConceptMix): these test only a few attribute-object combinations, whereas DIMCIM extensively generates many prompts and attributes per concept.
3. **Consistency metrics** (including CLIPScore-style and VQA-based approaches like TIFA and VQAScore): DIMCIM uses a modified VQAScore that considers only the specific attribute-object pair, avoiding the need for an external LLM for question generation and mitigating linguistic biases.

### The DIMCIM Framework (p. 3–5)

#### Definitions (p. 3)

DIMCIM is organized hierarchically around:

- **Concepts** C: everyday objects such as dog, hat, car.
- **Attribute types** T_c: axes of diversity per concept (e.g., for dog: color, breed, position; for hat: color, style, material).
- **Attributes** A_c,t: the values within each type (e.g., A_dog,color = {black, white, golden}).
- **Coarse prompt** p_c,−: a prompt containing a single target concept without any visual attributes (e.g., "The dog plays in the grass."). Used to measure default-mode diversity.
- **Dense prompt** p_c,a: the coarse prompt augmented with one explicit attribute (e.g., "The brown dog plays in the grass."). Used to measure generalization.

#### Attribute-Concept Scoring (p. 4)

For a prompt p_c, the model generates n images. For each image i, an **image-attribute score** s(i, a_j) is computed for every candidate attribute a_j in A_c,t using VQAScore, which asks a vision-language model "Does this Figure show {text}?" and returns the probability of a "Yes" answer. Crucially, the authors use truncated text containing only the concept and attribute (e.g., "a metal dining table") rather than the full prompt, because full prompts let irrelevant background context influence the score. The **attribute-concept score** S(p_c, a) is the difference between the mean image-attribute score of attribute a and the mean image-attribute score of all other attributes in A_c,t.

#### Summary Statistics (p. 4–5)

- **Does-It Metric (DIM)**: computed from coarse prompts. The attribute-level score S(p_c,−, a) indicates how much an attribute is generated relative to others of the same type in default mode; large positive or negative values indicate over- or under-representation. The summary DIM is 1 minus the mean of the absolute value of S(p_c,−, a) across all concepts and attributes — a high score means balanced, diverse default-mode generation.
- **Can-It Metric (CIM)**: computed from dense prompts. The attribute-level score S(p_c,a, a) measures whether the explicitly requested attribute is actually generated more than other attributes of the same type. The summary CIM is the mean of S(p_c,a, a) over all dense prompts.

### The COCO-DIMCIM Dataset (p. 5–6)

The benchmark is built from COCO:

- **Concept and attribute collection**: 30 everyday object classes are selected as concepts; for each, 31 COCO captions are randomly chosen as seed prompts, filtered with spaCy so the concept is the first noun/main subject. Human-related captions and concepts (e.g., containing "child," "person," "woman," "man") are excluded, since the work addresses visual attribute diversity of objects, not normative diversity in depicting people. Llama3.1 extracts visual descriptions from seed prompts and generates additional plausible attributes, then groups attributes by type. Attributes that are visually ambiguous or mutually non-exclusive (age, motion, size, model name, accessories) are manually filtered out.
- **Coarse prompts**: the LLM removes existing visual attributes from seed prompts while preserving context/environment (e.g., "A wooden table in a living room" becomes "A table in a living room").
- **Dense prompts**: the LLM injects one attribute at a time into each coarse prompt, skipping attributes that would produce unnatural prompts (e.g., "A bird perched in the air").

The resulting benchmark contains 30 concepts, 494 attributes, 930 coarse prompts, and 14,641 dense prompts. Each concept has on average 4.83 attribute types and 16.46 attributes, with 31 coarse prompts and an average of 488 dense prompts (15.74 per coarse prompt) per concept.

### Experiments and Results (p. 6–9)

#### Models Evaluated and Setup (p. 6)

Four models are evaluated: LDM2.1 (1.5B parameters, trained on ~5 billion public images, then higher-resolution and aesthetic fine-tuning), LDM3.5L (8.1B, a multi-modal diffusion transformer with two text encoders), FLUX.1-dev (12B, a rectified flow transformer), and a proprietary flow-matching model denoted **Flow-Int** (1.9B, using control conditions and latent perceptual loss). For each model, n = 30 images are generated per dense prompt and an equivalent number for coarse prompts. Scoring uses VQAScore with the InstructBLIP-Flan-T5-XXL model; findings were cross-validated with other VQAScore models. Main analysis uses classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale 7.5.

#### Overall Results: A Diversity–Generalization Trade-off (p. 6)

Table 1 shows that as model size increases, DIM decreases while CIM increases:

- LDM2.1 (1.5B): DIM 0.815, CIM 0.299
- Flow-Int (1.9B): DIM 0.802, CIM 0.315
- LDM3.5L (8.1B): DIM 0.799, CIM 0.374
- FLUX.1-dev (12B): DIM 0.785, CIM 0.326

Smaller models produce more balanced attribute distributions by default; larger models better generate explicitly requested attributes. Table 2 shows the same trade-off with CFG on Flow-Int: CFG 2.0 gives DIM 0.839 / CIM 0.261; CFG 5.0 gives 0.809 / 0.309; CFG 7.5 gives 0.802 / 0.315 — increasing guidance improves generalization but worsens default-mode diversity, likely because stronger guidance increases prompt-image consistency.

#### Failure-Mode Analysis (p. 6–8)

**Default-mode bias (DIM)**: For the concept airplane (attribute type purpose), "passenger" has a very high DIM across all models, "military" a very low negative score, and "cargo" and "private" moderate scores. For table (material), "wooden" scores very high and "glass" very low. For bird (state), "perched" scores high even though "flying" is equally common in the real world. Notably, the CIM scores for these under-represented attributes remain high — models can generate them when explicitly asked.

**Generalization failures (CIM)**:

- Models generalize well for attribute types like color, material, and pattern.
- Larger models generalize better for certain concept-attribute pairs ("digital" clock, "convertible" car, "freight" train) where smaller models score negatively; e.g., Flow-Int fails on "convertible" cars while LDM3.5L and FLUX.1-dev succeed. Conversely, FLUX.1-dev struggles with a boat on "land," which other models handle.
- All models struggle with **negation**: for the concept bed, prompts like "without blanket" and "without pillows" yield low CIM, with models generating the negated attributes anyway — confirming a known failure mode (Conwell et al., 2024).
- **A new failure mode**: low (negative) CIM but high DIM — models fail to generate an attribute when explicitly requested yet generate it in default mode. The key example is the "open"/"closed" distinction: models fail to consistently generate "closed"-wings birds (LDM3.5L) or "full" refrigerators (Flow-Int) when prompted, but produce both states with coarse prompts.

#### Training-Data Investigation (p. 8–9)

Using a version of Flow-Int trained only on CC12M, the authors compute DIM scores for both generated images and CC12M training images (concept images retrieved via VQAScore with a threshold of 0.8). The Pearson correlation across all concept-attribute pairs is **0.85**, indicating that default-mode diversity closely reflects attribute skew in training data (e.g., "ceramic" bowl scores high in both; "wooden" bowl low in both). DIMCIM also identifies anomalies: "flying" bird is prevalent in training data but not generated with coarse prompts, suggesting that increasing representation in training data may not always suffice to fix default-mode diversity.

### Discussion and Limitations (p. 9)

The authors summarize their findings: DIMCIM is a reference-free framework revealing a generalization/diversity trade-off with increasing model size, the ability of models to generate uncommon attributes only when explicitly prompted, the correlation between training-data and generated diversity, and new failure cases (attributes generable by default but not on request). Limitations include: reliance on external models (LLM for dataset construction, VLM for scoring), mitigated by realistic captions, manual filtering of attributes, and use of open-source models; the method does not distinguish between multiple valid definitions of a concept; and generalization is studied only under explicit prompting, not other methods such as external guidance.

### Appendix Highlights (p. 12–18)

- **Flow-Int training details**: the main version was trained on ImageNet, CC12M, YFCC, and an internally licensed dataset; the Section 5.3 version on CC12M only.
- **Additional failure modes**: LDM2.1 struggles with "empty" refrigerators; most models struggle with "broken" umbrellas (FLUX.1-dev especially); smaller models (Flow-Int, LDM2.1) generate "foals" better than FLUX.1-dev; no model can generate "inverted" bowls; FLUX.1-dev is poor at "curly"-haired dogs and boats on "land." Negation failures extend to suitcases "without wheels," elephants without tusks, and beds without blankets. Color, pattern, material, and dog-breed attributes are easy for all models.
- **Dataset creation pipeline and meta-prompts**: the appendix documents the LLM instructions (in Python functions) used to extract attribute types/values from seed prompts and to generate coarse and dense prompts, including the requirement that dense prompts be plausible captions of naturally occurring images.

## GLOSSAR

- **DIMCIM** — The overall "Does-it/Can-it" reference-free framework for measuring default-mode diversity and generalization capacity of text-to-image models.
- **Does-It Metric (DIM)** — The measure of default-mode diversity — whether a model generates a balanced distribution of attributes for a concept from generic, attribute-free (coarse) prompts; summary score is 1 minus the mean absolute attribute-concept score.
- **Can-It Metric (CIM)** — The measure of generalization capacity — whether a model generates an explicitly requested attribute more than other attributes of the same type when given dense prompts; summary score is the mean attribute-concept score over all dense prompts.
- **Default-mode diversity** — The variation of visual attributes a model produces for a concept when prompted generically, without explicit diversity-related instructions.
- **Generalization capacity** — A model's ability to generate diverse attributes for a particular concept when explicitly prompted to do so.
- **Concept** — An everyday object class (e.g., car, refrigerator, dog) around which the benchmark is hierarchically built.
- **Attribute type** — An axis of diversity relevant to a concept, such as color, material, breed, position, or state.
- **Attribute** — A specific value within an attribute type for a concept (e.g., "black" within dog:color).
- **Coarse prompt** — A prompt containing a single target concept with all visual attribute information removed, used to compute the Does-It Metric.
- **Dense prompt** — A coarse prompt augmented with one explicitly injected attribute, used to compute the Can-It Metric.
- **Seed prompt** — An original COCO caption containing the selected concept as its main subject, from which coarse and dense prompts are derived.
- **Image-attribute score s(i, a)** — The VQAScore-based probability that attribute a is present in generated image i.
- **Attribute-concept score S(p_c, a)** — The difference between the mean image-attribute score of attribute a and the mean score of all other attributes of the same type, for images generated from prompt p_c.
- **COCO-DIMCIM** — The benchmark dataset built from COCO (30 concepts, 494 attributes, 930 coarse prompts, 14,641 dense prompts) using the DIMCIM framework and Llama3.1.
- **Flow-Int** — The paper's designation for a proprietary flow-matching text-to-image model used in the experiments.
