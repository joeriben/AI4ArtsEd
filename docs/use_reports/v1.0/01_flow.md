## 2. What the controls did

- Green plus: adds material cards (text, image, sketch, PDF). Cards are draggable; the circle is the "zone" that selects what goes into the run.
- The six medium glyphs switch the output medium; a short click switches the medium, a long press opens the model list for that medium. The first node's label is the model name. "auto" hands the choice back to the material.
- The middle node is the run button; its label changes with what the run will do ("start the image adaption", "start the image generation", "start the text generation", "create the prompt" once a perspective lies in the zone).
- During a run the band replaces the three nodes with the pipeline stages, naming the model and provider of each stage and the sampler settings (seed, CFG, steps) of the inference. "hold to stop" (long press) aborts.
- The output node shows the result and the progressive picture during sampling; every result also lands on the table as a numbered card (#2, #3…). Image cards carry four small actions on hover: Add to favorites, Download, Print, Image Analysis.
- Perspective chooser: category circle → labelled bubbles → one bubble on the table → drag into the zone. The "?" badge on the bubble presumably explains the perspective (not opened).
- "choose a LoRA" glyph and the gear "Surface settings" were not used. "?" top right: Help: opens by itself on the first arrival with the page's text quoted in 1; every later click shows that text again.

## 5. Where a newcomer stumbles

- "0 %" with no further message. The band shows the inference stage but not whether it is queued, loading a model, or stuck.
- The "text" medium: the button says "start the text generation", but what happened was an image generation plus an image reading. The relationship between the text medium and the material in the zone is not visible beforehand.
- The perspective overlay: dragging a perspective from the overlay does nothing, only clicking places it.
- Medium switch by short click vs. model list by long press: nothing on the surface says that a long press exists.
- Results pile up on the table at the same spot (#2, #3, #4 overlap), the German reading card covers the pictures.
- Error text "Service not reachable. Tell the course leader." appeared after the service restart although the service was reachable a few seconds later; a second press worked.

## 6. What the help text should tell a user

The Help panel's own text, which opens on arrival, is quoted in 1: a working surface for artistic work with or without the platform's tools, where ideas in text, image and sound are laid side by side, combined and worked on further; what is learned (to choose, to judge, to decide how to go on; AI as part of creative work without standing at its centre); and the first move (prompt box, circle, Start). It should add what the surface is made of, in this order:

- **Material of any kind.** The green plus adds text, image, sketch and document (PDF); every result of a run falls back onto the table as a card and is material for the next run. The circle is the zone: as soon as material lies in it, the run reads only the zone; an empty zone reads the whole table (the page's own string).
- **The medium follows the material; override is possible** The six glyphs are image, video, sound, 3D, code and text; "auto" hands the choice back. Text is the one medium that reads instead of making: one piece in the zone is described, two are compared, and the long press on the text glyph lists five reading frames instead of models (structural media education, iconology, ethical: "Who is shown and who is absent, the power relations in the picture, the conditions it was made under", critical, descriptive). Image and video also list the paths that take a picture as input (Qwen Edit, Qwen Multi) and want one or two pictures on the table.
- **A perspective is a Prompt Interception.** A language model rereads the idea through the perspective; the page's own node text says "a pedagogical interception, not prompt polishing". The chooser shows seven category bubbles; the camera bubble showed me seven perspectives, and the configuration (`devserver/schemas/configs/interception`) holds 35 unhidden perspectives in these seven categories: Photography & Media (Good Picture, Bad Picture, Daguerreotype, Analog Photography, Analogue Copy, Cooked Film, Digital Photography, and Early Photography Landscape, which the bubble did not show me), Critical Thinking (Against the Model, One World, Planetarizer, Critique of Consumption, The Unspoken), Art History (Bauhaus, Literati, Renaissance), Creative Transformations (De-Kitsch, Sweetener, Slang, Amplifier, On the Contrary!, Your Story, Chain Translation, Word Game, Scene Graph, Listifier, Technical, Generative Techniques, Music Composer), Attitudes (Forceful, mad world, Sensitive), Research (Latent Lab, Surrealizer) and Art & Culture with "Your Call!": write a perspective yourself ("what should shape, alter, or question the idea?"), name it and save it under your account, where it is there again next time. Several perspectives in the zone run as a chain. The intercepted prompt appears as its own card, editable: change it by hand and run again.
- **The run is visible.** The band names every stage with its model (included elements, Prompt Interception, Prompt Adaption, check and translation, inference with seed, CFG and steps). A perspective that looks something up while reading leaves a source card under the circle ("Searched for", "Article", "Nothing was found under this term") with the summary that went into the reading. The back of every result card ("Turn the card") carries "prompt as submitted" and the line "AI marking: AI-generated (marked in the file)". A code result opens ("Open the code"), can be changed and run again; a 3D result turns.
- **A LoRA lies on the table like a perspective.** The grid glyph lists the adapters released to this session in three groups, Platform, Course and Own trainings, with strength and trigger; an adapter trained on the LoRA Training page from the group's own pictures is used here.
- **Favorites are shared material.** The bar at the foot switches between "My favorites" and "All favorites"; a favourite is dragged onto the board to keep working with it, your own or somebody else's.
- **The Help knows the run.** The page hands the material and the interception result to the Help panel, so it can be asked what the perspective did to the idea.

Only then the handling: short click on a glyph picks the medium, long press lists the models; a perspective is placed by clicking and dragged into the circle; a long press on "hold to stop" aborts; a run at 0 % waits for a model to load or for another run to finish; the gear holds "Clear canvas", which keeps the last result.

## 7. Use possibilities, action-oriented

- The artistic workflow: try a prompt out, change it, change the results again with other interceptions, try different models; make a picture and a sound from the same prompt and a video from the two (picture and sound together in the circle, video glyph); merge several pictures into one (two or three in the circle, long press on the image glyph, "Qwen Multi"); craft and combine, a picture with a text, a reading with a sketch; and arrange the results on the surface for comparison and for your own judgement, for example spatially by interception bubble: the works of one perspective around its bubble, those of another around its bubble, the default without perspective between them. Cards are dragged anywhere, the table zooms (+, −, 1:1), and only what lies in the circle is read, so the arrangement outside it stays as it is. I did not run sound, video and the merging.
- Write one idea, lay it into the circle, and run it under five perspectives in turn (Critique of Consumption, On the Contrary!, Against the Model, Sweetener, De-Kitsch); read the Prompt Interception card of each run before looking at the picture: what the perspective did to the idea stands there in words. Sort the results together with its respective perspective as clusters on the table an proceed working.
- Write a perspective of your own under "Your Call!", save it, and run the same material through it next to a platform perspective.
- Lay two perspectives into the circle at once and read the chain: the second rereads what the first wrote.
- Edit an intercepted prompt by hand on its card and run it again; compare with the unedited run.
- Put your own photograph on the table, choose the text medium and read it through two frames (ethical, then descriptive); then lay the reading into the circle as material and make a sound or a picture from it.
- Lay multiple images in the circle, if you want also a prompt and/or a perspective and lora. Things will happen.
- Run with "Against the Model" or "The Unspoken" and open the source cards under the circle to see what the perspective looked up.
- Discover that with tricky prompts, flow will look up wikipedia pages in its cultural original versions (if available) and throw the results on the table.
- Lay a LoRA from "Own trainings" on the table next to a sketch and run; then the same sketch without it. Not tried.
- Keep a card's back as the record of a work: "prompt as submitted" and the AI marking are the label for a wall or a catalogue.
- Switch the favorites bar to "All favorites", drag another group's picture onto the board and go on from it (or find the prompt on the back of the card and re-use it).
- Ask the Help panel, after a run, what the perspective changed in the idea.
- Throw an image and a generated sound into the action circle, and it will be combined into a movie file
- Draw a sketch and turn it into any media
- reuse prompt and images from the favorites in order to continue own or other's work
- load any document, use prompt interception, use the result as a new prompt interception
- load a picture, use text to get an interpretation of your choice, and proceed with the line above this one

## 8. Teaching scenarios

**S1 — The artistic workflow** (formal or non-formal, art, from grade 7, 90 min to a full day). One prompt is tried out and changed; its results are changed again with other interceptions; different models are tried on the same material; from the same prompt a picture and a sound are made and, with both in the circle, a video; two or three pictures are merged into one; results are crafted and combined, a picture with a text, a reading with a sketch; and on the surface the results are arranged for comparison and for the group's own judgement, spatially by interception bubble: around the "Cooked Film" bubble everything it made, around "On the Contrary!" everything that one made, the runs without perspective in the middle, the discarded ones at the edge. The arrangement is the judgement; it is read at the end from the table, not from a list. Sequence (90 min version): 15 min prompt, first runs, first changes; 45 min interceptions, models, sound and video, merging, each result placed as it arrives; 20 min the group re-arranges by its own criteria; 10 min each group walks the others through its table. Subject link: artistic process, variation and series, judging one's own work. Uncertainty: I arranged nothing, made no sound or video and merged nothing; my cards piled up at one spot (see 5).

**S2 — One theme, six weeks: the table as the studio** (formal, art, grades 8–12, 6 × 90 min). Flow is the working surface of the whole unit; the board is kept only for the browser session, so what is to come back next week is added to the favorites from its card and dragged onto the board again from "My favorites". Week 1: one theme sentence per student, run without perspective, then under five perspectives (Critique of Consumption, On the Contrary!, Sweetener, De-Kitsch, Against the Model); before each picture the Prompt Interception card is read and what the perspective put in is underlined; one card is edited by hand and run again. Week 2: an own perspective under "Your Call!", saved, run against a platform perspective; 30 min side trip to the Prompt Interception Lab (Language Model Comparison, Interception Scope) to see the same interception across models. Week 3: from the best prompt a picture and a sound, then a video from the two; three students' pictures merged into one. Week 4: own photographs and drawings as material: read through the ethical and the descriptive frame, a sketch turned into any medium, a picture changed with a text ("Qwen Edit"). Week 5: an adapter trained from the class's drawings on the LoRA Training page, laid on the table under "Own trainings". Week 6: the table arranged by interception bubble for the class's own judgement; the exhibition is built from the card backs ("prompt as submitted", AI marking); the favorites are the archive of the unit. Subject link: artistic process over time, series, medium change, judging one's own work; the side trip links the perspective to the instruments that show what it does. Uncertainty: I did not run sound, video, merging, own perspective, LoRA and the favorites across sessions.

**S3 — Our own perspective** (formal, language teaching, grades 9–12, 2 × 90 min). Groups write a perspective under "Your Call!": a text that says what should shape, alter or question an idea, in the group's own words (a stance, a genre, a way of seeing), name it and save it. Each group runs three materials (a sentence, a photograph, a short poem) through its perspective and through one platform perspective of the same category, reads the two interception cards for each, and revises its own wording twice. Day 2: groups exchange perspectives through the saved list and write a short review of what another group's perspective does to a text. Subject link: writing an instruction, register and stance, text revision. Uncertainty: I did not save a perspective; the save path is taken from the page's own strings.

**S4 — Picture, reading, picture** (formal, ethics or politics, grades 9–13, 90 min). Each student brings one photograph (own or a press photo, checked for rights) onto the table. First run: text medium, frame "ethical" ("Who is shown and who is absent, the power relations in the picture, the conditions it was made under"); second run: frame "descriptive". The two readings are compared with what the student sees. Then the ethical reading is laid into the circle as material and a new picture is made from it; the class compares what the chain kept and what it lost. Sequence: 10 min pictures; 25 min two readings; 20 min comparison; 20 min the new picture; 15 min plenary. Subject link: image and power, description and judgement, what a reading adds. Uncertainty: I ran only the default frame, on a generated picture.

**S5 — The class's own style as LoRA** (formal, art, grades 10–13, project of 3 × 90 min). The class trains two adapters on the LoRA Training page from its own drawings, one from one student's twelve sheets, one from the whole class's; both appear in Flow under "Own trainings". Day 2: the same sketch, drawn on the table, runs without adapter, with the single-hand adapter and with the class adapter, at two strengths; the class fixes criteria beforehand for "that is still ours". Day 3: the pictures with their card backs ("prompt as submitted", AI marking, the adapter's trigger) as a wall; discussion of whose work the pictures are. Subject link: style, authorship, what a dataset of twelve sheets can hold. Uncertainty: I trained nothing and laid no LoRA on the table.

**S6 — The chain across groups** (non-formal, youth media workshop, one day, age 14+). Four groups start with one sentence each. Every 45 minutes each group opens "All favorites", takes the newest result of the group to its left, and goes on from it in a different medium: a picture becomes a reading, the reading a sound, the sound and a picture a video, a sentence a sketch of code that is opened, changed and run again. Each group decides what it adds by hand (a sentence, a drawing, an edited interception) and what it lets a model make, and notes it on the card. The day ends with four chains of five or six cards each, read from the card backs. Subject link: collaboration, medium change, what passes from one medium to the next. Uncertainty: I did not run sound, video, code and favorites across accounts; the chain is built from the page's own media list and the favorites bar.

**(b) Mutatis mutandis**


**Beyond the framework**: the framework's example for Create 2 Advanced has learners join a text generator's outline, an image generator's picture and a coding assistant's code into one prototype. S1 and S2 do that with a picture and a sound joined into a video and with several pictures merged into one, and then go on to what no classroom example of the framework asks for: the results are arranged on the table by interception bubble, and the arrangement is read as the group's judgement of its own series. The framework has no learner expectation for judging a series of one's own work; Engage 3 Advanced (accept, revise or reject) is the nearest, and S1 and S2 go past it.

**Non-fits**: Engage 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, Manage 1, 2, Shape 1, 4 — the scenarios make and judge; nobody in them describes how the models work and no bias is measured. Those are the side trips to the instruments of reports 05–33.
