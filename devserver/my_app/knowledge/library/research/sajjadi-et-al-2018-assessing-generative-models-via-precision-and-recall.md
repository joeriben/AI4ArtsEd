# Assessing Generative Models via Precision and Recall

## PROFIL

The text argues that a single-value summary is inadequate for comparing generative models, proposing instead a novel definition of precision and recall for distributions that disentangles divergence into two separate dimensions: the quality of generated samples and the coverage of the target distribution. It sets itself against one-dimensional evaluation metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS), which conflate distinct failure cases like mode dropping and mode inventing. By decomposing model performance, the framework reveals that Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) tend toward high precision and low recall, whereas Variational Autoencoders (VAE) exhibit the opposite tendency. For arts education, this methodology offers a structured approach to critically evaluating AI-generated artworks by independently assessing aesthetic fidelity and stylistic comprehensiveness.

## WISSEN

### Problem Framing and Thesis

Deep generative models (VAE, GAN) suffer from a lack of quantitative evaluation: the target distribution is specified only implicitly—sampling is possible but likelihood evaluation is intractable, and even tractable likelihood would be misleading in high dimensions. Surrogate metrics such as Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) partially address this; FID is robust to image corruption, correlates with visual fidelity, and works on unlabeled data. However, all commonly used metrics yield a one-dimensional score and thus cannot distinguish between distinct failure cases. Two GANs trained on MNIST and CelebA obtain similar FIDs yet differ fundamentally: one produces realistic samples covering only a subset of digits (mode dropping), the other produces low-quality samples covering all digits. This motivates the thesis that a single-value summary is inadequate. The proposed approach disentangles divergence into **precision** (quality of samples from the learned distribution Q) and **recall** (proportion of the reference distribution P covered by Q), enabling separate quantification of mode dropping and mode inventing. Contributions: (1) novel definition of precision and recall for distributions with proven desirable properties, (2) efficient sample-based algorithm, (3) relation to total variation, IS, and FID, (4) empirical quantification of mode dropping/inventing on image and text data, (5) first metric experimentally confirming that GANs tend toward high precision/low recall while VAEs tend toward low precision/high recall.

### Related Work and Demarcation

The section surveys evaluation metrics for deep generative models to establish a gap. Log-likelihood comparison is rejected: high likelihood can coincide with low sample quality and vice versa, and kernel density estimation in high-dimensional spaces is intractable. The **Inception Score (IS)** measures sample quality via a classifier's conditional label entropy, but requires labeled data and offers weak guidance for model comparison. The **FID** embeds samples in a feature space, fits Gaussians, and computes a distance; it is sensitive to both spurious modes and mode dropping. An unbiased variant, the Kernel Inception Distance, shares high rank-order correlation with FID. Classifier-accuracy proxies require retraining per model and risk over-weighting a single artifact dimension. The demarcating thesis: all commonly used metrics are **one-dimensional**, yielding only a single score or distance. A prior precision/recall proposal based on distance to the true-data manifold is noted but dismissed as computable only on synthetic data, not complex data sets. This gap motivates a multi-dimensional precision/recall framework.

### PRD: Precision and Recall for Distributions

The section derives a distributional analogue of precision and recall. Precision measures how much of Q can be generated by a part of P; recall measures how much of P can be generated by a part of Q. Let S = supp(P) ∩ supp(Q). P and Q are each decomposed into a component on S and a component on the complement of S; the complement components represent recall loss (P's part missed by Q) and precision loss (Q's noise not generated by P). When P_S = Q_S, two scalars suffice. When P_S ≠ Q_S, the authors resolve the ambiguity by proposing a trade-off curve rather than a two-number summary: a distribution μ on S serves as a 'true' common component, and varying μ traces a frontier between precision and recall. Unlike binary-classification PR curves, this trade-off describes a fixed model, not different classifiers. **Definition 1**: Q has precision α at recall β w.r.t. P if P = βμ + (1−β)ν_P and Q = αμ + (1−α)ν_Q for some distributions μ, ν_P, ν_Q. **Definition 2**: PRD(Q,P) is the set of all attainable (α,β) plus (0,0). The set distinguishes cases that symmetric metrics (e.g., FID) conflate. **Theorem 1** — PRD(Q,P) satisfies:
- (i) (1,1) ∈ PRD(Q,P) ⇔ Q = P (equality)
- (ii) PRD(Q,P) = {(0,0)} ⇔ supp(Q) ∩ supp(P) = ∅ (disjoint supports)
- (iii) Q(supp(P)) = max_{(α,β)∈PRD(Q,P)} α (max precision)
- (iv) P(supp(Q)) = max_{(α,β)∈PRD(Q,P)} β (max recall)
- (v) (α′,β′) ∈ PRD(Q,P) if α′∈(0,α], β′∈(0,β], (α,β)∈PRD(Q,P) (monotonicity)
- (vi) (α,β) ∈ PRD(Q,P) ⇔ (β,α) ∈ PRD(P,Q) (duality)

Properties (i)–(ii) confirm equality and disjoint-support behavior; (iii)–(iv) connect to the support decomposition and distinguish mode-dropping (high precision, low recall) from mode-invention (low precision, high recall); (vi) gives precision and recall a symmetric dual interpretation.

### Algorithmic Realization and Theoretical Connection

Computing the set PRD(Q,P) from the original definitions is non-trivial, requiring checks of suitable distributions μ, ν_P, ν_Q across all α and β. Theorem 2 provides an equivalent definition independent of these distributions, enabling an elegant algorithm. The key insight: PRD(Q,P) is a union of line segments along α = λβ for all λ ∈ (0,∞), each segment running from the origin to the maximal achievable point (α(λ), β(λ)). The algorithm computes pairs α(λ) and β(λ) over an equiangular grid of λ values at angular resolution m ∈ ℕ, yielding an approximation ̂PRD(Q,P). Different distributions Q_i can be compared by plotting their respective PRD curves, with full sets approximated by interpolation between ̂PRD(Q_i,P) and the origin. For λ = 1, the formulation reduces to the total variation distance δ(P,Q) between P and Q, establishing that the proposed precision/recall notion generalizes total variation distance.

### Application to Mode Dropping and Inventing

The proposed algorithm evaluates precision and recall of deep generative models by embedding samples via a pre-trained Inception network (Pool3 layer), clustering the union of ˆP and ˆQ with mini-batch k-means (k=20), and comparing cluster-assignment histograms. Failing to populate clusters with many real samples hurts recall; populating clusters without real samples hurts precision. The procedure is averaged over multiple randomized clusterings.

Against symmetric metrics like FID, which cannot distinguish mode dropping from mode inventing: on CIFAR-10, ˆQ4 (dropping one class from ˆP) and ˆQ6 (inventing one spurious class) yield similar FID, yet their PRD curves differ strikingly. Adding correct modes increases recall; adding fake modes decreases precision. IS only increases as classes are added, being invariant to the reference distribution.

The algorithm requires only a meaningful embedding, extending to text: on MultiNLI sentences embedded via BiLSTM max-pooling, PRD curves confirm recall sensitivity to mode dropping. Unlike IS, the algorithm does not require labeled data or a dataset-specific classifier.

### Empirical Evaluation: Class Imbalance, GANs vs VAEs, and Conclusion

Two MNIST GANs with virtually identical FID yield divergent PRD curves: one generates few digit classes at high quality (high precision, low recall), the other covers all classes at lower quality (low precision, high recall). A classifier trained on generated samples confirms this via class histograms and average confidence scores (96.7% vs 88.6%). PRD curves relate to IS through entropy of conditional vs marginal label distributions (Spearman correlation -0.83 for precision, 0.89 for recall), but differ critically: PRD requires no classifier or labeled data, and captures finer-grained recall losses beyond per-class imbalance. To summarize PRD curves across many models, the symmetric F₁ score is insufficient; instead, the pair F_β and F_{1/β} (with β=8) asymmetrically weights precision versus recall. On Fashion-MNIST, this reveals VAEs generally achieve lower precision and/or higher recall than GANs, providing the first metric confirming the folklore that VAEs produce lower-quality samples while being less prone to mode collapse. The conclusion argues one-dimensional scores cannot capture distinct failure modes; the proposed precision/recall notions are theoretically sound, connected to total variation distance, FID, and IS, and applicable via an efficient sample-based algorithm.

### References and Formal Proofs

Lemma 1 establishes an equivalence: $(\alpha, \beta) \in \text{PRD}(Q,P)$ iff there exists a distribution $\mu$ such that $P(\omega) \geq \beta\mu(\omega)$ and $Q(\omega) \geq \alpha\mu(\omega)$ for all $\omega$, connecting Definition 1 to a constructive test.

Theorem 1 proves six properties of PRD independently:
- (i) **Equality**: $(1,1) \in \text{PRD}(Q,P)$ iff $P=Q$.
- (ii) **Disjoint support**: $\text{supp}(P) \cap \text{supp}(Q) = \emptyset$ iff $\text{PRD}(Q,P) = \{(0,0)\}$, shown by contraposition.
- (iii) **Maximum precision**: $\alpha \leq Q(\text{supp}(P))$ for all $(\alpha,\beta) \in \text{PRD}(Q,P)$, with existence shown by constructing $\mu$ on $S = \text{supp}(P) \cap \text{supp}(Q)$.
- (iv) **Maximum recall**: follows from applying (vi) to (iii).
- (v) **Monotonicity**: downward closure under scaling of $\alpha, \beta$.
- (vi) **Duality**: symmetry under swapping $\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta$, $P \leftrightarrow Q$.

Theorem 2 proves $\text{PRD}(Q,P) = \{(\theta\alpha(\lambda), \theta\beta(\lambda)) \mid \lambda \in (0,\infty), \theta \in [0,1]\}$ by showing both inclusions: forward via $\lambda = \alpha'/\beta'$ and $\theta = \beta'/\beta(\lambda)$; reverse by constructing $\mu(\omega) = \min(P(\omega), Q(\omega)/\lambda)/\beta(\lambda)$.

Figure 8 demonstrates PRD's diagnostic advantage: two collapsed GANs producing only 1's are indistinguishable under a trained classifier, yet PRD correctly identifies the right model as having slightly higher recall due to varied (tilted) 1's. Figure 10 reports Spearman correlations of −0.83 and 0.89 between unsupervised $F_{1/8}$, $F_8$ measures and supervised $P(y|x)$, $P(y)$ measures across GANs and VAEs on MNIST.

## GLOSSAR

- **precision** — The quality or fidelity of samples drawn from the learned distribution Q relative to the reference distribution P.
- **recall** — The proportion of the reference distribution P that is covered by the learned distribution Q.
- **mode dropping** — A failure mode where the generative model produces realistic samples but only covers a subset of the target distribution's modes.
- **Inception Score (IS)** — A metric using a pretrained classifier's label-distribution entropy to score generated image quality.
- **FID** — A distance between real and generated samples computed from Gaussian fits in a feature embedding space.
- **one-dimensional** — The property of yielding a single scalar score, which the authors identify as the shared limitation of existing metrics.
- **PRD(Q,P)** — The set of all attainable precision–recall pairs (α,β) for distribution Q with respect to reference distribution P, characterizing the trade-off between them.
- **supp(P)** — The support of distribution P: the set of states with positive probability.
- **ν_P / ν_Q** — Residual components in the mixture decomposition: ν_P is the part of P missed by Q (recall loss), ν_Q is the noise part of Q (precision loss).
- **total variation distance** — A measure of distance between two probability distributions, denoted δ(P,Q), to which the PRD framework reduces when λ = 1.
