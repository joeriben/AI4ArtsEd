# Diffusion Models Already Have a Semantic Latent Space

## PROFIL

The document is an ICLR 2023 paper by Mingi Kwon, Jaeseok Jeong, and Youngjung Uh of Yonsei University, written for the machine-learning research community. Its subject is the semantic image-editing capability of frozen, pretrained diffusion models, and its claim is that such models already contain a semantic latent space, contrary to the common belief that they lack one. The authors propose the "asymmetric reverse process" (Asyrp), which discovers and exploits this space — named "h-space", the bottleneck feature maps of the U-Net noise predictor — enabling semantic editing without fine-tuning, extra classifiers, or complicated inversion. The paper attributes to h-space four properties: homogeneity, linearity, robustness, and consistency across timesteps, and it introduces a generative-process design built on two quantifiable measures, editing strength of an interval and quality deficiency at a timestep. It positions itself against image guidance, classifier guidance, and per-attribute fine-tuning (DiffusionCLIP), and builds on DDPM, DDIM, CLIP-based directional loss, and stochastic noise injection following Karras et al., 2022. Its contents proceed from background and the problem of "destructive interference" (Theorem 1), through the asymmetric reverse process and h-space, implicit neural directions f_t, generative-process design with editing and quality-boosting intervals, experiments on CelebA-HQ, LSUN-bedroom/-church, AFHQ-dog, and METFACES with user studies against DiffusionCLIP, analysis of the h-space properties, and appendices covering related work, proofs, layer choice, hyperparameters, algorithms, training details, evaluation, and additional results. ## WISSEN

### Purpose and Thesis (p. 1–2)

This ICLR 2023 paper by Mingi Kwon, Jaeseok Jeong, and Youngjung Uh (Yonsei University) argues that frozen, pretrained diffusion models already contain a semantic latent space, contrary to the common belief that they lack one. The authors propose the "asymmetric reverse process" (Asyrp), which discovers and exploits this semantic latent space — named "h-space" — enabling semantic image editing without fine-tuning the model, training extra classifiers, or complicated inversion procedures. The paper claims h-space has four key properties: homogeneity, linearity, robustness, and consistency across timesteps. Additionally, it introduces a principled generative-process design using two quantifiable measures — editing strength of an interval and quality deficiency at a timestep — to achieve versatile editing and quality boosting. To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first attempt to discover the semantic latent space in frozen pretrained diffusion models.

The paper positions itself against three existing manipulation approaches for diffusion models: (1) image guidance, which mixes latent variables of a guiding image with unconditional latents but is ambiguous in which attribute it reflects; (2) classifier guidance, which requires training an extra classifier for noisy images and is computationally costly; and (3) fine-tuning the whole model per attribute (e.g., DiffusionCLIP), which requires multiple models for multiple descriptions. Unlike GANs, which have straightforward latent-space editing but suffer from difficult inversion of real images, diffusion models offer nearly perfect inversion (via deterministic DDIM), so discovering a semantic latent space in them would enable editing real images directly.

### Background (p. 2–3)

The paper reviews three foundations:
- **DDPM**: a latent variable model learning data distributions by denoising, with a forward Markov process of Gaussian transitions parameterized by a variance schedule, and a reverse process with a noise predictor.
- **DDIM**: a non-Markovian reformulation; with η = 0 the process becomes deterministic and guarantees nearly perfect inversion.
- **CLIP-based image manipulation**: directional loss with cosine distance between CLIP text and image embedding differences achieves homogeneous editing without mode collapse. For the facial attribute "smiling", the prompts "smiling face" and "face" are used as target and source descriptions.

The paper also uses an abbreviated sampling equation where P_t denotes the predicted x_0 and D_t denotes the direction pointing to x_t.

### Discovering the Semantic Latent Space (p. 3–5)

**The problem**: Simply updating x_T to optimize the directional CLIP loss leads to distorted images. Shifting the predicted noise ε_θt at each sampling step also fails: Theorem 1 states that the shifted terms in P_t and D_t cancel each other out ("destructive interference"), so the resulting image is nearly identical to the original.

**Asymmetric reverse process**: To break the interference, only P_t is modified by shifting ε_θt, while D_t is preserved. This modifies the reverse process toward the target attribute while x_t−1 follows the original flow at each step. The modified P_edit_t and original P_source_t serve as visual inputs for the directional CLIP loss, with regularization of their difference. However, ε-space lacks the properties needed for a semantic latent space.

**h-space**: Since ε_θt is implemented as a U-Net, the authors choose its bottleneck — the deepest feature maps h_t — to control ε_θt. By design, h_t has smaller spatial resolution and higher-level semantics. Adding Δh_t to the original feature maps successfully manipulates attributes. h-space exhibits: the same Δh leads to the same effect on different samples; linear scaling of Δh controls attribute magnitude (even negative scales); multiple Δh's manipulate multiple attributes simultaneously; Δh preserves image quality; and Δh_t is roughly consistent across timesteps.

**Implicit neural directions**: Directly optimizing Δh_t on multiple timesteps is difficult. Instead, an implicit function f_t(h_t) — a small network with two 1×1 convolutions concatenating timestep t — produces Δh_t. Learning f_t is more robust to learning-rate settings, converges faster, and generalizes to unseen timesteps and features. This allows training on a subsequence (S = 40 steps) and inference on any length of generative process through normalization: Δh scaled by S/Ŝ preserves the sum of Δh_t.

### Generative Process Design (p. 5–6)

The editing process has three phases: editing with Asyrp, traditional denoising, and quality boosting.

**Editing interval [T, t_edit]**: Since diffusion models generate high-level context early and fine details later, semantic changes are made in the early stage. **Editing strength** of an interval [T, t] is defined via LPIPS between the original image and the predicted image P_t — intuitively the perceptual change from T to t. The shortest interval with enough editing strength is found at LPIPS(x, P_t_edit) = 0.33. For attributes requiring larger visual changes (e.g., "pixar" > "smile"), the strength is increased by δ = 0.33·d(E_T(y_source), E_T(y_target)) (cosine distance between CLIP text embeddings), choosing t_edit with LPIPS = 0.33 − δ.

**Quality boosting interval [t_boost, 0]**: Stochastic noise injection improves quality (following Karras et al., 2022). **Quality deficiency** at t is defined as γ_t = LPIPS(x, x_t), the amount of noise in x_t compared to the original. Empirically, t_boost with γ_t_boost = 1.2 achieves quality boosting with minimal content change; the editing strength of [t_boost, 0] is guaranteed below 0.25. Most quality degradation comes from the DDIM reverse process itself, not Asyrp.

### Experiments (p. 6–9)

**Setup**: Experiments span CelebA-HQ and LSUN-bedroom/-church on DDPM++, AFHQ-dog on iDDPM, and METFACES on ADM with P2-weighting — all official pretrained checkpoints kept frozen. f_t is trained with S = 40 for 1 epoch on 1000 samples (real samples for in-domain attributes; random latent variables x_T ~ N(0, I) for out-of-domain ones). Training takes about 20 minutes on three RTX 3090 GPUs; inference uses Ŝ = 1,000 steps.

**Versatility**: The method edits attributes not in the training dataset (church → department store, factory, temple; smiling Poodle and Yorkshire terriers), changes identities, painting styles, and even ancient primates, across all U-Net-based architectures.

**Quantitative comparison**: Against DiffusionCLIP (which fine-tunes the whole model), a user study with 80 participants on 40 image sets shows Asyrp strongly preferred: e.g., 98.36% vs 1.64% on quality, 88.13% vs 11.88% on attribute, 94.92% vs 5.08% overall for CelebA-HQ in-domain; 76.81% vs 23.19% overall for LSUN-church. Additional metrics (directional CLIP similarity S_dir and segmentation consistency SC) show Asyrp outperforms on S_dir for all attributes; DiffusionCLIP achieves better or competitive SC because it focuses on texture/color changes while preserving structure.

**Analysis of h-space properties**:
- *Homogeneity*: one Δh_t optimized for an image produces the same attribute change in other images; one Δε_t distorts other images. The mean Δh over 20 samples produces nearly identical results.
- *Linearity*: linear scaling of Δh controls attribute magnitude, generalizing even to negative scales not seen during training; combinations of Δh's yield combined semantic changes.
- *Robustness*: random noise in h-space yields realistic images with small or semantic changes; random noise in ε-space severely distorts images.
- *Consistency across timesteps*: a time-invariant Δh_global (mean over the editing interval) yields similar results to per-timestep Δh_t, though Δh_t is chosen for best quality, especially in unseen domains.

### Conclusion (p. 9)

Asyrp facilitates image editing in h-space for pretrained diffusion models, with GAN-like latent-space properties. The full editing process is designed via editing strength and quality deficiency. Future directions include combining with fine-tuning or guidance techniques.

### Appendices: Supporting Material (p. 13–35)

- **Related work and discussion (Appendix A–B)**: Compared to GAN-based editing, Asyrp's advantage is freedom from difficult inversion; the tradeoff is slower sampling. Limitations: Asyrp seldom changes overall style or peripheral objects, editing mainly attributes of the main object. An ethics statement acknowledges potential misuse (disinformation, propaganda) and advocates safeguards.
- **Proof of Theorem 1 (Appendix C)**: Formal demonstration that shifting ε_θt in both P_t and D_t cancels out.
- **Choice of h-space (Appendix D)**: Among U-Net layers, the 8th layer (the bridge) is chosen — unaffected by skip connections, smallest spatial dimension, just before upsampling. Layers 1–6 hardly bring changes; layers 7 and 9 work but are hard to tune; after layer 9, severe artifacts appear.
- **Implicit function details (Appendix E–F)**: f_t has two 1×1 convolution layers with 512 channels. Non-accelerated sampling (1000 steps) with scaled Δh_t yields higher-quality images (about 1.5 seconds for 40 steps vs 40 seconds for 1000 steps).
- **Hyperparameter analysis (Appendix G–H)**: t_edit too high gives insufficient changes; too low causes excessive manipulation. t_boost is not sensitive, but larger intervals reduce preservation. Table 2 lists prompts, CLIP similarities, t_edit, and t_boost for all attributes (e.g., CelebA-HQ "smiling face": similarity 0.899, t_edit 513, t_boost 167; LSUN-church "Factory": similarity 0.702, t_edit 301, t_boost 293).
- **Algorithms (Appendix I)**: Algorithm 1 (inference: semantic encoding via inversion, then manipulation in three phases — editing with Δh from f_t, denoising, quality boosting with stochastic noise) and Algorithm 2 (training f_t with directional CLIP loss and reconstruction regularization).
- **Training details (Appendix J)**: λ_CLIP ranges 0.5–0.8 for in-domain attributes; unseen domains need stronger values (up to 2.0). λ_recon = CLIP similarity × 3. Attributes trained with random noise sampling are those where identity preservation is less important (e.g., Pixar, Zombie, all LSUN-church attributes).
- **Evaluation (Appendix K)**: User-study questions cover quality, attribute naturalness, and overall preference; for LSUN-church, diversity is also asked. DiffusionCLIP works better at changing holistic style but lacks semantic changes and diversity.
- **Directions (Appendix L)**: Comparison of f_t, optimized Δh_t, and Δh_global: training time f_t ≈ Δh_global < Δh_t; quality best for f_t and Δh_t; extensibility f_t > Δh_t > Δh_global. Consistency of h-space decreases at the end of the generative process.
- **Additional results (Appendix M–N)**: Asyrp works for conditional random sampling from Gaussian noise, on ImageNet pretrained models (though the latent space is not partitioned by classes there), and supports mixed interpolation between multiple attributes.

## GLOSSAR

- **Asyrp (asymmetric reverse process)** — A modified reverse diffusion process that alters only the predicted-x0 term P_t while preserving the direction term D_t, enabling semantic manipulation in frozen diffusion models.
- **h-space** — The semantic latent space discovered at the bottleneck (deepest feature maps h_t) of the diffusion model's U-Net, exhibiting homogeneity, linearity, robustness, and consistency across timesteps.
- **Homogeneity** — The property that the same shift Δh in h-space results in the same attribute change across different images.
- **Linearity** — The property that linearly scaling Δh linearly controls the magnitude of attribute change, including negative scales.
- **Robustness** — The property that random perturbations in h-space yield realistic images without severe distortion, unlike perturbations in ε-space.
- **Consistency across timesteps** — The property that Δh_t for a desired attribute change is roughly the same across different timesteps of the generative process.
- **Editing strength (ξ_t)** — A quantifiable measure of the perceptual change over an interval [T, t], defined via LPIPS between the original image and the predicted image P_t; used to determine the editing interval endpoint t_edit.
- **Quality deficiency (γ_t)** — The amount of noise in x_t compared to the original image, defined as γ_t = LPIPS(x, x_t); used to determine the quality-boosting interval endpoint t_boost.
- **Editing interval [T, t_edit]** — The early stage of the reverse process where Asyrp performs semantic editing; t_edit is chosen where LPIPS(x, P_t_edit) = 0.33 (minus δ for attributes requiring larger changes).
- **Boosting interval [t_boost, 0]** — The late stage where stochastic noise is injected to improve image quality; t_boost is chosen where γ_t_boost = 1.2.
- **Implicit neural direction (f_t)** — A small neural network (two 1×1 convolutions with timestep t concatenated) that produces Δh_t for given h_t and t, replacing direct per-timestep optimization.
- **Δh_global** — A time-invariant direction computed as the mean of Δh_mean_t over the editing interval, which yields editing results similar to per-timestep Δh_t.
- **Destructive interference** — The phenomenon whereby shifting the predicted noise ε_θt causes the changes in P_t and D_t to cancel each other out, leaving the generated image unchanged (Theorem 1).
- **P_t / D_t** — Abbreviations in the sampling equation — P_t(ε_θt(x_t)) denotes the predicted x_0 and D_t(ε_θt(x_t)) denotes the direction pointing to x_t.
